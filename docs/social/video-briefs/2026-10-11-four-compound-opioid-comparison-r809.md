## Scientific accuracy and framing — mandatory reviewer checks

1. **No universal human potency ranking.** Assay, route, active metabolites, exposure, effects, and risk differ. Do not swap in a numeric potency ladder.
2. **Do not treat kratom leaf, purified mitragynine, enhanced 7-OH, MGM-15, or isolated corynoxine B as the same exposure.**
3. **Mitragynine:** Human metabolism to 7-OH is documented, but 'small amount' is qualitative and requires EvidenceBridge sign-off on context. Do not imply that every consumer formulation has equivalent exposure.
4. **7-OH:** Risks and human-exposure evidence for concentrated products are stronger than for isolated corynoxine B. Avoid implying every risk is clinically confirmed at an exact dose.
5. **MGM-15:** Report scarcity of controlled human data. DEA's Aug. 26, 2026 temporary Schedule I order covers MGM-15, MGM-16 and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl; **do not imply that the same order finalized scheduling of all 7-OH products**.
6. **Corynoxine B:** Current human-opioid receptor laboratory results do **not** establish intoxication, respiratory depression, addiction, safety, or effective doses in humans.
7. **Overdose:** Emergency guidance is conditional on *suspected opioid overdose*, not a claim that naloxone has been tested against isolated corynoxine B or MGM-15 in humans.
8. No buying, making, dosing, potency-conversion, or product-sourcing instructions. No depictions of identifiable products or unsupported molecule models.
9. Technical words requiring voiced explanation include opioid receptor, dependence, Schedule I, and naloxone. Review whether 'withdrawal' also needs a spoken definition for the intended audience.
