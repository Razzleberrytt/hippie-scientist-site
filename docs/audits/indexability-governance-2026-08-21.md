# Indexability & Sitemap Governance — 2026-08-21

## Headline

The layer the site actually reads is **coherent**. A second, disagreeing copy of
the same metadata is what makes it look otherwise.

| Layer | Read by | Herbs indexable | Compounds indexable | Sitemap conflicts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `summary-indexes/*-summary.json` | `app/sitemap.ts`, profile page metadata | 240 / 291 | 112 / 565 | **0** |
| `{herbs,compounds}-detail/*.json` | `getRuntimeVisibility` → `canRender` | 20 / 291 | 459 / 565 | — |

**647 of 856 profiles (76%) disagree between the two copies.**

## The Taurine case, resolved

The reported conflict was "Taurine appears in the sitemap while being emitted as
`noindex`". Both halves of that statement are true of *different files*:

| | `indexability_status` | `robots` | `sitemap_included` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `summary-indexes/compounds-summary.json` | `PUBLISH` | `index,follow` | `true` |
| `compounds-detail/taurine.json` | `NEEDS_REVIEW` | `noindex,follow` | `false` |

`app/sitemap.ts` and the profile page both resolve their decision from the
summary index, so the live page is `index,follow` and its sitemap entry is
correct. The conflict is real as a **data-integrity defect** — two sources of
truth — and not real as an **SEO defect**. Nothing is advertised and blocked at
the same time.

Checked across the whole corpus, in the authoritative layer:

- URLs in the sitemap but noindexed: **0**
- Indexable profiles missing from the sitemap: **0**
- Profiles with no explicit `indexability_status`: **0**

These are now locked in by `lib/__tests__/sitemap-indexability-coherence.test.ts`,
which also asserts that the first two cannot be satisfied by noindexing
everything.

## Why the two layers disagree

The production deploy (`scripts/build-deploy.mjs`) parses the workbook and then
jumps straight to `build-related-runtime-maps`. It never runs
`apply-governance-overlay`, `postprocess-workbook-payloads`, or
`apply-participant-counts` — those exist only in `data:build`, which the deploy
does not call. The detail payloads are therefore not regenerated by a deploy;
they ship as committed, carrying whatever governance verdict was last written to
them, while the summary indexes are rebuilt from `herbs.json` / `compounds.json`
on every deploy.

Two copies, one refreshed per deploy and one frozen in the repository, drift
apart. That is the whole mechanism.

## What was *not* done, and why

The detail copy was **not** rewritten to match the authority. It is not inert:
`getRuntimeVisibility` reads `indexability_status` from the detail record to
decide `canRender`, which decides whether a page exists at all rather than
merely whether it is indexed. Reconciling the two layers would therefore change
which pages the site renders, and the direction of that change is a governance
decision:

- Adopting the summary index would make **240 herbs** indexable where the detail
  copy currently says 20.
- It would make **112 compounds** indexable where the detail copy says 459 —
  removing 347 compounds from the index.

Running the full `data:build` produces a third, self-consistent answer again:
239 herbs and 111 compounds indexable, sitemap matching exactly, 0 conflicts in
either direction. That is close to the summary-index figure and is the pipeline's
own considered output.

Per the brief's instruction not to make profiles indexable by default and not to
loosen standards to raise page counts, none of these were applied unilaterally.
**This needs a human decision**, and it is the single largest open item from this
repair.

### The consequence is larger than indexing

Reconciling is not only an SEO decision. `getRuntimeVisibility` reads the detail
copy to decide `canRender`, and `RuntimeEvidenceComparison` calls `notFound()`
when either side of a comparison fails to resolve:

```ts
if (!leftSide.record || !rightSide.record) notFound()
```

Building the site against a corpus with the governance overlay applied — where
Ashwagandha becomes `NEEDS_REVIEW` — makes **17 `/guides/compare/*` pages render
as 404s**, because the resolver drops any record whose detail verdict blocks
rendering:

`ashwagandha-vs-magnesium`, `ashwagandha-vs-saffron`, `bacopa-vs-citicoline`,
`berberine-vs-cinnamon`, `caffeine-vs-caffeine-l-theanine`,
`citicoline-vs-alpha-gpc`, `creatine-monohydrate-vs-alternative-forms`,
`l-theanine-vs-ashwagandha`, `l-theanine-vs-magnesium`, `lions-mane-vs-bacopa`,
`magnesium-glycinate-vs-citrate`, `magnesium-glycinate-vs-oxide`,
`magnesium-glycinate-vs-threonate`, `melatonin-vs-l-theanine`,
`melatonin-vs-valerian`, `rhodiola-vs-l-theanine`, `turmeric-vs-curcumin`.

On the committed corpus these pages build and render correctly, because
Ashwagandha is `PUBLISH` there. So the overlay does not merely demote profiles
in search — it silently removes curated comparison guides from the site.

Whoever takes the decision above should treat that as part of its cost, and
should probably decouple `canRender` from the indexing verdict first: whether a
page is *worth indexing* and whether it should *exist* are different questions,
and one flag currently answers both.

## Recommendation

1. Decide which layer is authoritative. The evidence points to the summary
   index: it is what both the sitemap and the pages already read, it is
   regenerated every deploy, and it agrees closely with a full `data:build`.
2. Then either stop writing indexability fields into the detail payloads
   entirely, or make both derive from one call, so a third answer cannot appear.
3. Consider running `apply-governance-overlay` in the deploy so production stops
   shipping frozen governance verdicts. This moves roughly 129 profiles and
   should ship on its own, with its own review.

## Guard in place meanwhile

`npm run report:indexability-divergence` compares the two layers, writes
`ops/reports/indexability-divergence.json`, and fails if the divergence grows
beyond the recorded baseline of **647**. The problem cannot quietly worsen while
it waits for the decision above.

## Governance reason codes

Reasons recorded on noindexed profiles, from the detail payloads:

| Reason | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `noindex-decision:hidden_until_grounded` | Profile withheld until evidence is attached |
| `production-invariant:human_claim_without_human_source` | A human-outcome claim with no human study behind it |
| `production-invariant:safety_claim_without_safety_source` | A safety claim with no safety source |
| `production-invariant:dose_statement_without_dose_source` | A dose stated with no dosing source |
| `summary-quality-missing` / `summary-too-thin` | Summary fails the depth check |
| `safety-context-missing` | No safety context recorded |
| `profile-status:needs_review` / `:partial` | Editorial state, not an evidence verdict |

These are legitimate caution, not misclassification: each names a specific
missing piece of evidence. They were left alone. Note that withdrawing an
unverifiable citation can *cause* one of these — a profile whose only safety
source had no identifier now correctly reports
`safety_claim_without_safety_source` — which is why citation quarantine runs
before the governance overlay rather than after.
