## DE19-01 — Magnesium sleep: statistically significant does not automatically mean clinically large

**New primary placebo RCT:** Schuster et al., *Nature and Science of Sleep* 2025, **[PMID 40918053](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40918053/)**, DOI 10.2147/NSS.S524348. **155 adults aged 18–65** reporting poor sleep were randomized double-blind to **a defined magnesium bisglycinate preparation (250 mg elemental magnesium)** or placebo. The comparison was self-reported **Insomnia Severity Index (ISI)** at week four, not a polysomnographic measure of actual sleep time. ISI changes favored magnesium: **−3.9 vs −2.3 points, P=.049, Cohen d=.2**, a **small** and borderline-significant group-level effect. Exploratory baseline magnesium dietary-intake subgroup observations were not a prospectively validated way to choose a responder. Other psychological endpoints were null.

**Older different population/form RCT:** Abbasi et al., *J Res Med Sci* 2012, **[PMID 23853635](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23853635/)**. **46 older patients with primary insomnia**, eight weeks, magnesium versus placebo; several sleep-log/ISI outcomes favored the intervention but the paper also reports **total sleep time P=.37 (null)** despite a distinct sleep-time variable positive, an endpoint-definition ambiguity that merits checking the original tables. The older preparation/exposure and older-insomnia population must not be used to infer equivalence of bisglycinate, glycinate, L-threonate, taurate, malate, citrate and oxide.

**Action:** **P0** verify wording on the best-magnesium-for-sleep comparison page. Clearly state the 2025 small effect, self-report nature, study duration, safety/elements/form and lack of direct comparative magnesium-form RCTs. Do **not** convert research protocol doses into recommendations; kidney/medication safety separately reviewed.

## DE19-02 — Glycine: modest experimental sleep-loss signal is not treatment of chronic insomnia

**Primary human experiment:** Bannai et al., *Front Neurol* 2012, **[PMID 22529837](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22529837/)**, DOI 10.3389/fneur.2012.00061. Healthy volunteers were experimentally restricted to **25% less than their normal sleep for three successive nights**, compared under bedtime glycine/placebo conditions. The authors reported lower **self-rated fatigue**, a trend toward less self-rated sleepiness and better **psychomotor vigilance**. This measured next-day function in an artificial restricted-sleep scenario, not chronic insomnia remission, polysomnographic total sleep, treatment of sleep apnea or long-term health protection. Researchers were affiliated with **Ajinomoto**, a manufacturer; assess full sponsor role and blinding.

**Evidence boundary:** An earlier glycine crossover on subjective morning alertness exists in the journal *Sleep and Biological Rhythms* (2006, DOI 10.1111/j.1479-8425.2006.00193.x), but is **not counted here as a PubMed-indexed original RCT** because a matching PubMed ID was not confirmed. The experimental paper should not be misreported as an independent long-term insomnia efficacy trial.

**Action:** P1 sleep ingredient/directness review; no diagnosis or dosing claims.

## DE19-03 — Oral GABA: positive short EEG result, high conflict/directness burden

**Primary:** Yamatsu et al., *Food Sci Biotechnol* 2016, **[PMID 30263304](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30263304/)**, DOI 10.1007/s10068-016-0076-9. The article reports a **randomized, single-blind, placebo-controlled crossover human EEG sleep experiment** with oral gamma-aminobutyric acid. Sleep-onset latency became shorter and non-REM sleep time increased in reported analyses; blood GABA increased briefly after administration. The abstract does not establish multi-month insomnia remission, effects on diagnosed anxiety disorder, long-term safety, or that peripheral blood GABA crossed the human blood–brain barrier.

**Provenance and overclaim control:** Every listed original author is affiliated with a commercial GABA-ingredient developer, **Pharma Foods International**. It is not a definitive independently replicated clinical treatment study, and a physiological EEG signal is not a proven broad OTC sleep-gummy effect. Review precise sample denominator, preregistration, outcome hierarchy and funding in full text before numerical public recommendation.

**Action:** P1 directness/funding warning; no consumer dose or medical claim.

## DE19-04 — Urolithin A: secondary muscle endurance must not replace null primary physical-function outcomes

**Original:** Liu et al., *JAMA Netw Open* 2022, **[PMID 35050355](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35050355/)**, DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2021.44279. Randomized double-blind experiment of **66 adults aged 65–90** over four months, urolithin A versus placebo. The **prespecified primary endpoints**, six-minute walking distance and hand-muscle maximal ATP production, **did not significantly improve** versus placebo. The authors reported improved **secondary muscle endurance** counts and selected metabolite/inflammatory biomarkers. It did not measure extension of lifespan, prevention of disability, reversal of age or long-term independence.

**Bias/identity:** The study was **funded by Amazentis**, with company author affiliations and declared sponsor involvement in study design, data and manuscript. All participants were White, mostly women, with a small US sample; the authors themselves described results as exploratory. Oral urolithin A is not equivalent to eating pomegranate or any product labelled "mitochondrial support."

**Action:** **P0** longevity/mitochondria claim audit. Keep primary null visible before the favorable secondary tests and record sponsor controls.

## DE19-05 — NMN: NAD-precursor rationale did not improve grip or walking in this small clinical trial

**Original:** Akasaka et al., *Geriatr Gerontol Int* 2023, **[PMID 36443648](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36443648/)**, DOI 10.1111/ggi.14513. **14 older men** (mean age 81) with diabetes plus reduced baseline grip strength or gait speed were randomized double-blind to NMN versus placebo for **24 weeks**. **Primary physical results were null**: no significant between-group improvement in grip strength or walking speed; reported exploratory indicators were also not significant. Borderline frailty prevalence **P=.066** is not a positive efficacy result.

**Boundary:** Very low power; null does not conclusively rule out a specific effect in other populations. Conversely a biological NAD hypothesis and favorable animal studies are **not human reversal-of-aging evidence**. The trial cannot define consumer-wide safety of unstandardized NMN preparations, and its commercial affiliations require independent disclosure verification.

**Action:** **P0** no unsupported "rejuvenates", "increases mobility" or life-extension claims. Preserve objective nulls.

## DE19-06 — Nicotinamide riboside: circulating NAD+ is a biomarker, not proven longer life

**Original:** Martens et al., *Nature Communications* 2018, **[PMID 29599478](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29599478/)**, DOI 10.1038/s41467-018-03421-7. This is a **2 × 6-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover** trial in healthy middle-aged/older adults. NR was **well tolerated over the short trial** and increased measured NAD+ metabolism. Cardiovascular physiological analyses (blood pressure and arterial stiffness) were presented as promising reasons for **further trials**, not proven durable reduction in strokes, heart attacks, cognitive impairment, disability or mortality.

**Inference firewall:** An increase in circulating NAD+ does **not automatically imply** increased mitochondrial function in every tissue, better exercise performance or lifespan. NR is not NMN, niacin, NAD infusion or a generic "NAD booster"; do not pool unlike interventions. No established dose or universal safety recommendation follows from this study.

**Action:** **P0** future longevity-product and antiaging article audit; source-specific formulations only.

## DE19-07 — Purified shilajit: changing testosterone lab values is not clinical androgen replacement

**Original:** Pandit et al., *Andrologia* 2016, **[PMID 26395129](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26395129/)**, DOI 10.1111/and.12482. A **90-day double-blind randomized placebo-controlled trial** in men aged **45–55** tested a *purified* shilajit preparation. Total testosterone, free testosterone and DHEAS blood measures increased versus placebo (reported P < .05), with LH/FSH described as maintained. PubMed's abstract does **not** establish better fertility, sexual function, patient-reported well-being, strength, protection from age-related disease or equivalence to testosterone therapy.

**Purity/quality boundary:** Shilajit varies greatly in source and contaminant profile; trial-grade "purified" material cannot validate the elemental/toxicant content of a retail resin or capsule. Hormone-related interactions and endogenous endocrine consequences require separate expert review.

**Action:** P1 chemical identity, contaminant certificate, hormonal-marketing and consumer safety editorial gate.

## DE19-08 — Collagen skin claims: positive sponsored RCTs but strong funding-quality warning

**Primary RCT:** Vleminckx et al., *J Cosmet Dermatol* 2024, **[PMID 39143887](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39143887/)**, DOI 10.1111/jocd.16458. **85 women aged 43–65** randomized to one hydrolyzed collagen-peptide preparation or placebo for 84 days; both groups also used an identical face cream after a lead-in period. The trial reports favorable **dermal density and hydration** differences by day 84; other measures showed time-dependent patterns, making within-group improvement an insufficient substitute for placebo-adjusted outcome analysis. Several authors were **employees of a collagen-peptide manufacturer** (Rousselot). Specific cosmetic measurements are not reduced clinical skin-cancer risk or general reversal of biological aging.

**Separate evidence synthesis, NOT an original cohort:** Myung and Park, *Am J Med* 2025, **[PMID 40324552](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40324552/)**, DOI 10.1016/j.amjmed.2025.04.034. Across **23 existing RCTs / 1,474 participants**, pooled collagen findings for hydration, elasticity and wrinkles were favorable overall, **but** non-pharmaceutical-industry-funded study subsets and higher-quality study subsets had **no significant benefit**, unlike industry-funded/low-quality subsets. This source is included as **a secondary synthesis, not a 13th independent randomized trial**.

**Action:** **P0** commercial/review-quality audit; show sponsor status and prevent ingredient-wide or guaranteed skin antiaging claims.

## DE19-09 — NAC cannabis cessation: adolescent positive cannot be transferred to adult null

**Younger population:** Gray et al., *Am J Psychiatry* 2012, **[PMID 22706327](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22706327/)**, DOI 10.1176/appi.ajp.2012.12010055. **116 treatment-seeking ages 15–21** in an eight-week double-blind RCT received NAC or placebo **on top of contingency management and brief counseling**. Weekly urine-negative cannabis outcomes favored NAC (**OR 2.4, 95% CI 1.1–5.2, P=.029**). An **erratum is linked** to this publication; verify its nature in source-level corrections audit. Not proof that self-directed NAC alone produces abstinence.

**Separate larger adult test:** Gray et al., *Drug Alcohol Depend* 2017, **[PMID 28623823](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28623823/)**, DOI 10.1016/j.drugalcdep.2017.04.020. **302 treatment-seeking adults aged 18–50**, six-site, 12-week double-blind placebo RCT with contingency management and medical management in both arms. **Primary cannabinoid-negative urine comparison null**, OR **1.00** (95% CI .63–1.59), **P=.984**; **22.3% vs 22.4%** of tests were negative. Adherence and age/behavioral context could contribute to disagreement but cannot erase the adult null or certify a youth-specific true effect.

**Action:** **P0** distinguish age, cointervention and objective urine endpoint; no self-treatment or addiction substitution advice. The two are **genuinely independent randomized cohorts**.

## DE19-10 — Myo-inositol PCOS fertility: combination live birth is not isolated supplement efficacy

**Original:** Agrawal et al., *Gynecol Endocrinol* 2019, **[PMID 30614289](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30614289/)**, DOI 10.1080/09513590.2018.1549656. **120 women with infertility and PCOS** randomized to **metformin plus myo-inositol versus metformin alone**. Women tried spontaneous conception, then received planned ovulation induction/intrauterine insemination if needed. **Primary live-birth endpoint** reported **33/60 (55%) vs 16/60 (26.7%), P=.002** for the combinations as tested; menstrual timing also improved, while many biochemical/hormone measures were comparable.

**Inference/safety boundaries:** This is a *small, specific add-on* study within planned fertility treatment, not an RCT of myo-inositol alone, generic inositol isomers, spontaneous birth in all patients, or prevention of obstetric complications. It cannot justify stopping metformin, replacing fertility care or recommending a dose to people trying to conceive. Full risk-of-bias, preregistered protocol, study-center practice, and broader subsequent syntheses are **not yet assessed**.

**Action:** **P0** reproductive-health independent review, prepared as a narrowly worded research question only.
