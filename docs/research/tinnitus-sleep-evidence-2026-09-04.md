# Tinnitus and Sleep — Evidence Dossier

Date: 2026-09-04
Public route: `/articles/tinnitus-and-sleep/`
Status: publication-ready evidence support

## Research question

How strong is the evidence that tinnitus contributes to insomnia, and what evidence supports CBT-I, tinnitus-directed CBT, audiology care and sound-based strategies for tinnitus-related sleep problems?

## Editorial conclusion

Tinnitus and insomnia frequently coexist, but tinnitus-related insomnia should not be explained by tinnitus loudness alone.

The evidence supports:

- insomnia-focused CBT-I as a direct treatment for tinnitus-related insomnia;
- tinnitus-directed CBT as an intervention that can also improve sleep;
- audiology-based care as useful but less effective than CBT-I for insomnia in the strongest direct RCT;
- cognitive-behavioral insomnia processes as important maintaining factors;
- keeping environmental sound strategies separate from evidence-based insomnia treatment.

## Exact quantitative anchors

### Direct CBT-I randomized trial

PMID 35762946:

- n = 102
- three groups:
  - CBT-I
  - audiology-based care (ABC)
  - sleep support group (SSG)
- CBT-I superior to ABC for insomnia and sleep efficiency post-treatment and at 6 months
- clinically meaningful improvement:
  - CBT-I: **>80%**
  - ABC: **47%**
  - SSG: **20%**
- CBT-I also improved tinnitus distress, sleep quality, functioning and some mental-health outcomes

Editorial wording:

> CBT-I improves tinnitus-related insomnia and can reduce tinnitus distress.

Do not rewrite as:

> CBT-I makes tinnitus quieter or eliminates the tinnitus signal.

### CBT-based tinnitus intervention meta-analysis

PMID 33360841:

- 5 trials included in systematic review
- 4 contributed to meta-analysis
- Insomnia Severity Index mean difference: **-3.28**
- 95% CI: **-4.51 to -2.05**
- p < 0.001
- I² = **0%**

Editorial rule:

The interventions were CBT-based but not necessarily identical full CBT-I protocols. Preserve that distinction.

### Internet/mobile tinnitus CBT

PMID 40818309:

- 9 studies
- insomnia severity: **MD -2.65** (95% CI -3.64 to -1.67)
- Tinnitus Functional Index: **MD -12.48** (95% CI -16.43 to -8.54)
- GAD-7: **MD -1.33** (95% CI -2.37 to -0.30)
- HADS-Anxiety: **MD -1.92**
- HADS-Depression: **MD -1.40**
- no significant pooled improvement in Tinnitus Handicap Inventory in that analysis

Editorial rule:

Do not transfer the iCBT tinnitus result into a claim that tinnitus-focused CBT is equivalent to insomnia-focused CBT-I.

### Tinnitus-insomnia observational association

PMID 40088765:

- 22 studies
- tinnitus associated with:
  - depression OR 1.92
  - anxiety OR 1.63
  - stress OR 1.17
  - insomnia **OR 3.07** (95% CI 2.36–3.98)

Editorial rule:

Use “associated with,” not “causes.” Mixed observational designs do not establish one-way causality.

## Mechanism / maintenance guardrails

Tinnitus-related insomnia may involve:

- increased salience in quiet environments
- selective attention
- threat appraisal
- frustration
- sleep effort
- clock monitoring
- conditioned arousal
- excessive time in bed
- irregular sleep behaviors

PMID 37008859 supports the importance of insomnia cognitions and behaviors and suggests tinnitus severity itself is not sufficient to explain sleep disturbance.

Do not imply these psychological/behavioral processes mean tinnitus is imaginary.

## CBT-I vs tinnitus CBT

### Insomnia-focused CBT-I

Primary target:

- insomnia disorder
- sleep efficiency
- conditioned wakefulness
- maladaptive sleep behaviors
- sleep-related beliefs

### Tinnitus-directed CBT

Primary target:

- tinnitus distress
- catastrophic interpretation
- attention
- avoidance
- coping and functioning

Both can affect sleep and tinnitus distress.

They are related but not interchangeable.

## Audiology-care guardrail

The direct RCT found audiology-based care improved insomnia/sleep efficiency relative to support, although CBT-I was superior.

Safe claim:

> Audiology care can help, but insomnia-specific CBT-I provides additional benefit when chronic insomnia is established.

Unsafe claim:

> Audiology treatment is irrelevant once CBT-I is available.

## Sound / masking guardrails

Sound enrichment may:

- reduce contrast between tinnitus and silence
- make tinnitus less salient
- provide comfort

But the following claims are not established:

- white noise cures tinnitus insomnia
- louder masking is more effective
- tinnitus must be fully inaudible to sleep
- continuous sound necessarily improves objective sleep

Link to broader `/articles/white-noise-and-sleep/` evidence rather than inheriting generic white-noise claims.

## Differential diagnosis guardrails

Tinnitus can coexist with:

- obstructive sleep apnea
- restless legs syndrome
- circadian delay
- insufficient sleep opportunity
- depression
- anxiety
- pain
- medication effects

Do not attribute all awakenings to tinnitus simply because tinnitus is noticeable at night.

## Final editorial verdict

**Moderate evidence.**

The direct CBT-I RCT and earlier randomized-trial synthesis provide unusually specific evidence for treating insomnia in tinnitus rather than only treating tinnitus and hoping sleep improves.

The central editorial message should remain:

> Chronic insomnia in a person with tinnitus deserves its own treatment pathway. Tinnitus can trigger or amplify sleep difficulty, but insomnia-specific cognitive and behavioral processes can become independently important and respond to CBT-I.
