# Sleep Research Expansion — Phase 2 — 2026-09-04

## Purpose

Phase 2 moves the sleep cluster from a growing article collection into a **decision-oriented research system**.

The core editorial rule is now:

> **Define the sleep bottleneck before ranking the intervention.**

That means the cluster should distinguish chronic insomnia, circadian delay, shift work, insufficient sleep opportunity, sleep-disordered breathing, restless legs, environmental disruption, substance effects, and post-waking impairment before recommending another supplement.

## New ingredient / nutrient research added in this phase

### Lemon balm

Route: `/articles/lemon-balm-for-sleep/`

Position: **Limited to promising; formulation-specific.**

Key anchors include the 2024 standardized-extract crossover trial (PMID 39683592), the 2025 narrative synthesis (PMID 41226605), the older lemon-balm combination insomnia trial (PMID 29741926), and a 2023 standardized-extract trial (PMID 37927585).

Guardrail: combination formulas and specialized phospholipid-carrier extracts do not validate tea, essential oil, or every generic lemon-balm capsule.

### Oral GABA

Route: `/articles/oral-gaba-for-sleep/`

Position: **Very limited.**

The 2020 systematic review (PMID 33041752) remains the main synthesis and explicitly characterized sleep evidence as very limited. Later small RCTs add signals without creating a dependable chronic-insomnia verdict.

Guardrail: “GABA is inhibitory in the brain” is not evidence that swallowed GABA reliably reproduces central GABA signaling or treats insomnia.

### Omega-3

Route: `/articles/omega-3-and-sleep/`

Position: **Mixed / endpoint-specific.**

The 2024 RCT meta-analysis (PMID 39583980) found benefits for sleep efficiency and subjective sleep quality but not sleep latency or total sleep time. The earlier 2020 synthesis (PMID 33382879) found no significant adult benefit across major sleep outcomes.

Guardrail: update the verdict with newer evidence without erasing the earlier null synthesis.

### Vitamin D

Route: `/articles/vitamin-d-and-sleep/`

Position: **Moderate for selected subjective sleep-quality outcomes; deficiency-context dependent.**

Two 2022 syntheses (PMIDs 35268051 and 35578558) support a sleep-quality signal while effects on sleep quantity and specific sleep disorders remain uncertain.

Guardrail: vitamin D is a nutrient-status intervention, not a same-night sedative. Sleep claims do not justify indiscriminate dose escalation.

### Hops

Route: `/articles/hops-for-sleep/`

Position: **Limited and combination-confounded.**

Most useful human data involve valerian + hops combinations. The 2025 feasibility trial (PMID 40462685) reported about 22 minutes more wearable-estimated sleep with one fixed combination, but it was small, exploratory, product-specific, and unable to isolate hops.

Guardrail: a valerian+hops trial does not establish a standalone hops effect.

## New environmental / non-drug research

### White noise

Route: `/articles/white-noise-and-sleep/`

Position: **Mixed to moderate in selected populations.**

The evidence story changed over time: the 2020 systematic review (PMID 33007706) rated the evidence very low quality, while the 2025 RCT meta-analysis (PMID 41151421) found favorable subjective sleep-quality effects in adult and older-adult patient populations.

Guardrail: newer positive pooled evidence updates the verdict; it does not erase heterogeneity or prove benefit in an already quiet bedroom.

### Weighted blankets

Route: `/articles/weighted-blankets-for-sleep/`

Position: **Limited to promising in adults.**

The 2024 pilot RCT (PMID 39501163) found greater subjective PSQI improvement in adults with insomnia, while objective actigraphy awakenings did not significantly separate. Meta-analyses (PMIDs 39447684 and 39341068) remain heterogeneous and sensitive to study selection.

Guardrail: no universal blanket-weight percentage has been established as a sleep prescription.

### Warm bath / shower before bed

Route: `/articles/warm-bath-shower-before-bed/`

Position: **Moderate for sleep-onset support.**

The 2019 meta-analysis (PMID 31102877) found shorter sleep-onset latency, improved sleep efficiency, and better subjective sleep quality, with a useful timing signal around 1–2 hours before bed. The 2025 hot-foot-bath meta-analysis (PMID 40492751) did not find a significant pooled sleep-quality effect.

Guardrail: whole-body passive heating and a foot bath are not interchangeable interventions.

### Music

Route: `/articles/music-for-sleep/`

Position: **Moderate for subjective sleep quality; weaker objective evidence.**

The 2022 Cochrane review (PMID 36000763) found moderate-certainty PSQI improvement but did not establish clear objective sleep improvements or a universal insomnia-severity effect.

Guardrail: general music evidence does not validate special frequencies, proprietary tracks, or guaranteed deep-sleep claims.

### Mindfulness

Route: `/articles/mindfulness-for-insomnia/`

Position: **Mixed; adjunctive rather than first-line.**

Older meta-analyses found improvements versus control conditions, while the 2022 MBSR update (PMID 36332952) and 2023 CBT-I-system review (PMID 37271575) show that benefits are much less impressive against active comparators or established insomnia treatment components.

Guardrail: beating a waitlist is not equivalent to beating CBT-I.

### Eye masks and earplugs

Route: `/articles/eye-masks-earplugs-and-sleep/`

Position: **Moderate in noisy/bright clinical settings.**

The 2026 systematic review (PMID 42442062) found subjective sleep-quality improvement across randomized ICU trials. Earlier meta-analyses (PMIDs 34096647 and 34925113) generally show a stronger signal for eye masks and combined eye-mask + earplug interventions than for earplugs alone.

Guardrail: ICU effect sizes should not be transferred directly to already dark, quiet bedrooms.

## New circadian / schedule decision layer

### Delayed sleep-wake phase vs insomnia

Route: `/articles/delayed-sleep-wake-phase-vs-insomnia/`

Position: **Moderate for the circadian-timing framework.**

A key distinction is whether sleep becomes relatively normal on a later preferred schedule. The 2021 adult light-therapy systematic review (PMID 34381579) found phase-shifting signals but a small controlled evidence base. The melatonin meta-analysis (PMID 21120122) found advances in endogenous melatonin timing and sleep onset.

Guardrail: timed light and melatonin are circadian interventions. They should not be reduced to generic sedation.

### Shift work sleep disorder

Canonical route: `/articles/shift-work-sleep-disorder/`

A duplicate `/articles/shift-work-sleep/` draft was created during parallel expansion, detected before hub integration, and deleted to prevent search cannibalization.

The canonical page is stronger because it is anchored to the newest AASM guidance plus recent light-therapy evidence and explicitly separates fixed from rotating schedules.

### Sleep inertia

Route: `/articles/sleep-inertia-grogginess-after-waking/`

This independently landed research page is now promoted into the canonical hub. It covers post-waking cognitive/performance impairment, nap-length myths, prior sleep loss, caffeine, bright light, and safety-sensitive work.

## Research standard going forward

Every sleep claim should preserve:

- population;
- endpoint;
- measurement method;
- comparator;
- magnitude;
- formulation or intervention directness;
- duration;
- safety context;
- important null outcomes;
- highest-level recent synthesis; and
- the disorder or behavioral boundary that could make the intervention irrelevant.

The cluster should become harder to imitate not by publishing more hype, but by making **the evidence structure itself visible**.
