# Sleep-Related Eating Disorder — Evidence Dossier

Date: 2026-09-04
Status: publication-ready support for `content/articles/sleep-related-eating-disorder.md`

## Research question

What distinguishes sleep-related eating disorder (SRED) from ordinary nighttime eating and night eating syndrome, what safety and medication triggers matter, and how strong is the treatment evidence?

## Editorial verdict

SRED is a non-REM parasomnia involving recurrent eating/drinking after sleep arousal with impaired awareness and often partial or complete amnesia.

Evidence grade: **Low-Moderate**.

Why:

- diagnostic phenotype is reasonably established;
- modern reviews consistently emphasize comorbid sleep disorders and medication triggers;
- one randomized topiramate trial provides direct efficacy evidence;
- however, epidemiology and most treatment literature remain low quality and heterogeneous.

## Anchor evidence

### 1. 2024 systematic literature review — PMID 38873533

Retrieved 94 primary and secondary reports covering epidemiology, risk factors, clinical data, differential diagnosis, structured evaluation and treatment.

Key conclusions:

- SRED can produce injuries, obesity/metabolic consequences and functional impairment;
- Z-drugs, benzodiazepines, antidepressants, antipsychotics and psychostimulants have been reported as triggers;
- psychiatric and neurologic conditions can coexist;
- structured assessment is possible;
- good-quality epidemiologic studies and clinical trials remain scarce.

Editorial rule: publication count ≠ strong evidence.

### 2. 2024 Sleep Medicine Clinics review — PMID 38368069

Key points:

- SRED is a NREM parasomnia;
- recurrent eating/drinking follows arousals;
- partial/complete amnesia is common;
- adverse health and quality-of-life effects are common;
- often accompanies untreated/unrecognized sleep disorders and/or psychoactive medications;
- management prioritizes comorbidities and potentially offending medications;
- clinical history is often sufficient;
- sleep testing may help identify coexisting disorders or competing events;
- limited data support topiramate and other medications in refractory cases.

### 3. PSG systematic review — PMID 37234216

Key points:

- SRED diagnosis is usually clinical;
- PSG is not required to confirm every case;
- PSG can help identify comorbid sleep disorders or clarify competing nocturnal phenomena.

Editorial rule: no “normal PSG rules out SRED” claim.

### 4. Randomized topiramate trial — PMID 32227216

Sample:

- 34 adults randomized;
- chronic SRED >6 months;
- at least 3 episodes/week;
- 13 weeks.

Results:

- topiramate: 74.7% → 33.2% nights/week with eating (n=15);
- placebo: 77.0% → 57.4% (n=17);
- p=0.035;
- CGI-I responders: 71% vs 27%, p=0.016;
- weight change: -8.5 lb vs +1.0 lb, p=0.001;
- common side effects: paresthesias and cognitive dysfunction.

Critical limitations:

- small sample;
- high dropout in both groups;
- flexible dosing;
- one trial is not a mature treatment evidence base.

Editorial rule: randomized evidence supports efficacy signal, not universal self-treatment.

### 5. Zolpidem case review — PMID 33870172

Forty published zolpidem-associated SRED cases.

Reported:

- 65% female;
- mean age 53;
- OSA 35%;
- depression 32.5%;
- RLS 25%;
- all had partial/full amnesia with compulsive eating;
- discontinuation resolved SRED in all 36 cases with discontinuation outcome data.

Editorial rule:

Strong dechallenge pattern, but still case-report evidence. Do not publish incidence or “zolpidem always causes SRED.”

### 6. WHO VigiBase disproportionality analysis — PMID 35807172

676 drug-associated SRED reports.

Common reports:

- zolpidem 243 (35.9%);
- sodium oxybate 185 (27.4%);
- quetiapine 97 (14.3%).

Significant disproportionality found for multiple psychoactive medications.

Editorial rule:

- pharmacovigilance detects signals;
- reporting odds ratio ≠ incidence;
- disproportionality ≠ proof of causality for an individual case.

### 7. Associated-conditions review — PMID 25495278

Key points:

- recurrent eating at sleep-to-arousal transitions;
- reduced awareness;
- high-calorie and sometimes inedible/toxic items;
- overlap with other sleep disorders.

Useful for safety and differential diagnosis.

### 8. 2015 treatment review — PMID 26037737

Discusses medication options and treating associated disorders.

High-value enduring rule:

- SRED associated with sleep fragmentation/other sleep disorders often benefits from treating the associated sleep disorder.

Editorial caution:

Older drug recommendations predate the 2024 systematic review and should not be converted into modern ranked first-line consumer advice.

### 9. Nighttime eating disorders review — PMID 18819825

Core distinction:

- NES = circadian eating-timing abnormality with waking awareness;
- SRED = parasomnia-like eating after sleep arousal, often with amnesia.

## Core distinctions

### SRED ≠ night eating syndrome

SRED:

- partial arousal;
- impaired awareness;
- amnesia common;
- parasomnia framework.

NES:

- waking awareness preserved;
- circadian/eating-disorder framework;
- deliberate remembered eating.

### SRED ≠ ordinary snacking

A fully awake choice to eat at night is not SRED.

### SRED ≠ sleepwalking, but overlap is real

Both involve complex behavior during incomplete arousal and can coexist.

### Partial memory does not rule out SRED

Awareness/amnesia exist on a spectrum.

## Safety hierarchy

Public content should prioritize:

1. injury prevention;
2. preventing access to toxic/inedible substances;
3. medication review;
4. assessment of sleepwalking, OSA, RLS and other sleep fragmentation;
5. selected PSG when differential diagnosis/comorbid disorder assessment requires it;
6. clinician-guided pharmacotherapy for persistent/refractory cases.

## Medication-trigger rule

Medication-associated SRED should be framed as a reason to contact the prescribing clinician.

Do not tell readers to abruptly stop:

- zolpidem;
- sodium oxybate;
- quetiapine;
- antidepressants;
- other psychoactive medication.

Withdrawal/rebound risk depends on the drug and context.

## Topiramate rule

Allowed:

- one small placebo-controlled RCT showed significant reduction in eating nights and more CGI-I responders;
- adverse effects included paresthesias and cognitive dysfunction;
- high dropout and small n limit certainty.

Not allowed:

- “topiramate is the best SRED drug”;
- dosing instructions;
- weight-loss framing as the treatment goal;
- consumer self-selection.

## PSG rule

Allowed:

- diagnosis can often be clinical;
- PSG can identify OSA/other sleep disorders or clarify unusual events.

Not allowed:

- “PSG is required for all SRED”;
- “normal PSG rules out SRED.”

## Supplement inheritance rule

No established evidence supports transferring general sleep-supplement efficacy into SRED treatment.

Do not imply:

- magnesium prevents SRED;
- melatonin treats SRED;
- calming herbs fix incomplete arousal;
- appetite supplements treat involuntary parasomnia eating.
