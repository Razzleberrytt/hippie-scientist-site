# Idiopathic Hypersomnia vs Narcolepsy — Evidence Dossier

Date: 2026-09-04
Status: publication-ready support for `content/articles/idiopathic-hypersomnia-vs-narcolepsy.md`

## Research question

How should the site distinguish idiopathic hypersomnia (IH) from narcolepsy—especially narcolepsy type 2—while preserving MSLT limitations, long-sleep phenotypes, sleep inertia, orexin biology, and diagnosis-specific treatment evidence?

## Editorial verdict

The cleanest diagnostic separation is between **narcolepsy type 1** and IH because cataplexy and orexin deficiency provide relatively specific biological/clinical anchors.

The difficult boundary is **IH vs narcolepsy type 2**:
- both can cause severe excessive daytime sleepiness;
- neither has cataplexy;
- NT2 lacks an established biomarker;
- classification depends heavily on MSLT SOREMP counts;
- MSLT has poor test-retest reliability and may underestimate long-sleep IH.

Evidence grade: **Moderate**.

## Anchor evidence

### 1. 2024 diagnostic-challenges systematic review

PMID: 39211350

- 97 publications included;
- IH burden extends beyond EDS;
- characteristic symptoms include sleep inertia/sleep drunkenness, long sleep duration and cognitive dysfunction;
- diagnostic overlap with other hypersomnolence disorders is substantial;
- reported diagnostic delays can be prolonged.

Editorial use:
- IH is not simply “sleeping a lot”;
- symptom burden is multidimensional.

### 2. 2026 IH diagnostic perspectives review

PMID: 41615003

Key points:
- IH may involve EDS, prolonged nocturnal sleep and/or sleep inertia;
- excessive sleep duration and excessive daytime sleepiness are distinct dimensions;
- MSLT may underestimate hypersomnia in long-sleep phenotypes;
- MSLT has poor test-retest reliability;
- IH vs NT2 distinction can depend on an unstable number of SOREMPs.

Editorial use:
- central diagnostic-gray-zone source;
- never present current classification as biologically perfect.

### 3. 2022 clinical diagnosis review

PMID: 36401976

Key features:
- EDS;
- sleep inertia;
- long unrefreshing naps;
- prolonged nighttime sleep in many patients;
- diagnosis requires objective testing plus clinical context;
- low prevalence and symptom overlap contribute to missed/misdiagnosis.

### 4. 2025 central hypersomnolence biomarker review

PMID: 40976193

Key points:
- PSG + MSLT remain central testing tools;
- CSF orexin is useful for NT1;
- MSLT repeatability is limited;
- no established biomarkers for NT2 or IH;
- differentiation remains imperfect.

### 5. 2021 AASM treatment guideline

PMID: 34743789

Adult IH:
- **modafinil — STRONG** recommendation;
- clarithromycin — conditional;
- methylphenidate — conditional;
- pitolisant — conditional;
- sodium oxybate — conditional.

Editorial rules:
- preserve recommendation strength;
- these are disease-specific clinician-guided treatments;
- do not translate to self-treatment of unexplained sleepiness.

### 6. 2022 lower-sodium oxybate phase 3 randomized-withdrawal trial

PMID: 34942138

- 154 participants entered safety population;
- 115 randomized to placebo vs continued lower-sodium oxybate after open-label optimization;
- ESS worsened after switch to placebo but remained stable with continued treatment;
- least-squares mean difference **-6.5 (95% CI -8.0 to -5.0)**;
- common treatment-emergent adverse events included nausea, headache, dizziness, anxiety and vomiting.

Editorial use:
- strong efficacy signal;
- randomized-withdrawal design must remain visible;
- industry funding should not be hidden in research interpretation.

### 7. 2025 sodium oxybate placebo-controlled RCT

PMID: 40359459

- 45 adults randomized;
- adjusted ESS difference **-6.86 (95% CI -9.73 to -4.00)**;
- IHSS difference **-11.61**;
- MWT latency difference **14.75 minutes**;
- treatment-emergent adverse events in 81.8% vs 26.1% placebo.

Editorial use:
- independent modern randomized efficacy anchor;
- do not provide dosing guidance in the public article.

### 8. Narcolepsy diagnostic-criteria review

PMID: 27359185

Use for:
- NT1 vs NT2 distinction;
- cataplexy/orexin specificity;
- MSLT/SOREMP role.

## Core distinctions to preserve

### IH ≠ simply long sleep

Some patients have long sleep duration, others are dominated by EDS/sleep inertia.

### IH ≠ ordinary sleep inertia

Sleep inertia occurs in healthy people and after sleep deprivation.
IH can produce unusually severe/prolonged sleep inertia, but symptom alone is not diagnostic.

### IH ≠ narcolepsy type 1

NT1:
- cataplexy and/or orexin deficiency with required objective context.

IH:
- no cataplexy;
- no established orexin-deficiency biomarker.

### IH vs NT2 = genuine gray zone

Do not overstate certainty when:
- no cataplexy;
- borderline MSLT;
- variable SOREMP count;
- long sleep phenotype.

## MSLT guardrails

Never say:
- “A normal MSLT rules out IH.”
- “Two SOREMPs prove a biologically distinct NT2 disease in every case.”

Preferred wording:
> MSLT is important but imperfect, and the IH-vs-NT2 boundary can be unstable across repeat testing.

## Long-sleep measurement rule

Long total sleep duration can be a major IH phenotype.

Actigraphy, sleep logs and prolonged sleep measurement may add useful information in selected cases.

Do not reduce IH diagnosis to daytime nap latency alone.

## Differential diagnosis rules

Before interpreting primary hypersomnolence:
- exclude insufficient sleep;
- consider circadian delay/shift work;
- evaluate OSA when indicated;
- review sedating medications/substances;
- consider medical/psychiatric contributors.

## Treatment evidence rules

### IH
AASM:
- modafinil strong;
- several alternatives conditional.

### Narcolepsy
AASM strong recommendations include multiple agents beyond modafinil.

Do not imply the treatment menus are identical or interchangeable.

## Oxybate rule

Allowed:
- randomized evidence supports clinically meaningful IH symptom improvement.

Not allowed:
- dosing schedules;
- self-selection advice;
- “oxybate response proves IH.”

## Treatment response ≠ diagnosis

Wake-promoting or sedating/wake-stabilizing response is not specific enough to establish disease identity.

## Supplement inheritance rule

Do not transfer:
- “energy supplement” evidence → IH treatment;
- magnesium sleep evidence → sleep inertia treatment;
- melatonin circadian evidence → central hypersomnolence treatment;
- caffeine alertness evidence → narcolepsy/IH diagnosis or disease therapy.

- “IH means everyone sleeps 11+ hours.”
- “Sleep inertia alone diagnoses IH.”
- “Cataplexy is part of ordinary IH.”
- “MSLT perfectly separates IH and NT2.”
- “A normal MSLT excludes long-sleep IH.”
- “Treatment response proves the diagnosis.”
- “IH medications are general treatments for being tired.”
