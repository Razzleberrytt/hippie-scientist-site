## DE18-01 — Peppermint oil in IBS: choose the prespecified endpoint, not the best-looking secondary scale

**Earlier favorable RCT:** Cappello et al. (2007), [PMID 17420159](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17420159/). Fifty-seven Rome II IBS patients with selected differential diagnoses excluded; four-week double-blind test of enteric-coated peppermint oil versus placebo. More than 50% baseline total IBS symptom-score reduction at four weeks was reported in **75% peppermint vs 38% placebo (P < .009)**. This is one small, narrowly selected population with a composite symptom scale, not proof for all IBS phenotypes.

**Larger prespecified endpoint test:** Weerts et al., *Gastroenterology* 2020, [PMID 31470006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31470006/), DOI 10.1053/j.gastro.2019.08.026; 190 randomized, 189 analyzed across two distinct oil-delivery formulations and placebo. FDA-defined abdominal-pain responders were **46.8% small-intestinal release**, **41.3% ileocolonic release**, **34.4% placebo**, **neither active group significantly superior to placebo** on this prespecified primary endpoint (P=.170 and .385); EMA-defined overall relief also **null**. The small-intestinal formulation did improve some **secondary** pain/discomfort/severity outcomes (P=.016, .020, .020). Mild adverse events more frequent on both active formulations.

**Additional independent test:** Nee et al., *Am J Gastroenterol* 2021, [PMID 34319275](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34319275/), DOI 10.14309/ajg.0000000000001395; six-week Rome IV placebo-controlled study. Both groups improved considerably from baseline, but mean IBS severity score change did **not** favor peppermint versus placebo (P=.97); secondary endpoints also **null**.

**Boundary/disposition:** Do not rebrand 2020 positive secondary measures as a positive FDA primary, pool these nonidentical endpoints, or infer equivalence for peppermint tea, topical oil or different delivery technology. **P0 claim audit** of any categorical IBS efficacy language, with primary outcome given first.

## DE18-02 — Tart cherry for insomnia: two pilot crossover signals do not establish a treatment

**Pilot A:** Pigeon et al., *J Med Food* 2010, [PMID 20438325](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20438325/), DOI 10.1089/jmf.2009.0096. Double-blind crossover of a **proprietary tart-cherry beverage** against placebo for two weeks per period with washout; **15 older adults** with chronic insomnia. Compared with placebo, insomnia-severity and time awake after sleep onset improved, but sleep latency, total sleep time and sleep efficiency **did not show significant placebo-adjusted improvement**. Within-beverage improvements across sleep diary variables are not evidence of all measures beating placebo.

**Pilot B:** Losso et al., *Am J Ther* 2018, [PMID 28901958](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28901958/). Balanced two-week crossover with polysomnography: **11 randomized, three excluded for sleep apnea, eight completed**. The authors reported approximately **84 additional PSG-measured minutes** of sleep during cherry exposure (P=.0182) and PSQI sleep-efficiency improvement, with other questionnaires **null**. A serum tryptophan-metabolism signal and an **in-vitro cell** experiment are not a causal demonstration of clinical action in humans.

**Boundary/disposition:** Different tiny selected groups, crossover/order effects, short treatment periods, multiple endpoints and beverage composition prevent broad insomnia-cure claims. No evidence that generic juice concentrate, capsules, gummies or the claimed mechanisms are clinically interchangeable. **P1** formulation and hierarchy check before consumer article amendment.

## DE18-03 — Cranberry capsules for asymptomatic urinary markers: not the same as preventing symptomatic UTI

**Original:** Juthani-Mehta et al., *JAMA* 2016, [PMID 27787564](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27787564/), DOI 10.1001/jama.2016.16141. **185 women** aged at least 65 in nursing homes, one-year, randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial; 147 completed. The **primary outcome** was bacteriuria plus pyuria on periodic tests, **not symptomatic infection**.

**Primary:** Adjusted bacteriuria-plus-pyuria prevalence **29.1% cranberry vs 29.0% placebo**, OR **1.01** (95% CI .61–1.66), P=.98: **null**. Symptomatic UTI events were only **10 vs 12**, without statistically significant between-group difference, so absence of a symptomatic benefit here is not a precise ruling on every recurrence-prevention population.

**Boundary/disposition:** Nursing-home residents frequently have asymptomatic urine findings; these biomarkers must not be mislabeled a diagnosed symptomatic UTI. This one cranberry capsule specification is not juice, every cranberry extract or a study of healthy younger women with confirmed recurrent symptomatic UTI. **P0** review of UTI wording; avoid both unsupported universal benefit and unsupported universal no-benefit claims.

## DE18-04 — Oral lavender Silexan for GAD: a formulation-specific signal, not equivalence to aromatherapy

**Original:** Kasper et al., *Int J Neuropsychopharmacol* 2014, [PMID 24456909](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24456909/), DOI 10.1017/S1461145714000017. Ten-week, double-blind, double-dummy **539-adult GAD trial** of a **specified oral lavender oil preparation, Silexan**, in two arms, paroxetine active comparator and placebo.

**Primary:** HAM-A anxiety scores declined by mean **14.1** and **12.8** points in the two Silexan groups, versus **9.5** placebo; both reported **P < .01 vs placebo**. Paroxetine comparator showed P=.10 versus placebo in the full analysis set; **this does not establish formal non-inferiority or superiority to a modern individualized prescription regimen**. The authors reported comparable short-term event frequency to placebo for Silexan, but long-term and population-specific safety were not tested here.

**Boundary/disposition:** Oral pharmaceutical-grade lavender oil is **not** lavender essential oil for inhalation, massage or self-mixed ingestion. Full conflicts and sponsor ties require review (the original author affiliations include a product manufacturer). No instructions to replace GAD treatment, nor a claim that all lavender is clinically effective. **P0** preparation, interaction and medicinal-claim audit.

## DE18-05 — Glucosamine plus chondroitin: overall knee-pain endpoint beats a post-hoc subgroup headline

**Original:** Clegg et al., *NEJM* 2006 GAIT, [PMID 16495392](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16495392/), DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa052771. **1,583 people** with painful knee osteoarthritis randomized to glucosamine, chondroitin, the combination, celecoxib or placebo for **24 weeks**; allowed rescue acetaminophen. The prespecified overall outcome was **at least 20% reduction in knee pain**.

**Result:** Glucosamine and chondroitin **alone or together did not significantly beat placebo overall**. The combination looked more promising in the **smaller moderate-to-severe pain subgroup**, which was explicitly exploratory and cannot displace the overall primary null or be treated as prospectively validated selection guidance. A positive celecoxib active control does not prove the supplement combinations effective.

**Boundary/disposition:** Knee pain is not the same as cartilage regrowth or slowed joint-space narrowing; other GAIT publications share participants and must not be counted as independent confirmation. Products vary by salt, source and combination. **P0** ensure primary trial null stays adjacent to any subgroup language; separate clinical guidance and interaction review.

## DE18-06 — Valerian and insomnia: 405-person primary null vs smaller preparation-specific favorable trial

**RCT A:** Oxman et al., *PLoS One* 2007, [PMID 17940604](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17940604/). Television/web-recruited, blinded trial: **405 analyzed adults** with insomnia symptoms and two weeks of valerian vs placebo. The prespecified clinically important self-rated sleep-quality improvement was **29% vs 21%**, difference **7.5 percentage points**, 95% CI **−0.9 to 15.9**, P=.08 — **primary null**. A separate global self-rating at trial end favored valerian modestly (P=.04): secondary self-report does not overturn the primary.

**RCT B:** A 2023 eight-week randomized study, [PMID 37899385](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37899385/), included **80 adults with sleep complaints**, **72 completers** in efficacy analysis. A **specific standardized Valeriana officinalis preparation** improved several PSQI and wrist-actigraphy outcomes vs placebo, with PSG assessments in a subset of 20 per arm. Small sample, short duration and specific formulation limit certainty.

**Boundary/disposition:** These are separate randomized cohorts and not a simple contradiction solved by publication year; recruitment, extract characterization, instruments and duration differ. Neither experiment establishes rare adverse-event or long-term medication-interaction safety. **P1** no class-wide 'clinically proven insomnia cure' language; preserve both directions.

## DE18-07 — Bacopa memory marketing must disclose a newer negative primary cognitive study

**Older small positive:** Calabrese et al., *J Altern Complement Med* 2008, [PMID 18611150](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18611150/), DOI 10.1089/acm.2008.0018. **54 adults at least 65**, 48 completers, randomized placebo trial of standardized whole-plant extract for 12 weeks. Delayed auditory-verbal recall and Stroop task favored Bacopa, but other attention/working-memory tasks and mood measures were **null**. The trial was not a test of dementia incidence or daily functioning.

**Newer primary cognitive null:** Lopresti and Smith, *Clin Drug Investig* 2025, [PMID 41091332](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41091332/), DOI 10.1007/s40261-025-01492-1. **101 randomized middle-aged adults** with self-reported cognition concerns, **87 completers** over 12 weeks using a different branded extract. **Primary verbal learning P=.391, attention P=.713, working memory P=.610**: none outperformed placebo. Some **secondary** self-rated stress/fatigue outcomes improved; adverse reactions, principally GI complaints/headaches, occurred more frequently in the active arm (**P=.024**). Reported author/contract research ties to nutraceutical companies merit disclosure review.

**Boundary/disposition:** No justified blanket claim that Bacopa improves intelligence, reverses cognitive decline or prevents dementia. Different populations and products preclude naïve pooling; the newer primary null belongs alongside earlier favorable task endpoints. **P1** nootropic evidence audit.

## DE18-08 — L-theanine: a 30-person crossover signal cannot prove treatment of anxiety or insomnia

**Original:** Hidese et al., *Nutrients* 2019, [PMID 31623400](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31623400/), DOI 10.3390/nu11102362. Randomized, double-blind placebo-controlled crossover, **30 generally healthy adults without major psychiatric illness**, four-week intervention periods.

**Outcomes:** Trait-anxiety, depression self-rating and subjective Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index scores decreased in active treatment periods (reported P=.006, .019, .013, respectively), and selected executive/verbal-fluency task measures improved. Some PSQI subcomponents improved compared with placebo. These are multiple, mostly self-report/task-level endpoints in a very small study, not an adequately powered trial for **diagnosed GAD**, objective insomnia remission or clinical treatment superiority.

**Boundary/disposition:** Do not generalize to caffeine-containing tea, combination sleep gummies, severe psychiatric illness or long-term use. Clarify which contrasts were within-period versus specifically between placebo and intervention before making a graded quantitative claim. **P1** stress-versus-condition and multiplicity review; no medication-substitution language.

## DE18-09 — Elderberry and laboratory-confirmed influenza: a small historical positive trial vs later null

**Original A:** Zakay-Rones et al., *J Int Med Res* 2004, [PMID 15080016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15080016/), DOI 10.1177/147323000403200205. A 60-person randomized double-blind placebo trial of a specified elderberry syrup initiated early in influenza-like illness reported symptom relief approximately **four days earlier** than placebo; small, historically specific preparation and illness enrollment.

**Original B:** Macknin et al. 2020, [PMID 32929634](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32929634/). **87 PCR-positive influenza participants**, including children and adults, randomized to elderberry preparation or placebo, with concurrent oseltamivir permitted. Prespecified time to all symptoms none/mild for a defined interval averaged **5.3 vs 4.9 days** (elderberry vs placebo; **P=.57**, primary null); complete symptom resolution **8.6 vs 8.7 days**, P=.87.

**Reconciliation:** Two independent randomized groups with different ages, product preparations, diagnostic standards and antiviral cointervention. Do not cherry-pick the 2004 small positive result to call elderberry an antiviral treatment, nor extrapolate the later null to all infection prevention or every product. Neither shows prevention of hospitalization, complications or transmission. **Existing article reference PMID 15080016 must be reused, not counted again as a novel citation.** **P0** site elderberry claims audit; conventional influenza care must not be displaced.

## DE18-10 — Omega-3 did not prevent late-life depression in VITAL-DEP; risk direction deserves accurate reporting

**Original:** Okereke et al., *JAMA* 2021, [PMID 34932079](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34932079/), DOI 10.1001/jama.2021.21187. Double-blind factorial **VITAL-DEP** substudy, **18,353 older adults** with no active clinically relevant depression symptoms at baseline; median treatment follow-up **5.3 years**. Tested **marine EPA/DHA** versus placebo as a **prevention** intervention; separately randomized vitamin D arm within the same research program.

**Prespecified co-primary results:** Composite incident/recurrent depression or clinically significant symptoms occurred in **651 omega-3 vs 583 placebo** participants; HR **1.13** (95% CI **1.01–1.26**), P=.03: a **small statistically unfavorable direction** for this prevention regimen, not a demonstrated benefit. Longitudinal PHQ-8 mood score difference **0.03 points** (95% CI −.01 to .07), P=.19: **null**. Separate vitamin D depression study in DE14-03 used the same overarching parent trial; preserve lineage, do not count it as fully independent randomization.

**Boundary/disposition:** This is **preventing depression in a particular older, symptom-free-at-baseline population**, not treating people diagnosed with major depressive disorder or testing high-EPA psychiatric adjuvant formulas. Its omega-3 preparation is also not equivalent to dietary fish or the STRENGTH cardiovascular prescription combination reviewed in DE16. **P0** prevent indication transfer and euphemistic reporting of an unfavorable primary comparison; do not suggest starting or stopping psychiatric treatment.
