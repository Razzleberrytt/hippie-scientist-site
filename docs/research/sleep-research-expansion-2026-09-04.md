# Sleep Research Expansion — 2026-09-04

## Goal

Expand the sleep cluster with evidence-dense topics that add new search intent, improve topical authority, and preserve null or conflicting findings rather than publishing repetitive “best sleep aid” copy.

## Added article targets

### 1. Tart cherry for sleep

**New page:** `/articles/tart-cherry-for-sleep/`

**Evidence position:** Limited and heterogeneous.

Key evidence:

- 2025 systematic review: 7 interventional studies; 3 reported improvement in at least one sleep indicator and 3 reported increased melatonin-related measures, but dose, population, formulation, duration, and outcomes varied substantially. PMID 40964149.
- 2018 older-adult insomnia crossover pilot: only 8 completers after exclusions; polysomnographic sleep time increased by 84 minutes. PMID 28901958. Treat as a fragile pilot estimate, not an expected effect size.
- 2010 chronic-insomnia pilot: improvement in wake after sleep onset / insomnia severity, but no placebo-separated improvement in sleep latency, total sleep time, or sleep efficiency. PMID 20438325.
- 2012 healthy-adult crossover study: increased urinary melatonin metabolite output with improvements in several actigraphy-derived outcomes. PMID 22038497.
- 2024 randomized crossover powder study in adults with overweight/obesity and sleep problems: no significant effect on measured sleep outcomes. PMID 39683518.
- 2022 controlled trial in healthy adults: no meaningful sleep benefit from Montmorency tart-cherry supplementation. PMID 35790450.

**Editorial rule:** Never convert “contains melatonin” into “works like melatonin.” Keep juice, concentrate, powder, and capsules separate unless direct comparability exists.

### 2. Saffron for sleep

**New page:** `/articles/saffron-for-sleep/`

**Evidence position:** Moderate short-term evidence, mainly subjective and product-specific.

Key evidence:

- 2022 meta-analysis of RCTs: pooled improvements in PSQI, ISI, and RSQ compared with placebo. PMID 35325766.
- 2023 systematic review: 5 RCTs, 379 participants, initial support for improved sleep quality. PMID 37484523.
- 2020 RCT: standardized saffron extract improved several self-reported sleep outcomes in adults with poor sleep. PMID 32056539.
- 2021 multi-dose RCT: replicated subjective sleep improvements in adults with unsatisfactory sleep. PMID 34438361.
- 2021 controlled trial using another saffron extract: positive sleep-quality signal in participants with mild-to-moderate sleep problems associated with anxiety. PMID 33925432.
- 2025 RCT in 165 adults with moderate insomnia: statistically significant but small reduction in Athens Insomnia Scale score, with improvements in some secondary sleep/stress outcomes and several null secondary outcomes. PMID 40698027.

**Editorial rule:** Saffron now deserves a stronger sleep evidence position than many herbs, but the correct summary is “modest short-term benefit with product-specific uncertainty,” not “clinically proven insomnia treatment.”

### 3. Chamomile for sleep

**New page:** `/articles/chamomile-for-sleep/`

**Evidence position:** Limited, outcome-specific.

Key evidence:

- 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis: favorable signal especially for nighttime awakenings/staying asleep; no consistent improvement in sleep duration, sleep efficiency, or daytime functioning. PMID 39106912.
- 2019 systematic review/meta-analysis: pooled subjective sleep-quality improvement, but only one RCT directly assessed insomnia severity and it was null. PMID 31006899.
- 2011 chronic primary insomnia RCT (34 adults): no significant between-group improvement in total sleep time, efficiency, latency, WASO, awakenings, or sleep quality. PMID 21939549.

**Editorial rule:** Distinguish “some subjective sleep-quality benefit” from “treats insomnia.” Do not transfer extract evidence directly to tea or isolated apigenin.

## Next high-ROI sleep research targets

### L-tryptophan for sleep

A 2022 systematic review/meta-analysis (PMID 33942088) found an overall sleep-quality signal and is a strong candidate for a dedicated article. Priority: verify outcome-level effects, dose-response findings, and safety/serotonergic interaction framing before publication.

### Sleep maintenance vs sleep onset

Create an educational intent page that separates:

- sleep-onset latency,
- wake after sleep onset,
- total sleep time,
- sleep efficiency,
- subjective sleep quality,
- insomnia severity,
- next-day functioning.

This can improve internal evidence discipline because supplement claims should specify which outcome changed instead of collapsing everything into “better sleep.”

### Objective vs subjective sleep measurement

Create a research-literacy article comparing sleep diaries, PSQI/ISI, actigraphy, consumer wearables, and polysomnography. This would support citations from many sleep articles and reduce overinterpretation of questionnaire-only trials.

### Sleep formulation equivalence

A recurring evidence issue across magnesium, tart cherry, saffron, chamomile, valerian, and multi-ingredient stacks is inappropriate extrapolation between formulations. Build an article explaining why extract identity, standardization, dose, matrix, and delivery form matter.

## Evidence-discipline standard

For future sleep expansion, every article should preserve:

1. positive and null outcomes,
2. sample size and population,
3. subjective vs objective measurement,
4. product/formulation directness,
5. duration of treatment,
6. whether the result is statistically significant versus practically large,
7. the highest-level recent synthesis when one exists.

This standard is especially important in sleep because many studies show movement in one outcome while leaving other sleep dimensions unchanged.
