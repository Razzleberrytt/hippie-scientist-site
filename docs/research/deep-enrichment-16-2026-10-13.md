## DE16-01 — Ashwagandha: stress scales and cortisol in one standardized root extract

**Primary:** Chandrasekhar et al., *Indian J Psychol Med* 2012, [PMID 23439798](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23439798/), [DOI 10.4103/0253-7176.106022](https://doi.org/10.4103/0253-7176.106022). Single-center, double-blind randomized placebo-controlled study enrolling **64 adults with a history of chronic stress**. The studied preparation was a particular full-spectrum high-concentration *Withania somnifera* **root extract**, administered for 60 days.

**Observed endpoints:** Relative to placebo, self-report stress-assessment scales favored the extract at day 60 (all reported P < .0001); measured serum cortisol also favored the extract (P = .0006). Mild adverse events were reported as comparable and the authors reported no serious events in this small, brief sample. These are measured stress and cortisol endpoints, not a demonstrated long-term reduction in disease, psychiatric remission, improved objective performance, or all-product safety.

**Directness audit:** A branded or specific root preparation is not interchangeable with every leaf extract, gummy, "withanolide percentage," or multiple-ingredient blend. Cortisol alone is not proof of better mental health. The 60-day trial cannot exclude rare liver, endocrine or medication-related harms; those require separately verified evidence. The paper's stated "safe and effective" conclusion extends beyond what this modest follow-up can establish as a universal claim.

**Editorial disposition:** P1 verify formulation identity, safety warnings and whether existing ashwagandha routes quote trial-specific results rather than class-wide promises; no unreviewed consumer claim promotion.

## DE16-02 — Kava: short positive GAD trial did not hold up in a larger multicenter test

**Primary A:** Sarris et al., *J Clin Psychopharmacol* 2013, [PMID 23635869](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23635869/), [DOI 10.1097/JCP.0b013e318291be67](https://doi.org/10.1097/JCP.0b013e318291be67). Six-week double-blind placebo study enrolling **75 adults** with generalized anxiety disorder, without comorbid mood disorders. After a placebo run-in, the reported intention-to-treat analysis covered **58** eligible participants. The Hamilton anxiety outcome favored a specific aqueous kava extract (P=.046), and reported remission at controlled-phase conclusion was **26% kava vs 6% placebo**. A small trial and run-in-selected sample warrant caution.

**Primary B:** Sarris et al., *Aust N Z J Psychiatry* 2020, [PMID 31813230](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31813230/), [DOI 10.1177/0004867419891246](https://doi.org/10.1177/0004867419891246). A separate **171-person, 16-week, multisite**, double-blind placebo study of aqueous dried-root kava extract in currently unmedicated people diagnosed with GAD found **no statistically significant anxiety advantage** (reported difference favored placebo by 1.37 points, P=.25). Remission **17.4% kava vs 23.8% placebo**, P=.46. Memory complaints and tremor/shakiness were more frequent in the kava arm, as were liver-function test abnormalities; no participant met the paper's specified herb-induced liver-injury criteria.

**Reconciliation:** These are distinct randomized cohorts. Do not select only the 2013 positive result or average endpoint directions casually; the larger, longer negative clinical trial materially changes confidence for *diagnosed* GAD. Absence of an adjudicated injury case in 171 participants is not evidence that rare hepatic injury cannot occur, nor does this establish safety of other extracts, concentrated kavalactone products, or combinations with sedatives.

**Editorial disposition:** **P0** contradiction register, extract matching and liver/sedation interaction review; halt any categorical "kava treats GAD" phrasing pending independent synthesis.

## DE16-03 — Lion's mane: a single favorable task is not a generalized intelligence boost

**Primary A:** Docherty et al., *Nutrients* 2023, [PMID 38004235](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38004235/), [DOI 10.3390/nu15224842](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu15224842). Randomized, blinded placebo-controlled pilot of **41 healthy adults aged 18–45**, examining 60-minute acute and 28-day outcomes after a particular *Hericium erinaceus* preparation. Acute Stroop-task speed improved (P=.005); the 28-day subjective-stress comparison was a **trend at P=.051, not conventionally significant**. The abstract explicitly also notes null and limited negative findings. The authors declared the manufacturer provided investigational product without a design/analysis role.

**Primary B:** Surendran et al., *Front Nutr* 2025, [PMID 40276537](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40276537/), [DOI 10.3389/fnut.2025.1405796](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnut.2025.1405796). In a **separate 18-person** blinded crossover study of healthy 18–35-year-olds, a specific fruiting-body extract produced **no significant overall effect** on global cognition or mood at 90 minutes. A selected pegboard test improved. This source was already considered in DE10-07; **reuse that exact PMID**, not an additional independent discovery attributed to this dossier.

**Directness:** A Stroop or pegboard task is not proof of improved everyday attention, ADHD symptoms, academic grades or dementia prevention. Neither trial establishes the clinical effectiveness of a mushroom-coffee blend, nor can one infer equivalent effects from preparations with different mushroom material or extract ratios. The small samples make task-level multiplicity and replication important.

**Editorial disposition:** P1 compare current mushroom-coffee and lion's mane statements with both global and task-specific outcomes and avoid multiplying counts for repeat source citations.

## DE16-04 — Creatine: laboratory sleep-loss resilience must not become a blanket "brain boost"

**Primary A:** McMorris et al., *Psychopharmacology* 2006, [PMID 16416332](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16416332/). Double-blind comparison of **19 participants** (creatine n=10, placebo n=9) under 24 hours of sleep deprivation with intermittent mild exercise. After seven days of creatine loading, several cognitive/psychomotor outcomes and mood-change measures deteriorated less in the creatine group at 24 hours, but **plasma catecholamine and cortisol differences were null**.

**Primary B:** Gordji-Nejad et al., *Scientific Reports* 2024, [PMID 38418482](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38418482/), [DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-54249-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-54249-9). A separate placebo-controlled laboratory sleep-deprivation study coupled serial phosphorus/proton magnetic-resonance spectroscopy with cognitive tests after a **single high experimental creatine dose**. The authors reported measurable changes in cerebral high-energy-phosphate-related signals and improved processing/cognitive performance during approximately 21 hours of sleep deprivation. A provocative mechanistic readout does not establish chronic cognition benefit or ordinary consumer dose-equivalence.

**Lineage and boundary:** Two independent human experiments, but different dose timing, tasks, measurements and protocols; do **not** pool their task outcomes as if one validated a universal effect. The high experimental single-dose design must not be converted to a public self-dosing recommendation. No diagnosis of cognitive impairment or improvement in rested, healthy daily-life function is demonstrated by these specific experiments.

**Editorial disposition:** P1 crosswalk to existing creatine-brain-health route, label sleep-deprivation population and distinguish human task effect from MRI spectroscopy biomarkers and healthy-rested cognition.

## DE16-05 — Collagen and knee pain: population and endpoint can reverse the sales headline

**Primary A (null):** Bongers et al., *Applied Physiology, Nutrition, and Metabolism* 2020, [PMID 31990581](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31990581/), [DOI 10.1139/apnm-2019-0654](https://doi.org/10.1139/apnm-2019-0654). In a **167-person** placebo-controlled 12-week trial among physically active middle-aged/older adults with self-reported knee pain, both arms improved on pain and function. The between-group visual-analog pain change was **-1.6 vs -1.9**, P=.42; KOOS and Lysholm improvements did not differ significantly. Inflammatory and cartilage/bone markers were also not improved by supplementation.

**Primary B (positive):** Zdzieblik et al., *Nutrients* 2021, [PMID 33562729](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33562729/), [DOI 10.3390/nu13020523](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu13020523). A distinct placebo-controlled **180-completer** 12-week trial in physically active adults **aged 18–30** with exercise-related knee pain and without diagnosed joint disease reported greater exercise-pain reduction for a particular type-I collagen peptide preparation (**-21.9 vs -15.6 mm; P=.024**).

**Reconciliation:** These two trials cannot be described as a direct failed replication because age ranges, knee-pain populations, preparations and task outcomes differ. Equally, the younger cohort's exercise-pain response cannot overturn the null older-adult trial or establish cartilage regeneration, fracture prevention or osteoarthritis structural repair. Confirm full-text product funding and prespecified endpoint hierarchy before clinical promotion.

**Editorial disposition:** P1 product/age/endpoint matrix. Keep both positive and null direct randomized contrasts on the same review card; do not treat generic "collagen works for joints" as evidence-grade language.

## DE16-06 — Panax ginseng in advanced cancer: before-after fatigue gains were not treatment effect

**Primary:** Yennurajalingam et al., *Oncologist* 2017, [PMID 28874596](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28874596/). Double-blind placebo-controlled trial of standardized **Panax ginseng** among patients with advanced cancer and cancer-related fatigue; **127 randomized and 112 evaluable**. The prespecified primary comparison was change in FACIT-F fatigue score to day 29.

**Result:** Both groups improved meaningfully from baseline. Average day-29 change was **7.5 points in Panax vs 6.5 points in placebo; P=.67**, a **null between-arm result**. The within-ginseng-arm P value is not an efficacy comparison.

**Species/cohort guard:** **Panax ginseng** is not the same botanical species as **Panax quinquefolius** (American ginseng), which had a different cancer-survivor trial already assessed in DE12-03 ([PMID 23853057](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23853057/)). Advanced cancer and post-treatment survivor fatigue are different populations. The American-ginseng study's primary four-week result was P=.07; report its later follow-up separately rather than pretending the studies resolve one identical endpoint. No self-treatment advice for active cancer or co-administered oncology drugs may be inferred.

**Editorial disposition:** P1 make species, clinical setting, primary endpoint and trial independence explicit; avoid "ginseng combats cancer fatigue" promotional shorthand.

## DE16-07 — Curcumin for major depression: later-week symptom signal is not medication equivalence

**Primary trial:** Lopresti et al., *J Affect Disord* 2014, [PMID 25046624](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25046624/), [DOI 10.1016/j.jad.2014.06.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2014.06.001). Randomized, double-blind placebo-controlled eight-week study of **56 participants with major depressive disorder** using a specified curcumin preparation. The primary instrument was IDS-SR30.

**Timed finding:** During **weeks 0–4 both groups improved**; over **weeks 4–8**, a group-by-time comparison favored curcumin on total IDS-SR30 (**P=.045**) and mood subscale (**P=.014**). Trait-anxiety comparison was a **non-significant trend** (P=.097). This is partial, time-window-specific support from a small trial, not a claim of proven antidepressant parity, prevention or long-term remission.

**Same-trial secondary analysis:** Lopresti et al., *Eur Neuropsychopharmacol* 2015, [PMID 25523883](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25523883/), [DOI 10.1016/j.euroneuro.2014.11.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euroneuro.2014.11.015) examined biomarkers from this **same randomized study**. It is **not an independent treatment replication**, even if indexed under its own PMID. Exploratory biomarker predictors cannot independently prove a mechanism or treatment benefit.

**Directness:** MDD symptom scales in selected adults are not interchangeable with everyday stress, antioxidant assays, or osteoarthritis pain studied in DE14-04. No basis to recommend stopping or replacing prescribed antidepressants; safety, drug interaction and comparative-treatment evidence needs separate direct review.

**Editorial disposition:** **P0** prevent duplicated clinical trial counts, preserve null early-time window and keep treatment-substitution warnings alongside any cautiously presented research.

## DE16-08 — STRENGTH: omega-3 formulation did not reduce clinical cardiovascular events

**Primary:** Nicholls et al., *JAMA* 2020, [PMID 33190147](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33190147/), [DOI 10.1001/jama.2020.22258](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2020.22258). Large randomized, double-blind trial comparing a particular high-exposure **EPA+DHA carboxylic-acid formulation** with corn-oil placebo in statin-treated patients with elevated cardiovascular risk, hypertriglyceridemia and low HDL. The trial was discontinued early for lack of observed clinical benefit.

**Outcome boundary:** The prespecified composite of cardiovascular death, nonfatal heart attack/stroke, coronary revascularization and unstable-angina hospitalization **did not show a significant benefit**. Omega-3 effects on circulating lipids are not a surrogate license to assert reduced myocardial infarction or mortality in this population. The formulation differs from prescription purified EPA, mixed over-the-counter fish oil and ordinary fish consumption; do not transfer this null directly across products without studying the distinct trials.

**Safety follow-up:** Review the original paper's atrial-fibrillation event definitions, denominators and competing safety outcomes at full text before publishing exact risk rates or broad class-wide warnings. This investigation intentionally does not state unconfirmed subgroup percentages. Do not use these outcomes to advise stopping clinician-prescribed therapies.

**Editorial disposition:** **P0** evidence-vs-biomarker boundary for omega-3 marketing and direct reference linking; not an across-the-board conclusion about all marine omega-3 interventions.

## DE16-09 — CoQ10 blood pressure: clinic, ambulatory and pooled evidence are not interchangeable

**Primary:** Young et al., *Am J Hypertens* 2012, [PMID 22113168](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22113168/). Double-blind 12-week crossover comparison in **30 patients with metabolic syndrome and inadequately controlled hypertension** despite unchanged antihypertensive therapy. CoQ10 did **not** significantly improve the primary **24-hour ambulatory** systolic (P=.60) or diastolic BP (P=.12). Certain *secondary* daytime diastolic BP-load thresholds changed; these should not be substituted for the null primary endpoint.

**Evidence synthesis (not an additional randomized cohort):** Ho et al., *Cochrane Database Syst Rev* 2016, [PMID 26935713](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26935713/), [DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD007435.pub3](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007435.pub3). Because one trial was judged high risk of bias, the authors pooled just **two RCTs (50 total participants)** and found no statistically significant reduction in systolic BP (**-3.68 mmHg, 95% CI -8.86 to 1.49**) or diastolic BP (**-2.03 mmHg, 95% CI -4.86 to 0.81**).

**Reconciliation:** A literature search yielding several small favorable before-after estimates does not overturn controlled ambulatory BP findings and restricted-quality synthesis. The Cochrane review includes the Young trial, so **do not count review and trial as two independent RCTs**. Neither outcome establishes fewer strokes or heart attacks. The small number of high-quality participants limits confidence; it is not an absolute proof of no effect anywhere.

**Editorial disposition:** P1 audit any CoQ10 hypertension claim for measurement method, review-date, placebo comparison, and source independence. Migraine and statin-myalgia CoQ10 investigations in DE14/DE11 have separate endpoints.

## DE16-10 — Dried kratom leaf pharmacokinetics: measured plasma 7-OH is not an isolated-product trial

**Primary:** Huestis et al., *Molecules* 2024, [PMID 38474495](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38474495/), [DOI 10.3390/molecules29050984](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules29050984). Controlled, double-blind, placebo-controlled dose-escalation research used **encapsulated dried kratom leaf powder** across single-dose and **15-daily-exposure** phases with laboratory measurement of mitragynine and 7-hydroxymitragynine in plasma. The paper reported proportional mitragynine/7-OH peak-exposure changes over the tested **leaf-powder exposures**, plus accumulation and different time-to-peak/half-life estimates for the two analytes.

**Critical exposure firewall:** This is **not** a randomized efficacy or comparative-safety trial of isolated synthetic 7-OH, concentrated 7-OH tablets, pseudoindoxyl, novel analogs, or retail products with unidentified contents. Detection of 7-OH after leaf ingestion does not establish which compound caused subjective effects or clinical outcomes. In vitro receptor-pathway statements in the abstract are not human proof of absence of respiratory depression, dependence, withdrawal or overdose risk.

**Conflict of interest:** The PubMed record discloses that Johnson Foods funded the clinical study of its MitraLeaf powder and that multiple authors were paid consultants; two authors also reported American Kratom Association consultancy. This financial context belongs beside any presentation of the data. A pharmacokinetic protocol should never be turned into a suggested dosing schedule.

**Editorial disposition:** **P0** link as human exposure/metabolism evidence on existing mitragynine/7-OH authority pages only after independent claim-level review; never let source-count growth falsely signal a clinical safety clearance.
