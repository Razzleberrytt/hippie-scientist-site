# Shift Work Sleep Disorder — Evidence Dossier — 2026-09-04

## Research question

What should the sleep cluster say about shift work disorder (SWD) now that the AASM has issued updated guidance?

## Core verdict

Shift work disorder is best treated as a **systems problem** rather than a supplement-selection problem. The evidence supports multiple conditional interventions targeting different outcomes: excessive sleepiness during work, daytime insomnia after work, and partial circadian adaptation.

## Highest-value evidence

### 2025-2026 AASM clinical practice guideline

- AASM clinical practice guideline finalized in 2025 and published in SLEEP in 2026.
- DOI: 10.1093/sleep/zsag091.0777
- Recommendations are conditional rather than strong.
- Key supported strategies include:
  - bright light during the night shift;
  - caffeine before and/or during night work;
  - strategic pre-shift naps;
  - clockwise rather than counterclockwise rotation;
  - snack/no meal rather than a full meal during the night shift for excessive sleepiness;
  - selected melatonin use for daytime sleep and schedule transitions;
  - CBT-I for shift workers with insomnia symptoms;
  - selected prescription alertness-promoting therapy for clinically diagnosed SWD.

### AASM systematic review and GRADE assessment

- Version: May 2025.
- 3,045 records screened; 118 studies provided data suitable for statistical analysis.
- Evidence includes controlled simulated-shift studies, field studies in shift workers, and a smaller number of trials in formally diagnosed SWD.
- Important limitation: much of the evidence base is not composed of long-term real-world trials in clinically diagnosed SWD.

### Light-therapy meta-analysis

- PMID: 39747347
- 11 randomized/randomized-crossover studies.
- Pooled total sleep time improvement: ~32.5 minutes.
- Pooled sleep-efficiency improvement: ~2.9 percentage points.
- Significant heterogeneity in illuminance, timing, dose and population.

### 2025 clinical review

- PMID: 39864934
- Reinforces that not all shift workers develop SWD.
- Emphasizes differential diagnosis and individualized management.

## Key distinctions to preserve

### Shift worker vs shift work disorder

Working nights does not automatically mean SWD. SWD requires clinically meaningful symptoms linked to the recurring schedule.

### Fixed nights vs rotating shifts

Stable night workers may be able to achieve partial circadian adaptation. Frequently rotating workers usually cannot and should not be given the same light/melatonin advice.

### Alertness intervention vs sleep intervention

Caffeine and bright light may improve work-shift alertness but can undermine subsequent sleep if poorly timed.

### Sedation vs circadian alignment

Melatonin is not merely a sedative. Timing determines both sleepiness and circadian signaling.

### Sleep quantity vs safety

Driving risk, sleep inertia and workplace performance are critical outcomes, not side notes.

## Claim guardrails

1. Do not say all night workers should take melatonin.
2. Do not give a universal bright-light clock time without knowing schedule and desired circadian direction.
3. Do not imply that complete circadian adaptation is realistic for rotating workers.
4. Do not interpret conditional AASM recommendations as high-certainty universal prescriptions.
5. Preserve sleep inertia warnings after pre-shift naps.
6. Preserve caffeine's tradeoff with post-shift sleep.
7. Do not turn the meal-timing recommendation into a blanket fasting recommendation.
8. Distinguish clinically diagnosed SWD from ordinary sleep restriction after occasional late shifts.
