# Antihistamines and Sleep — Evidence Dossier — 2026-09-04

## Research question

What can the site defensibly say about first-generation antihistamines used as OTC sleep aids, especially diphenhydramine and doxylamine?

## Verdict

**Diphenhydramine:** limited short-term hypnotic evidence; not supported as a chronic-insomnia treatment. Sedation is real, but objective benefit is inconsistent, tolerance can develop rapidly, and next-day impairment is measurable.

**Doxylamine:** sedating and widely used, but direct placebo-controlled insomnia evidence is substantially thinner than marketing familiarity suggests.

## High-value evidence anchors

### AASM chronic insomnia guideline

- PMID: 27998379
- Recommendation: clinicians should not use diphenhydramine for sleep-onset or sleep-maintenance insomnia in adults.
- Important framing: this addresses chronic insomnia treatment; it does not deny acute sedation.

### Older placebo-controlled diphenhydramine trial

- PMID: 6348106
- 111 mildly/moderately insomniac primary-care patients.
- 50 mg at bedtime in a crossover design.
- Reported improvement in several subjective sleep outcomes versus placebo.
- Supports a short-term hypnotic signal.

### Mixed later insomnia trial

- PMID: 16335333
- Diphenhydramine produced modest subjective improvements.
- Few placebo comparisons were significant.
- No significant group difference on polysomnographic sleep-continuity variables.
- Useful guardrail against converting subjective sedation into claims about objective sleep architecture.

### Older-adult randomized crossover trial

- PMID: 18344728
- Mean participant age ~74 years.
- Compared temazepam, diphenhydramine and placebo over 14-night periods.
- Temazepam showed broader placebo-separated sleep benefit; diphenhydramine did not reproduce that pattern.
- Older-adult safety context is especially relevant.

### Rapid tolerance to sedation

- PMID: 12352276
- Randomized double-blind crossover study.
- Diphenhydramine 50 mg twice daily for four days.
- Initial sleepiness and psychomotor impairment largely disappeared by day four.
- Do not turn this into an exact bedtime rule, because the study evaluated daytime sedative effects rather than nightly insomnia efficacy.

### Next-day residual central effect

- PMID: 21105284
- Nighttime diphenhydramine 50 mg produced substantial next-morning brain H1-receptor occupancy on PET.
- Supports the concept of residual next-day CNS exposure.

### Doxylamine evidence limitation

- PMID: 28777365
- Adults with short-term insomnia improved during short-term doxylamine treatment.
- Critical limitation: two doxylamine products were compared with each other rather than against placebo.
- Therefore the study does not estimate placebo-separated doxylamine efficacy.

### Older-adult anticholinergic risk

- 2023 AGS Beers Criteria
- DOI: 10.1111/jgs.18372
- First-generation antihistamines with strong anticholinergic properties are potentially inappropriate for many adults age 65+.

## Claim guardrails

1. Do not say diphenhydramine "doesn't work." It has short-term sedative and subjective sleep evidence.
2. Do not equate sedation with durable insomnia treatment.
3. Do not generalize diphenhydramine evidence to doxylamine or to all antihistamines.
4. Do not use the rapid-tolerance study to claim a universal exact number of nights before efficacy disappears.
5. Do not imply that OTC status means suitable for chronic nightly use.
6. Preserve next-day performance as a clinically relevant endpoint.
7. Treat older adults as a distinct higher-risk population because of anticholinergic burden.
