## Suggested spoken script (~55–70 seconds depending on natural delivery)

“Here are two chemicals related to the kratom plant: **mitragynine** and **7-hydroxymitragynine**, usually shortened to **7-OH**. Their names are complicated. The basic idea isn't.

An **atom** is one of matter's tiny building blocks. A **molecule** is a group of atoms connected together. These flat drawings are maps showing which atoms connect.

Look at this spot on the 7-OH map. It includes an oxygen-and-hydrogen pair. Scientists call that pair a **hydroxyl group**. You don't need to memorize the name—just see what changed between the two maps.

Now the flat drawing becomes a 3D model. It's **one possible shape estimated by a computer**. It is not a recording of a real molecule moving inside your brain.

There's an important limit: the drawing alone cannot tell us how strong a substance is or exactly how it affects someone.

If you want to check these maps, the chemical records are in **PubChem**, a public chemistry database. Their record numbers are on screen.

Good science starts with what we can actually check—not what a picture makes us imagine.”
