# U-47700

> **Overdose warning:** U-47700 can produce a classic opioid overdose with slowed or stopped breathing. If someone is difficult to wake or breathing abnormally, call 911 and give naloxone if available.

## Quick answer

**U-47700 is a non-fentanyl synthetic opioid and potent mu-opioid receptor agonist.** It was originally described in medicinal-chemistry research and later appeared in counterfeit pills and illicit products.

Human case literature documents decreased consciousness, depressed breathing, pulmonary edema and fatal intoxications.

## What the clinical review found

A 2017 review identified 16 patients from the available human literature. Survivors commonly presented with decreased mental status and reduced respiratory rate consistent with an opioid toxidrome. Fatal cases often involved pulmonary edema.

No controlled clinical safety trials established a safe recreational exposure.

## Why routine screens can miss it

Standard opioid immunoassays may not recognize U-47700.

Published clinical reports required chromatographic and mass-spectrometric methods for confirmation.

A negative routine opioid screen therefore should not overrule a convincing opioid toxidrome.

## Counterfeit-product risk

U-47700 has been identified in counterfeit opioid products and in multi-drug mixtures. That means pill appearance or a seller's label cannot establish chemical identity.

## Naloxone

Because U-47700 acts through mu-opioid receptors, naloxone is appropriate when overdose is suspected. Emergency monitoring is still needed because duration, amount and co-drugs may be unknown.

## Bottom line

U-47700 is a well-documented example of an old research molecule becoming a dangerous illicit-market opioid. Its history illustrates why unfamiliar opioid names, negative routine screens and counterfeit labels can all delay recognition of a genuine opioid emergency.
