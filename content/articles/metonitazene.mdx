# Metonitazene

> **Overdose warning:** Metonitazene is a potent opioid. If someone is unresponsive or breathing slowly or abnormally, call 911 and administer naloxone if available.

## Quick answer

**Metonitazene is a potent nitazene-class synthetic opioid with documented fatal human exposures.**

A U.S. forensic series confirmed it in 20 postmortem cases. Fentanyl frequently co-occurred, but metonitazene was the only opioid detected in a meaningful subset.

## U.S. postmortem evidence

In 20 confirmed cases, investigators documented metonitazene at very low blood concentrations relative to many traditional opioids.

Fentanyl appeared in more than half of the cases, while metonitazene was the sole identified opioid in 30%.

That does not define a universal lethal concentration, but it supports strong concern for intrinsic opioid toxicity.

## International fatal case

A Japanese postmortem report later documented fatal acute metonitazene poisoning, reinforcing that the risk is not geographically isolated.

## Detection

Nitazenes may be missed by routine opioid immunoassays. Modern forensic work relies on LC-MS/MS or high-resolution mass spectrometry and, increasingly, metabolite biomarkers.

## Bottom line

Metonitazene is a well-established modern nitazene threat: potent opioid pharmacology, fatal casework, low biological concentrations and real analytical-detection challenges.
