# Norflurazepam: Complete Active-Metabolite, Toxicology, Dependence & Safety Monograph

> **Emergency safety:** A person who cannot be awakened, has slow or irregular breathing, blue/gray lips, choking or gurgling while unresponsive, a seizure, collapse, or severe confusion after a benzodiazepine or unknown pill needs emergency care. Norflurazepam is a long-lived benzodiazepine-active compound; opioids, alcohol and other sedatives can greatly increase CNS and respiratory danger. Naloxone does not reverse benzodiazepines but should be given when opioid exposure is possible.

## Quick answer

**Norflurazepam is a long-lived 1,4-benzodiazepine also known as N-desalkylflurazepam, desalkylflurazepam and norfludiazepam.** It occupies an unusual position in the RC/NPS library because it can be both:

1. an **active metabolite** produced after other benzodiazepines; and
2. a substance sold directly in designer-drug markets.

That dual identity is the most important fact for interpreting norflurazepam evidence.

Human pharmacokinetic research from licensed benzodiazepines provides strong evidence that desalkylflurazepam can persist for days and accumulate with repeated exposure. But those studies largely measured the molecule after it was **formed from a parent medicine**, not after an anonymous direct norflurazepam RC product.

## Evidence at a glance

| Question | Best current evidence |

| Chemical identity | **Well established.** CAS 2886-65-9; multiple accepted synonyms. |
| Active metabolite of other benzodiazepines? | **Yes.** Strong human PK evidence. |

## Identity and aliases

**Canonical name:** Norflurazepam  
**CAS:** 2886-65-9  
**FDA UNII:** X9U41NXR6M  
**Common synonyms:** N-desalkylflurazepam, desalkylflurazepam, norfludiazepam  
**Class:** 1,4-benzodiazepine  
**Approved direct-use medicine:** not established

FDA's substance registry maps these names to the same chemical entity and also records related names arising from its role in the metabolism of other benzodiazepines.

## History: old metabolite, newer NPS

Norflurazepam is scientifically much older than its recent RC-market identity.

Human pharmacokinetic literature from the 1970s-1980s studied it as a major active metabolite of benzodiazepine hypnotics.

European NPS surveillance later identified the molecule itself in powders/tablets sold as a new psychoactive substance. EUDA's benzodiazepine review records direct NPS identification in Europe in 2016.

This creates an evidence asymmetry:
- **stronger evidence** for the molecule as a metabolite;
- **much weaker evidence** for direct unregulated use as a standalone RC.

## Relationship to flurazepam and other medicines

Norflurazepam is a major active metabolite associated with **flurazepam**.

It also appears in the metabolic pathways of several related benzodiazepines, including drugs such as flutoprazepam and quazepam-related compounds.

That means a norflurazepam result may have multiple possible origins.

A laboratory should not automatically translate:
> “norflurazepam detected”

into:
> “the person directly consumed an RC sold as norflurazepam.”

Parent compounds and metabolite patterns matter.

## Pharmacodynamics

Norflurazepam is pharmacologically active at benzodiazepine-sensitive **GABA-A receptors**.

Human and experimental work on flurazepam metabolites supports its contribution to prolonged benzodiazepine activity.

Expected benzodiazepine-type effects include:
- sedation;
- anxiolysis;
- psychomotor slowing;
- impaired coordination;
- memory impairment;
- anticonvulsant activity.

Direct modern receptor-subtype studies of standalone norflurazepam are limited compared with established prescription benzodiazepines.

## Pharmacokinetics: why this molecule is genuinely long lived

The long persistence of desalkylflurazepam is supported by multiple controlled human studies.

Published human research has reported:
- mean half-life around **71 hours** in one study;
- mean around **84 hours** with substantial individual variability in another;
- broader clinical-review ranges around **40-150 hours**;
- approximately **90 hours** after flutoprazepam in normal subjects.

Repeated flurazepam administration can lead to substantial accumulation of desalkylflurazepam.

### A crucial evidence boundary

These values are scientifically useful because the molecule in blood is norflurazepam/desalkylflurazepam.

But the route by which it appeared was usually **metabolism of another pharmaceutical benzodiazepine**.

That is not identical to directly ingesting an unregulated powder or tablet containing norflurazepam.

Direct-use product composition, absorption and exposure conditions can differ.

## Age and variability

Controlled flurazepam research included younger and older adults and demonstrated substantial between-person variability in desalkylflurazepam kinetics.

Long-lived benzodiazepine metabolites can be particularly important in older adults because accumulation may contribute to residual sedation, falls and cognitive impairment.

The precise direct-norflurazepam risk in older adults has not been separately quantified.

## In-vitro metabolism of directly sold norflurazepam

A 2019 study characterized norflurazepam sold through the research-chemical market and incubated it with pooled human liver microsomes.

Tentatively identified phase-I products included hydroxylated and dihydroxylated metabolites.

This is helpful for toxicology method development but is not a substitute for direct human pharmacokinetic trials of standalone norflurazepam.

## Human exposure surveillance

The U.S. National Poison Data System study of designer benzodiazepines from 2014-2017 included norflurazepam among tracked substances.

Across all queried designer benzodiazepines, 234 single-agent exposures were reported, with common effects including drowsiness/lethargy and slurred speech; some cases required intubation, hospital admission or intensive care.

Those aggregate outcomes demonstrate that designer benzodiazepines can cause clinically important toxicity.

They should **not** be misreported as norflurazepam-specific rates.

## Counterfeit-pill death investigations

A 2024 North Carolina postmortem series examined suspected counterfeit-pill deaths from 2020-2022.

Novel benzodiazepines detected included **desalkylflurazepam**, alongside drugs such as flualprazolam, etizolam, bromazolam and flubromazolam.

The study's design is important:
- the cases involved suspected counterfeit pills;
- multiple substances were common;
- detection identified exposure;
- detection did not automatically make desalkylflurazepam the sole cause of death.

This is exactly where the metabolite problem matters most: desalkylflurazepam can reflect a direct novel-benzodiazepine exposure, a parent benzodiazepine, or a more complex drug history.

## Acute toxicity

Expected benzodiazepine-type toxicity includes:
- somnolence;
- ataxia;
- slurred speech;
- impaired judgment;
- amnesia;
- confusion;
- reduced consciousness.

Because norflurazepam is long lived, prolonged impairment and accumulation are particularly plausible.

Direct standalone norflurazepam monointoxication cohorts are not available to define a molecule-specific severity distribution.

## Interactions and polysubstance risk

### Opioids
Opioids and benzodiazepine-active compounds can cause additive CNS and respiratory depression.

### Alcohol
Alcohol can amplify sedation, ataxia, disinhibition and amnesia.

### Other benzodiazepines
Parent benzodiazepines that generate norflurazepam can overlap with the metabolite itself, producing a layered exposure rather than a single-drug time course.

### Other sedatives
Z-drugs, gabapentinoids and other CNS depressants can add impairment.

Long persistence means an interaction may occur well after the initial exposure.

## Tolerance

Repeated benzodiazepine receptor exposure can produce tolerance.

Subjective tolerance to sedation does not guarantee equal tolerance to memory impairment, driving impairment or dangerous opioid interactions.

Norflurazepam-specific prospective tolerance studies are not available.

## Physical dependence

A long-lived active benzodiazepine can sustain receptor adaptation and physical dependence.

Physical dependence means withdrawal can occur when exposure falls.

It is not synonymous with addiction or a benzodiazepine use disorder.

## Withdrawal and delayed onset

Benzodiazepine withdrawal can include:
- severe anxiety;
- insomnia;
- tremor;
- autonomic activation;
- perceptual disturbance;
- confusion;
- seizures;
- delirium.

Because human data establish that desalkylflurazepam can decline very slowly, withdrawal may be **delayed** compared with shorter-lived benzodiazepines.

A person feeling well for a day or two after stopping does not prove they are not dependent.

The 2025 multisociety guideline advises against abrupt discontinuation when benzodiazepine physical dependence is likely.

There is no validated norflurazepam-specific self-taper schedule.

## Addiction / benzodiazepine use disorder

Signs of a broader use disorder can include:
- inability to cut down;
- compulsive use;
- repeated blackouts or injuries;
- hazardous driving;
- craving;
- continued use despite work, health or relationship harm;
- substantial time spent obtaining or recovering.

Physical dependence alone does not establish the diagnosis.

## Treatment and recovery support

Acute treatment is supportive and prioritizes:
- airway and breathing;
- mental status;
- trauma;
- co-exposures;
- prolonged observation when clinically indicated.

Flumazenil can reverse benzodiazepine effects in selected medical situations, but it can precipitate seizures or withdrawal and is not a universal antidote.

When dependence is likely, medically supervised assessment is safer than abrupt cessation or a self-calculated “long benzo” conversion.

## Drug testing and detection

Definitive identification can use chromatographic mass spectrometry.

But **finding the molecule is only the first interpretive step**.

A useful panel may need to evaluate:
- flurazepam;
- hydroxyethylflurazepam;
- norflurazepam/desalkylflurazepam;
- other related parent benzodiazepines;
- direct NPS candidates;
- co-intoxicants.

Routine immunoassays may indicate a benzodiazepine class exposure without identifying the source molecule.

## The parent-metabolite attribution problem

Norflurazepam is one of the clearest examples of why toxicology is not simply “name a molecule, name the drug taken.”

If norflurazepam is present, possibilities include:
1. direct norflurazepam exposure;
2. metabolism of flurazepam;
3. metabolism of another related benzodiazepine;
4. multiple overlapping exposures.

The correct interpretation depends on the rest of the toxicology profile and case history.

## Counterfeit and mislabeled product risk

Counterfeit pills can contain unexpected benzodiazepines, fentanyl or other substances.

The North Carolina death series demonstrates that desalkylflurazepam can appear in this market context.

A tablet imprint or vendor label cannot establish:
- which parent drug was present;
- whether norflurazepam was directly added;
- concentration;
- absence of opioids.

## Forensic interpretation

Detection proves the molecule was present.

It does **not** automatically prove:
- direct ingestion of norflurazepam;
- the amount taken;
- degree of impairment;
- sole causation of death.

Interpretation should incorporate:
- parent compounds;
- metabolite ratios/patterns where validated;
- specimen timing;
- co-drugs;
- tolerance;
- scene evidence;
- analytical panel limitations.

## Epidemiology and current trend

Norflurazepam was identified as an NPS in Europe in 2016 and has continued to appear in designer-benzodiazepine surveillance.

NDEWS online-monitoring work also flagged norflurazepam as a web-surveillance topic in late 2025, indicating renewed discussion/market attention.

Online discussion volume is a discovery signal, not evidence of prevalence, purity or clinical severity.

## Legal and regulatory context

International and national status is fragmented.

EU/UNODC technical sources identify norflurazepam as a benzodiazepine NPS, while individual countries regulate it differently. Belgium, for example, includes norfludiazepam/norflurazepam in its controlled-substance annex as of a March 2026 update.

For the United States, this October 3, 2026 review did not identify a specific DEA final scheduling rule naming norflurazepam in the federal materials reviewed. That does not guarantee legality; state law and analogue provisions can differ.

This review also did not identify a specific current CND scheduling decision naming norflurazepam in the international-control materials reviewed. Jurisdiction-specific primary law should be checked for legal questions.

## Special populations

Direct standalone norflurazepam evidence is sparse in:
- pregnancy/breastfeeding;
- adolescents;
- liver/kidney disease;
- respiratory disease.

Older-adult concerns are supported indirectly by the extensive human pharmacokinetic literature on desalkylflurazepam formed from flurazepam, where slow elimination and accumulation are clinically relevant.

## Myths versus evidence

### “Norflurazepam detected means the person took norflurazepam.”
**False.** It is also an active metabolite of multiple benzodiazepines.

### “The 70-90 hour half-life proves every direct RC exposure lasts exactly that long.”
**False.** Those estimates largely come from norflurazepam formed from pharmaceutical parent drugs and show substantial variability.

### “Long acting means safer.”
**False.** Long persistence can increase accumulation, delayed interactions and residual impairment.

### “No symptoms the next day means there is no dependence.”
**False.** Slow elimination can delay withdrawal.

### “A counterfeit pill marked like a prescription drug contains that prescription drug.”
**False.** Postmortem and drug-checking evidence repeatedly show unexpected benzodiazepines and opioids.

## Evidence ledger

| Claim | Evidence strength | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Norflurazepam and desalkylflurazepam are the same molecule | **High** | Identity well established |
| It is an active metabolite of flurazepam and related drugs | **High** | Strong human PK literature |
| It has multi-day human elimination | **High for metabolite exposure** | Most data arise after parent-drug administration |
| Direct standalone norflurazepam PK is known | **Not established** | No robust direct-use human PK trials |
| It appears in U.S. poison-center/counterfeit-pill surveillance | **Moderate** | Attribution and analytic confirmation vary by dataset |

| Dependence and delayed withdrawal are plausible | **High by class and persistence; low direct-RC specificity** | No prospective standalone cohort |

## Major evidence gaps

Important gaps include:
- direct controlled administration pharmacokinetics of norflurazepam itself;
- standalone intoxication cohorts;
- impaired-driving concentration-response data;
- direct-use dependence incidence;
- withdrawal timing after direct NPS exposure;
- current counterfeit-pill prevalence;
- parent/metabolite attribution methods in mixed exposures;
- pregnancy and organ-impairment data.

For U.S. treatment referrals, [SAMHSA’s National Helpline](https://www.samhsa.gov/find-help/helplines/national-helpline), **1-800-662-HELP (4357)**, is available 24 hours a day. [FindTreatment.gov](https://findtreatment.gov/) lists treatment services. Referral services do not replace emergency care.

## Bottom line

Norflurazepam is one of the most analytically important “double identity” drugs in the RC/NPS cluster.

**As a molecule, its long human persistence is well supported. As a directly consumed RC, the clinical evidence is much thinner.**

That distinction changes everything from half-life claims to death-certificate interpretation. A norflurazepam result can reflect an active metabolite, direct use, or both.

The reference-grade conclusion is therefore: **long-lived benzodiazepine biology is real; direct-RC outcome precision is not; and parent-metabolite attribution is essential before turning a toxicology result into a story about what someone took.**
