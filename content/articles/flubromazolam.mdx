# Flubromazolam: Prolonged Intoxication, Coma, Pharmacokinetics & Withdrawal Risk

> **Safety note:** Flubromazolam can produce prolonged severe CNS depression. Combining it with opioids, alcohol, or other sedatives increases the risk of coma, airway compromise, and fatal overdose.

## Quick answer

**Flubromazolam is a highly potent designer triazolobenzodiazepine** with published cases of prolonged, severe intoxication.

Unlike many RC benzos that are known mainly from seized samples, flubromazolam has human case reports and preliminary pharmacokinetic data.

The clinical signal is concerning: deep sedation can last long enough that repeated dosing or co-use becomes especially difficult to judge.

## Severe intoxication case

A widely cited clinical report described a 27-year-old with:

- deep coma;
- unreactive pinpoint pupils;
- acute respiratory failure;
- hypotension;
- severe creatine kinase elevation/rhabdomyolysis.

The patient required mechanical ventilation and intensive supportive treatment.

The report established that flubromazolam exposure can produce **life-threatening, prolonged intoxication**.

## Why duration matters

A long-acting sedative creates risks beyond the first period of subjective intoxication.

Someone may:

- think the drug has “worn off” before meaningful blood concentrations have cleared;
- take another dose;
- add alcohol or another depressant;
- wake still impaired;
- accumulate exposure across consecutive days.

This is especially difficult in unregulated solutions where the labeled concentration may not be independently verified.

## Pharmacokinetic evidence

A 2018 study provided preliminary human pharmacokinetic data and described flubromazolam as a **highly potent designer benzodiazepine**.

The evidence base remains small, so exact half-life estimates should not be treated as a universal timer for recreational products.

Metabolism can vary with individual physiology, liver enzyme activity, repeated exposure, and co-administered drugs.

## Bradycardia and prolonged CNS depression

Another clinical case and review documented prolonged bradycardia after flubromazolam use.

This reinforces a broader point: designer benzodiazepine toxicity can involve more than sleepiness.

Severe intoxication can require:

- airway support;
- respiratory monitoring;
- cardiovascular monitoring;
- treatment of complications from prolonged unconsciousness.

## Dependence and withdrawal

Flubromazolam can produce benzodiazepine-type dependence.

A long duration does not protect against withdrawal. It can instead delay the onset, making it harder to connect symptoms to the last exposure.

Severe benzodiazepine withdrawal can include seizures and delirium.

## Flumazenil is not a DIY antidote

Published case literature sometimes discusses flumazenil, a benzodiazepine receptor antagonist.

That does **not** make flumazenil a general at-home overdose treatment.

In people who are benzodiazepine-dependent or who have mixed overdoses, flumazenil can precipitate seizures and other complications. Its use is a medical decision based on the exposure context.

## U.S. legal status

DEA permanently placed flubromazolam in Schedule I effective **April 1, 2026**.

## What Reddit reports add

Flubromazolam has long been described online as unusually hypnotic and amnestic.

A peer-reviewed study that analyzed online trip reports also found that users frequently characterized flubromazolam as strongly hypnotic and amnestic.

That is useful corroboration of a safety signal, but forum reports still cannot establish safe dose equivalence.

## Bottom line

Flubromazolam is one of the designer benzodiazepines where the phrase “research chemical” understates how much real-world harm has already been documented.

Published cases show severe prolonged CNS depression, including coma and respiratory failure, and pharmacokinetic work supports concern about extended impairment.
