# Meclonazepam: Complete Evidence, Metabolism, Toxicology, Dependence & Safety Monograph

> **Emergency safety:** Extreme sedation, inability to wake, slow or irregular breathing, blue/gray lips, seizure, collapse or severe confusion after meclonazepam or an unknown sedative requires emergency care. Alcohol, opioids and other CNS depressants can substantially increase risk. Naloxone does not reverse a benzodiazepine, but should be given when opioid exposure is possible.

## Quick answer

**Meclonazepam is a 3-methyl derivative of clonazepam with an unusual history: it was investigated as an antiparasitic lead for schistosomiasis before CNS effects limited its usefulness, then later reappeared as a designer benzodiazepine/NPS.**

Modern human evidence is dominated by **analytical toxicology and intoxication surveillance**, not controlled therapeutic trials. Human urine studies have characterized important metabolites, and Sweden's STRIDA program analytically confirmed meclonazepam in acute intoxication cases.

The evidence is strong enough to establish real human exposure and benzodiazepine-type risk, but not strong enough to justify a clonazepam conversion, a precise human half-life, a safe recreational dose or a molecule-specific withdrawal schedule.

## Identity

**Canonical name:** Meclonazepam  
**Alias:** 3-methyl clonazepam  
**CAS:** 58662-84-3  
**Molecular formula:** C16H12ClN3O3  
**Class:** 1,4-benzodiazepine / designer benzodiazepine  
**Relationship:** 3-methyl derivative of clonazepam  
**Approved U.S. medical use:** none established

UNODC classifies meclonazepam among benzodiazepine NPS with sedative/hypnotic effects.

## History: antiparasitic lead to NPS market

Meclonazepam is unusual among RC benzodiazepines because it was not simply synthesized for an internet market.

Historical research investigated meclonazepam for **schistosomicidal activity**. A 2012 medicinal-chemistry paper notes that it had demonstrated antiparasitic activity in humans, but adverse CNS effects prevented its clinical use for that purpose.

It later resurfaced in the NPS market and was reported to UNODC's Early Warning Advisory. Finland and several other jurisdictions subsequently placed it under national control as designer-benzodiazepine markets expanded.

## Chemistry and relationship to clonazepam

Meclonazepam differs from clonazepam by a methyl substituent at the 3-position. It remains a nitrobenzodiazepine and shares important structural features with clonazepam.

That relationship supports a benzodiazepine-class mechanism but **does not validate milligram equivalence**. Small chemical changes can alter receptor affinity, metabolism, duration and active-metabolite exposure.

## Pharmacodynamics

Direct modern receptor profiling is limited relative to established prescription benzodiazepines.

The clinically relevant framework is **benzodiazepine-site positive allosteric modulation of GABA-A receptors**, producing class effects such as:
- sedation;
- anxiolysis;
- psychomotor impairment;
- memory impairment;
- muscle relaxation;
- anticonvulsant activity.

Historical antiparasitic work is important because it confirms that CNS effects were substantial enough to limit drug development. It does not provide a safe contemporary use range.

## Metabolism and pharmacokinetics

Human urine and high-resolution mass-spectrometry studies provide the strongest direct meclonazepam pharmacokinetic evidence.

Important metabolites include:
- **amino-meclonazepam**, formed through reduction of the nitro group;
- **acetamido-meclonazepam**, a downstream product useful in urinary identification;
- additional metabolites characterized through HRMS workflows.

These findings matter because the parent compound may not be the most persistent or sensitive marker in every specimen.

What remains poorly established:
- population half-life;
- concentration-effect relationship;
- accumulation during repeated nonmedical use;
- clinically validated duration of impairment.

## Human intoxication evidence

STRIDA surveillance in Sweden documented meclonazepam among analytically confirmed NPS-benzodiazepine intoxications.

Across the broader designer-benzodiazepine cohort:
- CNS depression was the principal clinical pattern;
- polysubstance exposure was common;
- some patients required high-acuity observation.

Those data establish that designer-benzodiazepine intoxication can be clinically serious. They do **not** allow every symptom or severity measure to be attributed to meclonazepam alone.

## Acute toxicity

Expected benzodiazepine-type toxicity includes:
- marked drowsiness;
- slowed speech;
- poor coordination;
- amnesia;
- confusion;
- disinhibition;
- reduced level of consciousness.

Direct meclonazepam monointoxication data are too sparse to define a toxic concentration or fatal threshold.

## Interactions and polysubstance risk

### Opioids
Combining an opioid with a benzodiazepine-type sedative can deepen sedation and respiratory depression. This is especially important when an unregulated product's composition is uncertain.

### Alcohol
Alcohol can amplify sedation, poor coordination, amnesia and injury risk.

### Other sedatives
Other benzodiazepines, Z-drugs, gabapentinoids and sedating medications can produce additive CNS depression.

There is no scientifically defensible “safe combination” table for an unapproved designer benzodiazepine.

## Tolerance and physical dependence

Repeated benzodiazepine-type exposure can produce tolerance and physical dependence.

Direct prospective meclonazepam studies are lacking, so statements about dependence should be labeled **class-supported**, not molecule-specific clinical proof.

Physical dependence is not identical to addiction. It describes neuroadaptation and withdrawal risk; a use disorder includes impaired control and continued use despite harm.

## Withdrawal

There is no validated meclonazepam-specific withdrawal timeline.

Benzodiazepine withdrawal can include:
- rebound anxiety;
- insomnia;
- tremor;
- autonomic symptoms;
- agitation;
- perceptual disturbance;
- confusion;
- seizures;
- delirium.

The 2025 multisociety benzodiazepine-tapering guideline recommends avoiding abrupt discontinuation in patients likely to be physically dependent.

This page intentionally does not provide a DIY conversion to clonazepam or another benzodiazepine.

## Addiction and recovery support

Warning signs of a benzodiazepine/sedative use disorder include:
- repeated inability to reduce use;
- craving;
- hazardous use;
- recurrent blackouts or injuries;
- use despite work or relationship harm;
- escalating time spent obtaining, using or recovering.

Treatment should address both the sedative exposure and the underlying problem it may have been used to manage, such as anxiety, insomnia or another substance-use pattern.

U.S. readers can use **FindTreatment.gov** and SAMHSA treatment resources. Seizure, delirium, profound sedation or abnormal breathing requires emergency care.

## Testing and detection

Routine benzodiazepine immunoassays are not comprehensive designer-benzodiazepine screens.

Definitive meclonazepam testing has relied on:
- LC-MS/MS;
- LC-HRMS;
- parent drug plus amino- and acetamido-metabolite targets.

A negative routine screen therefore does not reliably rule out exposure.

## Counterfeit and mislabeled products

Powder labels, vendor descriptions and tablet appearance are not chemical confirmation.

Potential problems include:
- wrong benzodiazepine;
- multiple benzodiazepines;
- unexpected opioids or other depressants;
- concentration variability;
- incomplete certificates of analysis.

Product identity should be treated as an analytical question rather than an assumption.

## Forensic interpretation

A meclonazepam-positive result establishes exposure. It does not automatically establish:
- the consumed amount;
- exact timing;
- degree of impairment;
- sole causation in a death;
- absence of other active substances.

Metabolites can improve exposure detection, but toxicology still requires clinical and investigative context.

## Epidemiology and current trends

Meclonazepam is tracked by UNODC as a benzodiazepine NPS but appears less prominent in recent North American forensic literature than bromazolam.

Low detection frequency should not be interpreted as evidence that the molecule is intrinsically safer. It primarily limits the precision of risk estimates.

## Legal and regulatory context

UNODC records meclonazepam in its Early Warning Advisory and documented national controls imposed by several countries during the designer-benzodiazepine expansion.

Legal status is jurisdiction-specific. This review does not treat an online vendor's availability or one country's schedule as evidence of legality elsewhere.

Readers needing a legal answer should verify the current primary law for their location.

## Special populations

Direct meclonazepam evidence is essentially absent for:
- pregnancy and breastfeeding;
- adolescents;
- older adults;
- liver/kidney impairment;
- respiratory disease.

Class-level benzodiazepine concerns remain relevant, particularly with opioids, respiratory vulnerability, fall risk and prior withdrawal seizures.

## Myths versus evidence

### “It is clonazepam with one methyl group, so the dose is basically the same.”
**Unsupported.** Structural similarity is not clinical equivalence.

### “The old antiparasitic research proves it is medically safe.”
**False.** CNS adverse effects were one reason its antiparasitic development was limited.

### “A negative benzodiazepine screen rules it out.”
**False.** Designer-benzodiazepine detection depends on assay cross-reactivity and panel design.

### “Dependence means addiction.”
**False.** Physical dependence and substance use disorder are related but distinct.

### “No published withdrawal case means withdrawal is safe.”
**False.** Sparse molecule-specific data do not erase established benzodiazepine-class withdrawal risk.

## Major evidence gaps

Important unanswered questions include:
- controlled human receptor/PK studies;
- half-life and active-metabolite kinetics;
- monointoxication case series;
- dose/concentration-response;
- prospective dependence and withdrawal data;
- pregnancy and organ-impairment outcomes;
- contemporary prevalence and product composition.

## Bottom line

Meclonazepam has a deeper scientific history than its status as an RC might suggest, but its **modern human safety evidence remains sparse**.

The evidence clearly supports real human exposure, characteristic benzodiazepine metabolism and a benzodiazepine-type risk framework. It does **not** support precise clonazepam equivalence, a recreational dose guide, a toxic concentration or a unique withdrawal calendar.

For harm reduction, the correct stance is to preserve the strong class warnings—**sedation, amnesia, polysubstance respiratory risk, physical dependence and dangerous withdrawal**—while being explicit about where molecule-specific evidence stops.
