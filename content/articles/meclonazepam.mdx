# Meclonazepam: Complete Evidence, Metabolism, Intoxication, Dependence & Safety Monograph

> **Emergency safety:** Extreme drowsiness, inability to wake, abnormal breathing, blue/gray lips, choking or gurgling while unresponsive, seizure, collapse, or severe confusion after an unknown pill or powder requires emergency assessment. Meclonazepam is a benzodiazepine-type sedative; alcohol, opioids and other depressants can add to sedation and respiratory danger. Naloxone does not reverse benzodiazepines but should be used when opioid exposure is possible.

## Quick answer

**Meclonazepam is a clonazepam-related nitrobenzodiazepine, commonly described as (S)-3-methylclonazepam.** It was discovered during pharmaceutical research into antischistosomal drugs and later re-emerged in designer-benzodiazepine markets.

The modern evidence base is dominated by analytical toxicology. Human urine studies identify major metabolites useful for proving exposure, and Swedish STRIDA surveillance confirmed meclonazepam in acute intoxication cases.

The compound's unusual antiparasitic history can make it sound pharmacologically exotic, but the central human safety issue is familiar: **benzodiazepine-type CNS depression, amnesia/impairment, interaction with other depressants, physical dependence and potentially dangerous withdrawal**.

## Identity and stereochemistry

**Canonical name:** Meclonazepam  
**Common description:** (S)-3-methylclonazepam  
**CAS:** 58662-84-3  
**Molecular formula:** C16H12ClN3O3  
**Class:** nitro-substituted 1,4-benzodiazepine

The stereochemistry matters. “Meclonazepam” ordinarily refers to the **S-isomer**, while racemic or R-isomer records may have different identifiers. This is a useful reminder that similar names in vendor catalogs do not always identify exactly the same chemical entity.

## History: an antiparasitic lead before an RC benzodiazepine

Meclonazepam was discovered by Roche in the 1970s during research on treatments for schistosomiasis.

Its history is unusual because the molecule had **two pharmacologic identities**:
- antiparasitic activity against schistosome worms;
- benzodiazepine-like sedative effects in the human host.

Sedation limited its therapeutic development as an antiparasitic. Modern 2024 medicinal-chemistry research revisited the scaffold specifically to design derivatives that could retain antischistosomal activity without strong human GABA-A sedation.

Years after its pharmaceutical research, meclonazepam appeared in the novel-psychoactive-substance market.

## Chemistry and relationship to clonazepam

Meclonazepam is closely related to clonazepam but includes a methyl substitution at the 3-position.

That relationship is informative for:
- expected benzodiazepine-site activity;
- nitrobenzodiazepine metabolism;
- likely CNS-depressant effects.

It does **not** establish:
- a clonazepam-equivalent dose;
- identical duration;
- identical receptor-subtype activity;
- identical withdrawal timing.

## Pharmacodynamics

The historical sedation seen during meclonazepam development and its close nitrobenzodiazepine structure support benzodiazepine-site modulation of **GABA-A receptors**.

Benzodiazepine-site positive allosteric modulation can produce:
- sedation;
- anxiolysis;
- psychomotor slowing;
- impaired coordination;
- amnesia;
- anticonvulsant effects;
- muscle relaxation.

The literature is not strong enough to publish a modern receptor-subtype potency profile as if it were equivalent to controlled human pharmacology.

## Metabolism: one of the strongest parts of the evidence base

Meclonazepam has been studied using:
- authentic human urine;
- human liver microsomes;
- hepatocytes;
- animal models;
- high-resolution mass spectrometry.

The most important human urinary markers include:
- **amino-meclonazepam**;
- **acetamido-meclonazepam**.

A separate human urine study of nitrobenzodiazepines found meclonazepam to be extensively metabolized and identified **7-acetaminomeclonazepam** as an especially useful urine target.

This pattern resembles other nitrobenzodiazepines, where reduction of the nitro group and subsequent acetylation can dominate urinary detection.

## Pharmacokinetic limits

Despite good metabolite identification, major human PK questions remain unresolved.

There is no robust modern dataset defining:
- population half-life;
- time to peak concentration;
- concentration-effect relationships;
- accumulation with repeated exposure;
- effects of liver disease;
- interaction magnitude with CYP inhibitors/inducers.

Analytical detectability should not be confused with a validated clinical time course.

## Human intoxication evidence

The Swedish STRIDA project found meclonazepam in **26 analytically confirmed acute intoxication cases** from its 2012-2016 surveillance period.

Across the full designer-benzodiazepine cohort:
- polysubstance exposure was very common;
- CNS depression was the dominant syndrome in mono-NPS-benzodiazepine cases;
- some patients required intensive-care observation.

Those data establish that meclonazepam reached real patients and belongs in clinical toxicology. They do not establish a meclonazepam-only toxic dose or fatal concentration.

## Acute toxicity

Expected benzodiazepine-type toxicity includes:
- somnolence;
- slurred speech;
- ataxia;
- impaired judgment;
- amnesia;
- confusion;
- reduced consciousness.

Severe respiratory depression is most concerning when other depressants are present.

Because direct meclonazepam monointoxication data are sparse, the page deliberately separates **class-supported hazards** from **molecule-specific measured incidence**.

## Interactions

### Opioids
Opioids plus benzodiazepine-type sedatives can produce profound sedation and respiratory depression.

### Alcohol
Alcohol can amplify amnesia, psychomotor impairment and CNS depression.

### Other benzodiazepines and sedatives
Co-use with other benzodiazepines, Z-drugs, gabapentinoids, barbiturates or sedating medications can compound impairment.

No reliable interaction equation exists for an unregulated designer-benzodiazepine product.

## Tolerance and physical dependence

Repeated benzodiazepine exposure can produce tolerance and physical dependence.

Direct prospective meclonazepam studies are lacking, so claims about exact dependence frequency or speed would be speculative.

Physical dependence means withdrawal can occur after exposure falls. It does not automatically mean a person has a benzodiazepine use disorder.

## Withdrawal

A meclonazepam-specific withdrawal timeline has **not** been established.

Benzodiazepine-class withdrawal can include:
- severe anxiety;
- insomnia;
- tremor;
- agitation;
- perceptual disturbance;
- autonomic symptoms;
- confusion;
- seizures;
- delirium.

The 2025 multisociety guideline advises clinicians not to abruptly discontinue benzodiazepines in patients likely to be physically dependent.

That safety principle is relevant to meclonazepam even though no guideline has validated a molecule-specific conversion or taper protocol.

## Addiction / benzodiazepine use disorder

A substance use disorder involves more than tolerance or withdrawal.

Warning signs can include:
- inability to control use;
- repeated blackouts;
- craving;
- hazardous use;
- continued use despite work, relationship or health harm;
- substantial time spent obtaining or recovering from use.

Someone can be physically dependent without meeting criteria for a use disorder, and someone with a use disorder may also have substantial physical dependence.

## Treatment and support

Acute care is primarily supportive and focuses on:
- airway and breathing;
- mental status;
- injuries;
- co-exposures;
- prolonged sedation.

Flumazenil is a medical tool for selected cases, not a universal antidote, because it can precipitate seizures or withdrawal in susceptible patients.

When regular exposure suggests dependence, medically supervised assessment is safer than an improvised abrupt stop or internet conversion schedule.

U.S. treatment resources include **FindTreatment.gov** and SAMHSA substance-use services.

## Drug testing and detection

Routine benzodiazepine immunoassays may not reliably identify every designer benzodiazepine.

Definitive detection can rely on:
- LC-HRMS;
- LC-MS/MS;
- high-resolution metabolite libraries;
- urinary amino/acetamido metabolites.

A negative screen does not necessarily exclude meclonazepam if the assay or confirmation panel does not include the relevant compound/metabolites.

## Counterfeit and product identity

Research-chemical labels are not analytical confirmation.

Potential problems include:
- wrong compound;
- stereochemical ambiguity;
- uneven tablet content;
- mixtures of multiple sedatives;
- opioid contamination or co-formulation;
- changing batches under the same product name.

Testing one batch cannot certify every future batch.

## Forensic interpretation

Meclonazepam detection establishes exposure, not automatically causation.

Interpretation should consider:
- other detected drugs;
- specimen type;
- collection timing;
- parent-to-metabolite pattern;
- tolerance;
- clinical findings;
- whether the analytical method can distinguish relevant compounds.

Urinary metabolite detection is particularly useful for confirming ingestion but is poor evidence for reconstructing exact impairment at a prior moment.

## Epidemiology and current trend

STRIDA's 26 meclonazepam cases establish meaningful presence in a defined Swedish intoxication population during 2012-2016.

That number should not be generalized into current global prevalence. Modern public-health attention has shifted toward other designer benzodiazepines, but lower surveillance frequency does not make meclonazepam pharmacologically benign.

## Legal and regulatory status

As of this October 3, 2026 review, no specific U.S. federal scheduling order naming meclonazepam was identified in the primary federal materials reviewed.

That is **not** a guarantee of legality:
- state schedules can differ;
- analogue statutes may be relevant depending on facts and jurisdiction;
- laws change;
- possession/distribution laws may depend on intended use and other circumstances.

This page therefore avoids a blanket “legal” or “illegal” label.

## Special populations

Direct meclonazepam evidence is absent for:
- pregnancy and breastfeeding;
- adolescents outside toxicology casework;
- older adults;
- liver or kidney impairment;
- chronic respiratory disease.

Benzodiazepine-class concerns remain relevant, especially falls/cognitive impairment in older adults and respiratory risk with opioids or pulmonary disease.

## Myths versus evidence

### “Meclonazepam is just clonazepam with a known conversion.”
**Unsupported.** Close structure does not create validated clinical equivalence.

### “Its antiparasitic history means it is medically proven safe.”
**False.** Sedation limited that development, and modern RC use is not equivalent to regulated clinical research.

### “A negative routine benzo screen rules it out.”
**False.** Detection depends on assay cross-reactivity and metabolite coverage.

### “Low current popularity means low risk.”
**False.** Prevalence and pharmacologic hazard are different questions.

### “Dependence means addiction.”
**False.** Physical dependence and substance use disorder are distinct concepts.

## Major evidence gaps

Priority research gaps include:
- modern human receptor/pharmacodynamic studies;
- population pharmacokinetics;
- monointoxication cohorts;
- impaired-driving concentration data;
- fatality series with causal adjudication;
- prospective dependence/withdrawal characterization;
- pregnancy and organ-dysfunction data;
- contemporary market prevalence.

## Bottom line

Meclonazepam has enough human evidence to deserve a dedicated monograph: it has a real pharmaceutical research history, human metabolite data and analytically confirmed intoxication cases.

But the evidence does **not** justify the precision often attached to RC-benzodiazepine discussions. There is no validated clonazepam conversion, no established safe recreational dose, no reliable toxic threshold and no meclonazepam-specific withdrawal schedule.

The strongest evidence-based frame is **benzodiazepine-class safety + direct analytical evidence + explicit uncertainty wherever molecule-specific human data stop**.
