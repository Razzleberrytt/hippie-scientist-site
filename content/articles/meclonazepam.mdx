# Meclonazepam: Complete Evidence, Toxicology, Dependence, Withdrawal & Safety Monograph

> **Emergency safety:** Profound sedation, inability to wake, abnormal breathing, blue/gray lips, collapse, seizure, aspiration or severe confusion after meclonazepam or an unknown sedative requires emergency care. **Naloxone does not reverse meclonazepam**, but should still be used when opioid exposure is possible.

## Quick answer

**Meclonazepam is a clonazepam-related 1,4-benzodiazepine, often described as 3-methylclonazepam.** It has an unusual history: the molecule was investigated for activity against schistosome parasites, but benzodiazepine CNS effects limited its usefulness as an antiparasitic drug. It later reappeared in designer-benzodiazepine markets.

The best modern evidence is not a clinical efficacy program. It consists mainly of **human metabolism studies, analytical toxicology and acute-intoxication surveillance**.

Human urine work identifies amino- and acetamido-meclonazepam metabolites as useful markers of exposure. Swedish STRIDA surveillance also detected meclonazepam in acute intoxication cases.

The major evidence limit is just as important: there is no high-quality modern clinical program establishing a safe recreational dose, a clonazepam conversion, a dependable half-life, a toxic concentration threshold, or a meclonazepam-specific withdrawal timeline.

## Identity

**Canonical name:** Meclonazepam  
**Alias:** 3-methylclonazepam; Ro 11-3128  
**CAS:** 58662-84-3 for the named S-isomer in PubChem/WHO identity records  
**Class:** 1,4-benzodiazepine / nitrobenzodiazepine  
**Molecular formula:** C16H12ClN3O3  
**Structural relationship:** 3-methyl derivative of clonazepam  
**Approved U.S. medical use:** none established  
**Evidence grade:** low

UNODC classifies meclonazepam within the benzodiazepine/sedative-hypnotic NPS group.

### Meclonazepam is not clonazepam with a known multiplier

The structural relationship helps explain why benzodiazepine-like CNS effects are expected. It does not establish a clinically validated dose conversion, equal duration or equal therapeutic index.

---

## History: antiparasitic lead to designer benzodiazepine

Meclonazepam was developed during medicinal-chemistry work on benzodiazepines and was investigated for **schistosomicidal activity**.

Later research revisited clonazepam, meclonazepam and related 1,4-benzodiazepines as antiparasitic leads. The 2012 medicinal-chemistry study noted prior human schistosomicidal evidence but also the CNS adverse effects that prevented practical clinical adoption for that purpose.

Years later, meclonazepam appeared in the NPS/designer-benzodiazepine market and in toxicology surveillance.

This history is a useful warning against a common misconception: **a molecule having legitimate research history does not mean an internet product has pharmaceutical quality, validated dosing or a modern safety program.**

---

## Chemistry and relationship to clonazepam

Meclonazepam is a **nitrobenzodiazepine** closely related to clonazepam.

The nitro group is important analytically because metabolic reduction produces amino metabolites. Human studies found **amino-meclonazepam and acetamido-meclonazepam** among the major urinary products.

That chemistry is highly useful for toxicology. It still does not provide a human potency conversion.

---

## Pharmacodynamics

### GABA-A benzodiazepine-site mechanism

As a benzodiazepine-type drug, meclonazepam is expected to enhance inhibitory GABA signaling through benzodiazepine-sensitive **GABA-A receptors**.

Class-consistent effects include:

- sedation;
- anxiolysis;
- impaired coordination;
- slowed reaction time;
- anterograde amnesia;
- muscle relaxation;
- impaired judgment;
- reduced consciousness at high exposure.

Direct modern receptor/subunit studies specific to meclonazepam are much thinner than the evidence for established prescription benzodiazepines. That distinction should remain visible.

### What antiparasitic activity does not tell us

Schistosomicidal activity is a separate biological property. It does not prove that meclonazepam is safe or useful as a sedative, and it does not validate nonmedical dosing.

---

## Pharmacokinetics and metabolism

Meclonazepam has one of the better analytical-metabolism records among otherwise sparsely studied designer benzodiazepines.

### Human urinary metabolites

Studies using authentic human urine identified:

- **amino-meclonazepam**;
- **acetamido-meclonazepam**;
- smaller amounts of parent meclonazepam in some samples.

The 2016 human urine study found extensive metabolism and highlighted **7-acetamidomeclonazepam** as a useful urine target.

The 2017 AAPS Journal study compared human urine with human liver microsomes, hepatocytes and an animal model, strengthening confidence in the proposed metabolic pathways.

### What is still missing

The literature does not provide a robust modern clinical pharmacokinetic program defining:

- a reliable population half-life;
- concentration-effect thresholds;
- therapeutic windows;
- interaction magnitude with specific CYP inhibitors/inducers;
- a validated relationship between urine metabolite abundance and impairment.

Analytical metabolism evidence is not the same thing as clinical PK certainty.

---

## Human effects and acute intoxication

### STRIDA surveillance

The Swedish STRIDA project detected **26 meclonazepam cases** in its NPS-benzodiazepine intoxication dataset.

Across the broader cohort, **89% involved other drugs**, and CNS depression was the dominant clinical pattern. Some patients with NPS-benzodiazepine exposure required ICU observation/support.

This supports clinically meaningful intoxication risk, but the high prevalence of co-exposures prevents a clean meclonazepam-only dose-response curve.

### What is directly established

Human evidence establishes:

- real exposure;
- detectability in acute intoxication cases;
- benzodiazepine-type CNS depression in the broader NPS-benzodiazepine clinical context;
- human metabolism and urinary markers.

It does **not** establish a precise isolated-toxicity threshold.

---

## Acute toxicity and overdose

Expected severe benzodiazepine-type toxicity includes:

- profound sedation;
- poor coordination;
- slurred speech;
- amnesia;
- confusion;
- reduced consciousness;
- falls/injury;
- aspiration;
- respiratory compromise when other depressants are involved.

### Why co-drugs matter

The majority of NPS-benzodiazepine intoxication cases in STRIDA involved more than one drug.

That means any attempt to label a single blood/urine level as “toxic” without context can be misleading.

---

## Interactions and polysubstance risk

### Opioids

Opioids plus benzodiazepine-type sedatives can produce a dangerous combination of respiratory depression and reduced arousal.

Naloxone treats the opioid component only.

### Alcohol

Alcohol can amplify sedation, amnesia, disinhibition, falls and aspiration risk.

### Other sedatives

Other benzodiazepines, Z-drugs, gabapentinoids and sedating medications can add to impairment.

### Unknown gray-market mixtures

An RC-labelled product may contain a different benzodiazepine, multiple sedatives or an opioid. Product identity uncertainty is therefore part of the interaction risk itself.

---

## Tolerance

No prospective meclonazepam tolerance studies define how quickly tolerance develops.

Benzodiazepine-class evidence supports neuroadaptation with repeated exposure, but tolerance can be uneven. A person may feel less sedated while remaining impaired and physically dependent.

---

## Physical dependence

Repeated meclonazepam exposure can plausibly produce **physical dependence** based on benzodiazepine-class pharmacology.

Direct meclonazepam dependence incidence is not known.

Physical dependence means withdrawal can occur after reduction or cessation. It is not identical to addiction.

---

## Withdrawal

There is no prospective meclonazepam withdrawal cohort and no validated meclonazepam taper protocol.

Benzodiazepine withdrawal can include:

- severe anxiety;
- insomnia;
- tremor;
- autonomic symptoms;
- perceptual disturbance;
- agitation;
- confusion;
- seizures;
- delirium.

The 2025 multisociety benzodiazepine guideline advises against abrupt cessation in people likely to be physically dependent.

### What cannot be responsibly claimed

The evidence does not justify:

- a fixed meclonazepam withdrawal start time;
- a universal peak day;
- a clonazepam conversion ratio;
- a DIY taper percentage;
- a promise that a particular duration of use cannot cause dependence.

---

## Dependence vs benzodiazepine use disorder

A person can become physically dependent while taking a drug regularly without developing compulsive use.

A use disorder involves a broader pattern of:

- craving;
- inability to cut down;
- escalating use;
- hazardous use;
- repeated blackouts/injury;
- continued use despite harm;
- major time spent obtaining/using/recovering;
- interference with responsibilities.

That distinction affects treatment planning.

---

## Treatment and recovery support

There is no meclonazepam-specific detox medication or validated home-conversion protocol.

When dependence is likely, clinicians generally use established benzodiazepine-withdrawal principles, individualized to the person and actual exposure.

Emergency or inpatient evaluation may be appropriate for:

- seizures;
- delirium;
- severe agitation/confusion;
- unstable vital signs;
- complicated alcohol/benzodiazepine co-withdrawal;
- severe polysubstance overdose.

## Drug checking, toxicology and detection

### Metabolites matter

Because meclonazepam is extensively metabolized, testing only for parent drug can reduce sensitivity.

Human studies support amino- and acetamido-metabolite targets, particularly **7-acetamidomeclonazepam** in urine.

### Routine immunoassays

Routine benzodiazepine immunoassays were not designed to comprehensively identify every designer benzodiazepine.

Cross-reactivity varies. A negative screen cannot reliably exclude meclonazepam.

### Definitive methods

Published studies use:

- nano-LC-HRMS;
- LC-HRMS/MS;
- other targeted chromatographic mass-spectrometry methods.

These methods allow more specific identification of parent drug and metabolites.

---

## Counterfeit and mislabeled product risk

Meclonazepam's name may appear on research-chemical listings, but labeling is not chemical confirmation.

Potential problems include:

- a different benzodiazepine sold under the name;
- multiple active sedatives;
- inaccurate concentration;
- product degradation;
- incomplete analytical certificates.

A certificate of analysis only answers the analytes and sample actually tested.

---

## Forensic interpretation

A positive urine or blood result establishes exposure.

It does not automatically establish:

- exact time of use;
- degree of impairment at an earlier moment;
- whether the drug caused all observed symptoms;
- whether it was the sole cause of death;
- a precise dose.

Forensic interpretation must integrate metabolites, specimen timing, co-drugs, clinical findings and scene history.

---

## Epidemiology and current trend signal

Meclonazepam has appeared in European NPS monitoring and intoxication surveillance but has not reached the same recent forensic prominence as bromazolam.

Lower prevalence does not mean lower pharmacologic risk. It means the population-level evidence is thinner.

The current high-value signals are:

- STRIDA acute-intoxication detections;
- human metabolism studies;
- broader designer-benzodiazepine forensic reviews.

---

## Legal and regulatory status

UNODC lists meclonazepam in its Early Warning Advisory database as a benzodiazepine/sedative-hypnotic NPS.

As of **October 3, 2026**, this review does not identify a U.S. federal scheduling order specifically naming meclonazepam. State law is different: Delaware explicitly lists meclonazepam among controlled benzodiazepines, and other states have enacted designer-benzodiazepine controls.

Legal status can change and may also depend on analogue or intended-use provisions. A vendor describing a product as “legal” is not a reliable legal analysis.

---

## Special populations

Direct evidence is inadequate for:

- pregnancy;
- breastfeeding;
- adolescents;
- older adults;
- liver/kidney impairment;
- respiratory disease.

Benzodiazepine-class concerns are especially important with respiratory vulnerability, fall risk, opioid use, alcohol use or multiple sedating medications.

---

## Myths vs evidence

### “Meclonazepam is clonazepam with one predictable change.”

**False.** It is structurally related but clinically under-characterized. No validated conversion exists.

### “Its antiparasitic research history means it was proven safe.”

**False.** CNS adverse effects were a reason it did not become a practical antiparasitic therapy.

### “A urine metabolite level tells you how impaired someone was.”

**False.** Urine is useful for exposure detection, not a simple impairment meter.

### “A negative routine benzodiazepine screen rules it out.”

**False.** Designer benzodiazepines may require targeted mass spectrometry.

### “Withdrawal risk is unknown, so abrupt stopping must be safe.”

**False.** Benzodiazepine-class dependence and withdrawal risks remain relevant even when molecule-specific prospective studies are missing.

### “Physical dependence is the same as addiction.”

**False.** They can overlap but are distinct clinical concepts.

## What remains unknown

Major evidence gaps include:

- controlled human PK;
- receptor-subtype pharmacology in clinically interpretable models;
- isolated overdose cohorts;
- blood concentration-response data;
- frequency of amnesia/blackouts;
- long-term cognitive outcomes;
- dependence incidence;
- withdrawal onset/duration;
- pregnancy/breastfeeding safety;
- validated equivalence or taper conversions;
- current prevalence in North American illicit markets.

---

## Bottom line

Meclonazepam deserves a dedicated monograph because it is **chemically defined, historically important, analytically detectable and documented in human intoxication surveillance**.

But it remains far less clinically characterized than clonazepam. The responsible evidence stance is therefore to separate what is known from what is borrowed from the benzodiazepine class.

**Human metabolite and exposure evidence are real. Precise potency, half-life, toxic thresholds and withdrawal timing are not established.** The practical safety priorities are CNS depression, polysubstance exposure, dependence, medically dangerous withdrawal and the possibility that routine screening will miss the compound.
