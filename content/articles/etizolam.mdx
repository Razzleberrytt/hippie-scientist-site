# Etizolam: Counterfeit Xanax, Opioid Deaths, Dependence & U.S. Schedule I Status

## Quick answer

**Etizolam is a thienodiazepine drug with benzodiazepine-like effects.**

It has been used medically in some countries, but it also became widely available in unregulated online markets and counterfeit tablets.

In the United States, etizolam is now permanently **Schedule I**, effective April 1, 2026.

## Why etizolam is different from many RC benzos

Etizolam was not invented solely for recreational markets.

It has a history of legitimate medical use outside the United States.

That means there is more pharmacologic and clinical information than exists for many newer designer benzodiazepines.

But a pharmaceutical history does not make an unregulated tablet predictable.

## Counterfeit Xanax

A study of counterfeit benzodiazepine tablets found large variation in etizolam content among visually similar pills.

That matters because a consumer may assume:

- the imprint identifies alprazolam;
- every tablet in a batch contains the same amount;
- the product is pharmaceutical.

Those assumptions can all be wrong.

## Etizolam in opioid-related deaths

A U.S. case series described 10 decedents with both etizolam and opioids in their systems.

Investigators concluded that many appeared **unaware of the etizolam exposure**.

That is an important public-health pattern: benzodiazepines may be present as unexpected adulterants in opioid use.

## Pharmacology

Etizolam enhances GABA-A receptor signaling and produces familiar benzodiazepine-like effects:

- anxiolysis;
- sedation;
- muscle relaxation;
- psychomotor impairment;
- amnesia.

At high exposure or in combinations, severe CNS depression can occur.

## Dependence and withdrawal

Regular etizolam use can produce tolerance and physical dependence.

Withdrawal can include:

- severe anxiety;
- insomnia;
- tremor;
- agitation;
- seizures.

A history of medical use does not eliminate those risks.

## Alcohol and opioids

Combining etizolam with opioids or alcohol increases the likelihood of:

- profound sedation;
- loss of consciousness;
- aspiration;
- respiratory compromise.

## U.S. legal status

DEA permanently placed etizolam in Schedule I effective **April 1, 2026**, along with clonazolam, diclazepam, flualprazolam, and flubromazolam.

## Reddit signal

Etizolam is one of the most historically discussed “RC benzos,” often used as a comparison point for newer compounds.

Its reputation online is partly a legacy of years when pharmaceutical blister packs or bulk products were easy to obtain internationally.

Modern illicit products should not be assumed equivalent to those formulations.

## Bottom line

Etizolam sits at the intersection of legitimate pharmaceutical history and modern counterfeit-drug risk.

The strongest safety lesson is not whether etizolam “feels like Xanax.” It is that **unregulated tablets can contain variable amounts or appear unexpectedly in opioid-related deaths.**
