# 6-APB (Benzofury): Serotonergic Stimulant Toxicity, Psychosis & Evidence

## Quick answer

**6-APB is a benzofuran new psychoactive substance often described as an MDMA-like entactogen or stimulant.**

It affects serotonin, dopamine and norepinephrine systems and has documented acute human toxicity.

The published literature is much smaller than the size of its online reputation.

## Why it gets compared with MDMA

6-APB has monoaminergic pharmacology that can produce empathogenic and stimulant effects.

That overlap explains the comparison, but it does not establish identical duration, cardiovascular effects, serotonin toxicity risk or long-term safety.

## Confirmed psychosis case

A published case report described a previously healthy 21-year-old who developed agitation and paranoid behavior after 6-APB exposure.

Toxicological analysis confirmed 6-APB, while cannabis-related compounds were also present.

The case cannot define the average response to 6-APB, but it confirms that severe behavioral toxicity can occur.

## Stimulant and serotonergic risks

Important concerns include:

- hyperthermia;
- fast heart rate;
- elevated blood pressure;
- agitation;
- confusion;
- tremor;
- serotonin toxicity;
- dehydration and prolonged exertion.

## Why mixtures matter

Recreational NPS products frequently contain more than one drug.

A product sold as 6-APB can also be mislabeled, and a toxicology case can involve cannabis, stimulants or other serotonergic compounds.

That makes single-compound assumptions risky.

## The human evidence is small but not trivial

6-APB does not have the clinical-trial literature available for approved medicines or even the research base now available for MDMA. Still, analytically supported intoxication reports establish that serious behavioral toxicity can occur.

The published psychosis case is especially important because it demonstrates that an entactogen-like drug can produce severe agitation and paranoid thinking in a real-world exposure. Cannabis was also detected, so the case should not be simplified into a claim that 6-APB always causes psychosis.

The correct conclusion is narrower: **severe psychiatric toxicity is possible and co-exposures can modify the presentation**.

## Cardiovascular and temperature concerns

Benzofuran entactogens act on monoamine systems involved in heart rate, blood pressure, thermoregulation, and serotonin signaling.

That means risk can build through a combination of direct pharmacology and context: prolonged dancing or exertion, warm environments, dehydration, sleep loss, and other stimulants can all push physiology in the wrong direction.

There is not enough controlled human evidence to define a dependable safety margin across those conditions.

## Why long duration changes the risk picture

Community reports often describe 6-APB as long-lasting. Even without relying on anecdotal timing as a dosing guide, duration matters conceptually because a longer intoxication gives cardiovascular and thermoregulatory stress more time to accumulate.

It also creates a longer window for someone to combine additional substances because they think the first drug “isn’t working enough.”

This site deliberately avoids redosing advice for that reason.

## Emergency warning signs

Urgent assessment is appropriate for seizure, severe chest pain, very high temperature, collapse, severe confusion, marked muscle rigidity, or agitation that cannot be safely contained.

A drug being sold as a social or empathogenic substance does not make those signs less serious. The safest evidence-based framing is that 6-APB has **real human toxicity signals, limited controlled data, and substantial uncertainty around unregulated products**.

## Bottom line

6-APB has enough confirmed human toxicity evidence to reject the idea that “legal-high” or “MDMA-like” means predictable.

The safer interpretation is **serotonergic stimulant with limited human characterization and real severe-toxicity reports**.
