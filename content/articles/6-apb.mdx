# 6-APB (Benzofury): Serotonergic Stimulant Toxicity, Psychosis & Evidence

## Quick answer

**6-APB is a benzofuran new psychoactive substance often described as an MDMA-like entactogen or stimulant.**

It affects serotonin, dopamine and norepinephrine systems and has documented acute human toxicity.

The published literature is much smaller than the size of its online reputation.

## Why it gets compared with MDMA

6-APB has monoaminergic pharmacology that can produce empathogenic and stimulant effects.

That overlap explains the comparison, but it does not establish identical duration, cardiovascular effects, serotonin toxicity risk or long-term safety.

## Confirmed psychosis case

A published case report described a previously healthy 21-year-old who developed agitation and paranoid behavior after 6-APB exposure.

Toxicological analysis confirmed 6-APB, while cannabis-related compounds were also present.

The case cannot define the average response to 6-APB, but it confirms that severe behavioral toxicity can occur.

## Stimulant and serotonergic risks

Important concerns include:

- hyperthermia;
- fast heart rate;
- elevated blood pressure;
- agitation;
- confusion;
- tremor;
- serotonin toxicity;
- dehydration and prolonged exertion.

## Why mixtures matter

Recreational NPS products frequently contain more than one drug.

A product sold as 6-APB can also be mislabeled, and a toxicology case can involve cannabis, stimulants or other serotonergic compounds.

That makes single-compound assumptions risky.

## Bottom line

6-APB has enough confirmed human toxicity evidence to reject the idea that “legal-high” or “MDMA-like” means predictable.

The safer interpretation is **serotonergic stimulant with limited human characterization and real severe-toxicity reports**.
