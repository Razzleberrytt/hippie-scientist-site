# Nitazene Opioids

> **Overdose warning:** Nitazenes can cause profound opioid respiratory depression. If a person is unresponsive or breathing slowly or abnormally, call 911 and administer naloxone if available.

## Quick answer

Nitazenes are **synthetic 2-benzylbenzimidazole opioids**.

Important current examples include:

- isotonitazene;
- protonitazene;
- metonitazene;
- etonitazene;
- several newer analogues and metabolites.

They are important because some analogues have very high mu-opioid receptor potency and increasingly appear in counterfeit or mixed drug supplies.

## Why comparisons with fentanyl can mislead

Some nitazenes are more potent than morphine or fentanyl in specific receptor or animal assays.

But there is no single “nitazenes are X times fentanyl” number.

Potency varies by analogue and experimental system.

The clinically useful conclusion is simpler: **some nitazenes can produce life-threatening opioid toxicity at very small amounts, and illicit-product concentration may be unknown.**

## What overdose looks like

Confirmed nitazene intoxication can resemble other severe opioid poisonings:

- slowed or stopped breathing;
- severe sedation;
- pinpoint pupils;
- inability to awaken;
- blue or gray lips;
- collapse.

## Naloxone

Naloxone remains appropriate when opioid overdose is suspected.

Some confirmed nitazene cases have required repeated antagonist treatment and prolonged observation.

That does not mean there is a universal nitazene-specific naloxone dose. Clinical response guides treatment.

## Why routine testing can miss them

Many routine hospital or workplace screens were not designed for novel nitazenes.

Advanced mass-spectrometry methods may be needed.

This means a negative routine opioid screen does not always exclude exposure to a novel synthetic opioid.

## Counterfeit drugs

Nitazenes have been detected in counterfeit or illicit products sold as other drugs.

This can create an especially high-risk scenario: a person may not know an opioid is present at all.

## What comes after nitazenes? Orphines

Forensic surveillance in 2026 also identified a newer benzimidazolone opioid family sometimes called **orphines**, including cychlorphine, chlorphine and spirochlorphine. These are chemically distinct from nitazenes but can occupy the same high-risk synthetic-opioid market niche.

See [Orphine opioids: cychlorphine, chlorphine, spirochlorphine and the post-nitazene shift](/articles/orphine-opioids/).

## High-priority profile

- [Protonitazene](/articles/protonitazene/)

Future profiles should include isotonitazene, metonitazene, etonitazene and newer nitazene analogues with meaningful toxicology signals.

## Bottom line

Nitazenes are one of the most important modern NPS opioid families because potency, product identity, and routine detectability can all work against the user.

Any unexplained severe opioid toxidrome deserves emergency treatment regardless of what the product was sold as.
