# Designer Synthetic Opioids Beyond Nitazenes

> **Overdose warning:** Unknown synthetic opioids can cause rapid respiratory depression. If someone is unresponsive or breathing slowly or abnormally, call 911 and administer naloxone if available.

## Quick answer

The designer-opioid market is broader than fentanyl and nitazenes.

Major non-fentanyl examples include:

- U-47700 and related U-series opioids;
- brorphine and newer “orphine” analogues;
- 2-methyl-AP-237;
- AP-238 and other cinnamylpiperazine opioids.

## U-47700

U-47700 became a major example of an old medicinal-chemistry compound reappearing as a recreational NPS.

Published clinical reviews document classic opioid poisoning, including reduced consciousness and depressed breathing.

See [U-47700](/articles/u-47700/).

## Brorphine

Brorphine emerged after earlier synthetic opioids were scheduled and has been detected in fatal and non-fatal forensic casework, often with fentanyl or benzodiazepines.

See [Brorphine](/articles/brorphine/).

## AP-237 / AP-238 analogues

2-methyl-AP-237 and AP-238 are cinnamylpiperazine synthetic opioids.

Published postmortem casework and receptor studies establish real mu-opioid activity and fatal exposures.

See [2-Methyl-AP-237](/articles/2-methyl-ap-237/).

## Why routine screens can fail

Standard opioid immunoassays were not built around many novel synthetic opioids.

A person can therefore present with a convincing opioid toxidrome while an ordinary screen is negative.

## Naloxone

Naloxone remains appropriate when opioid poisoning is suspected, regardless of whether the exact novel opioid is known.

Unknown duration and co-drugs mean emergency monitoring is still important after reversal.

## Bottom line

The synthetic-opioid market repeatedly replaces one controlled molecule with another structurally distinct agonist.

A negative standard opioid screen or unfamiliar chemical name should never outweigh the clinical signs of respiratory depression.
