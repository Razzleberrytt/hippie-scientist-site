# 3-MeO-PCE

## Quick answer

**3-MeO-PCE is a PCP/PCE-family arylcyclohexylamine dissociative with published human forensic and metabolism evidence.**

Serious cases include polysubstance fatalities and a recent case in which severe behavioral disturbance may have contributed to a fatal fall.

## Fatal forensic evidence

A 2021 report described a death involving 2F-DCK, 3-MeO-PCE, 5-MeO-DMT, amphetamine, cocaine and cannabis.

The authors considered the death a polysubstance respiratory-depression/cocktail effect, so it would be inaccurate to call that an isolated 3-MeO-PCE fatal dose.

## Human metabolism

A 2024 study used human urine from known exposures plus experimental models to map 3-MeO-PCE metabolism and propose useful biomarkers for forensic detection.

## Behavioral risk

A 2026 case described a man who died after falling from a window. Toxicology found 3-MeO-PCE, its metabolite 3-HO-PCE, tramadol and alcohol.

The authors considered acute dissociation or agitation a plausible contributor to the circumstances while appropriately avoiding certainty.

## Why dissociative behavior matters

Severe dissociation can impair perception, judgment, balance and recognition of danger. A drug does not need to cause direct organ failure to contribute to fatal injury.

## Bottom line

3-MeO-PCE has enough forensic evidence to establish real-world serious harm, but not enough controlled human evidence for confident potency or safety thresholds.
