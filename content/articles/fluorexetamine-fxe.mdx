# FXE (Fluorexetamine)

## Quick answer

**Fluorexetamine (FXE) is an arylcyclohexylamine dissociative related to ketamine and methoxetamine.**

It is analytically confirmed in the recreational drug market and has appeared in modern surveillance of ketamine analogues.

The human compound-specific evidence remains thin.

## Real-world detection

CFSRE documented fluorexetamine as a novel hallucinogen/dissociative in 2022 using forensic analytical methods.

A 2026 French addictovigilance study later included FXE among ketamine analogues identified in real-world cases.

## Why the FXE name is confusing

The gray-market name “FXE” has historically been used inconsistently around related fluorinated arylcyclohexylamines.

That makes analytical confirmation important.

A product name cannot reliably distinguish fluorexetamine from a positional isomer or another ketamine analogue.

## Class-level risks

Arylcyclohexylamine intoxication can involve:

- impaired consciousness;
- severe dissociation;
- agitation;
- hypertension;
- accidents and injuries;
- loss of coordination;
- polysubstance complications.

For FXE specifically, incidence and toxic thresholds remain poorly defined.

## Bottom line

FXE is real and analytically documented, but the evidence is much thinner than the online discussion volume.

The page should therefore emphasize identity uncertainty and limited human data rather than pretend to know precise potency or duration.
