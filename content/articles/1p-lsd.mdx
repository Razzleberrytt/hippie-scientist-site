# 1P-LSD: Masterclass Monograph

> **Emergency safety:** Seizure, collapse, chest pain, severe overheating, persistent loss of consciousness, dangerous agitation, rapidly worsening confusion, or major breathing abnormalities require urgent medical assessment. If a product sold as 1P-LSD produces an unexpectedly severe or atypical syndrome, product substitution or another drug should be considered.

## Quick answer

**1P-LSD is 1-propionyl-LSD, an N1-acyl lysergamide and designer psychedelic closely related to LSD.**

Unlike many research-chemical psychedelics, it has direct human pharmacokinetic evidence. A controlled 2020 study in **two volunteers** found that after oral 1P-LSD, LSD was readily detected in serum and urine, supporting rapid hydrolysis and a **prodrug relationship**.

The same study found that the time course and subjective psychedelic effects were broadly comparable to LSD. Mechanistically, that makes 1P-LSD one of the better-characterized RC psychedelics.

The limitation is sample size. Two people can strongly answer a narrow metabolism question; they cannot establish population-wide psychiatric safety, cardiovascular risk, uncommon adverse-event rates, interaction safety, or the reliability of gray-market blotters.

## History and market emergence

1P-LSD appeared on the NPS market as a lysergamide sold in blotter and powder form.

Its modern scientific characterization began with analytical and animal work published in 2016. That work established its chemical identity and showed LSD-like, 5-HT2A-dependent behavioral effects in animals.

By 2020, controlled human administration confirmed the key mechanistic hypothesis: 1P-LSD is rapidly converted to LSD in humans.

UNODC now lists 1P-LSD in its lysergamide NPS database. In 2024 reporting, **20 countries or territories** reported 1P-LSD, making it one of the more geographically reported classic-hallucinogen NPS in that dataset.

That is a surveillance measure, not a population-prevalence estimate.

## Chemistry and relationship to LSD

1P-LSD adds a **propionyl group at the indole N1 position** of LSD.

That modification changes the parent molecule's chemistry and analytical behavior, but the human pharmacokinetic evidence supports rapid removal of the propionyl group in vivo to form LSD.

This distinction matters because:

- the product can be analytically 1P-LSD before use;
- the body can rapidly convert it to LSD;
- a biological sample may contain LSD with little or no detectable parent 1P-LSD;
- legal classification may still treat the molecules differently depending on jurisdiction.

## Pharmacodynamics

LSD's psychedelic effects are strongly linked to **5-HT2A receptor agonism**.

Preclinical 1P-LSD work found LSD-like behavior blocked by a selective 5-HT2A antagonist, consistent with serotonergic psychedelic activity.

Later biotransformation research supported the view that 1-acyl lysergamides act mainly as **prodrugs** that generate LSD.

The key evidence boundary is that an animal potency ratio is not a human potency-conversion table. Mouse head-twitch data should not be converted into a recreational equivalence.

## Human pharmacokinetics

The 2020 controlled study administered 1P-LSD orally and intravenously to two volunteers and measured parent drug plus LSD.

### Oral administration

After oral exposure, the investigators detected LSD in serum and urine and found evidence consistent with extensive conversion of 1P-LSD to LSD.

The calculated oral bioavailability of LSD after 1P-LSD was close to complete in those participants.

### Intravenous administration

After intravenous exposure, parent 1P-LSD could be detected only for a relatively short period, while LSD persisted longer.

### Elimination

The study estimated an LSD terminal half-life around **6 hours** in this experimental context.

These are valuable mechanistic data, but the sample of two means they should not be treated as a complete population PK model.

## Analytical stability and the parent-metabolite problem

A 2019 LC-MS/MS validation paper adds a crucial forensic point: 1P-LSD can hydrolyze to LSD in biological samples, especially under some storage conditions.

In the investigated intoxication case, LSD was measurable while 1P-LSD itself was not detected.

That creates two interpretation problems:

1. **biology:** rapid in-vivo conversion can leave LSD as the dominant analyte;
2. **sample handling:** ex-vivo hydrolysis can also contribute under some conditions.

A toxicology result therefore needs analytical context before concluding exactly which lysergamide was originally consumed.

## Human effects: what is actually documented

Controlled human data support a psychedelic subjective-effect profile broadly comparable in time course to LSD.

The direct human dataset remains tiny.

That means the literature does **not** establish:

- uncommon severe-reaction rates;
- risk in people with cardiovascular disease;
- risk in people with bipolar or psychotic disorders;
- repeated-use outcomes;
- interaction safety across psychiatric medications;
- safety of unverified commercial blotter strength.

## Acute psychedelic risks

Potential acute problems include:

- panic or severe anxiety;
- disorientation;
- impaired judgment;
- accidental injury;
- dangerous behavior during extreme confusion;
- sympathetic activation;
- prolonged distress.

Seizure, collapse, severe hyperthermia, persistent unconsciousness, chest pain or major breathing abnormalities should not be normalized as “just a bad trip.”

Those features also raise concern for a substituted or mixed product.

## Interactions and polysubstance risk

### MAO inhibitors

There are no controlled 1P-LSD/MAOI safety trials. Because the parent compound converts to LSD and because MAO inhibitors can substantially alter monoamine pharmacology, combinations should not be assumed safe.

### Serotonergic stimulants and entactogens

Combining multiple serotonergic or sympathomimetic drugs can increase physiological and psychological complexity, including temperature and cardiovascular stress.

### Lithium and other psychiatric medications

LSD-class psychedelics have clinically important interaction concerns with some psychiatric medications, but compound-specific 1P-LSD data are sparse.

The correct conclusion is uncertainty, not a universal compatibility list.

## Tolerance

Direct repeated-dose human 1P-LSD tolerance studies are lacking.

LSD develops rapid tolerance through serotonergic mechanisms, so similar tolerance with a prodrug that generates LSD is biologically plausible.

A tolerance discussion should not be turned into advice for escalating exposure.

## Dependence and addiction

Classic psychedelics do not generally produce the same physical-dependence syndrome seen with opioids, alcohol or benzodiazepines.

There is no established 1P-LSD physical-withdrawal syndrome.

That does not mean repeated use is risk-free. A person can still develop maladaptive or compulsive use patterns, use despite psychiatric harm, or repeatedly put themselves in unsafe situations.

## Withdrawal

A clinically defined 1P-LSD withdrawal syndrome is **not established**.

No evidence supports a taper schedule.

Post-use fatigue, sleep disruption or emotional after-effects can occur after intense psychedelic experiences, but those are not equivalent to a proven physical withdrawal syndrome.

## Treatment and support

There is no 1P-LSD-specific antidote.

Acute management is based on the clinical syndrome: safe environment, monitoring, management of severe agitation or complications, and evaluation for substituted drugs when the presentation is atypical.

Persistent anxiety, derealization, perceptual disturbance, mania, psychosis or functional impairment after psychedelic exposure deserves professional evaluation.

For U.S. readers with problematic substance use, **FindTreatment.gov** and the **SAMHSA National Helpline (1-800-662-HELP)** can help locate services.

## Drug checking, toxicology and detection

Definitive identification may use:

- LC-MS/MS;
- LC-HRMS;
- validated reference standards.

Analysts need to account for rapid conversion and sample stability.

A routine drug screen may not specifically identify 1P-LSD. Depending on timing and method, **LSD may be the analyte detected instead**.

This is not test-evasion guidance; it is a forensic-interpretation issue.

## Counterfeit and mislabeled product risk

A blotter sold as “1P-LSD” is not chemically verified by:

- artwork;
- taste;
- seller reputation;
- subjective similarity to LSD.

A mislabeled product could contain another lysergamide or an entirely different potent psychedelic.

Analytical confirmation matters because two products capable of producing “psychedelic” effects may differ sharply in toxicity.

## Forensic interpretation

A positive LSD result does not necessarily prove direct LSD ingestion when 1P-LSD exposure is possible.

Conversely, parent 1P-LSD may disappear quickly from biological matrices.

Interpretation should consider:

- the analytical method;
- sample timing;
- sample storage;
- presence of parent drug;
- presence of LSD;
- clinical context;
- product testing if available.

## Epidemiology and current trend

UNODC's 2025 summary of 2024 NPS data reported 1P-LSD from **20 countries/territories**, ranking it among the most widely reported classic-hallucinogen NPS in that dataset.

This measures country-level reporting, not how many people used it.

The most defensible trend statement is that 1P-LSD has become an **internationally established lysergamide NPS**, not merely a niche online name.

## Legal and regulatory status — dated October 3, 2026

### International

UNODC states that lysergamide NPS including 1P-LSD have been reported internationally and that **no lysergamide NPS has been placed under international control since 2015**.

LSD itself is internationally controlled; 1P-LSD should not be assumed to share the exact same treaty status solely because it converts to LSD.

### United States federal law

The current Schedule I list in 21 CFR 1308.11 does not specifically name 1P-LSD.

That does **not** guarantee legality. Federal analogue-law questions can depend on chemical similarity, intended human consumption and case-specific facts.

### State law

States can list the exact molecule. Virginia, for example, specifically includes **1-propionyl lysergic acid diethylamide (1P-LSD)** in Schedule I.

## Special populations

There are inadequate 1P-LSD-specific data for:

- pregnancy and breastfeeding;
- adolescents;
- older adults;
- cardiovascular disease;
- seizure disorders;
- bipolar disorder;
- psychotic disorders.

Because 1P-LSD produces LSD after administration, known LSD-class concerns are relevant context, but exact compound-specific risk estimates are not available.

## Myths and common misconceptions

### “1P-LSD is completely different from LSD.”

Not pharmacokinetically. Human evidence strongly supports conversion to LSD.

### “Because it is a prodrug, it is safer than LSD.”

Not established. Prodrug conversion explains pharmacology; it does not prove lower risk.

### “Two human subjects prove it is safe.”

No. The study strongly answers metabolism questions but is far too small for broad safety conclusions.

### “If only LSD shows up in toxicology, the person must have taken LSD.”

Not necessarily. 1P-LSD can convert rapidly to LSD.

### “A blotter labeled 1P-LSD proves identity.”

No. Product identity requires analytical confirmation.

## FAQ

### Is 1P-LSD converted to LSD?

Yes. Controlled human pharmacokinetic evidence strongly supports rapid conversion.

### Does 1P-LSD have human studies?

Yes, but the key controlled PK study involved only **two volunteers**.

### Is 1P-LSD safer than LSD?

There is no evidence base strong enough to make that conclusion.

### Can toxicology mistake 1P-LSD exposure for LSD exposure?

Interpretation can be difficult because 1P-LSD rapidly forms LSD and parent drug may be absent. Analytical timing and sample handling matter.

### Is 1P-LSD internationally controlled?

UNODC lists it as a lysergamide NPS and notes that no lysergamide NPS has been placed under international control since 2015.

### Is it federally Schedule I in the U.S.?

1P-LSD is not specifically named in the current federal Schedule I list, but other federal legal theories and state laws may apply. Virginia specifically schedules it.

## Bottom line

1P-LSD is one of the clearest examples of an RC where the **mechanistic story is stronger than the safety dataset**.

Controlled human evidence makes the prodrug relationship to LSD compelling. What remains thin is everything a large clinical literature would normally answer: uncommon adverse events, interactions, repeated use, vulnerable populations and real-world product accuracy.

The useful harm-reduction conclusion is therefore precise: **1P-LSD is pharmacokinetically close to taking a precursor that generates LSD, but an unverified 1P-LSD product is not automatically predictable, legal or safe.**
