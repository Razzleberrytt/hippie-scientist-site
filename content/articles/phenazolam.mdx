# Phenazolam (Clobromazolam): Emerging Designer Benzo Evidence & Safety

## Quick answer

**Phenazolam is a novel designer benzodiazepine also known as clobromazolam.**

It is structurally similar to bromazolam and has become increasingly visible in U.S. forensic surveillance.

The evidence base is still thin: phenazolam is better characterized as an **emerging toxicology signal** than as a clinically understood drug.

## Why phenazolam is showing up now

Novel drug markets often replace one controlled or disrupted compound with a close analogue.

CFSRE noted rising phenazolam positivity during 2025 and discussed it in the context of bromazolam's international control and the tendency of NPS markets to shift toward related substitutes.

That does not prove a simple one-for-one replacement, but the pattern is consistent with how designer-drug markets evolve.

## What is known about its pharmacology?

Direct human pharmacology is poorly established.

Based on its benzodiazepine structure and similarity to related triazolobenzodiazepines, phenazolam is expected to enhance GABA-A receptor signaling and produce benzodiazepine-like central nervous system depression.

The key word is **expected**. Structural analogy is useful, but it is not a substitute for controlled human pharmacokinetic or dose-response studies.

## What forensic testing has found

CFSRE reported phenazolam in both blood specimens and drug materials.

It has appeared:

- alone;
- with other benzodiazepines;
- with opioids;
- with stimulants;
- alongside other novel psychoactive substances.

That polysubstance context makes real-world effects difficult to attribute to phenazolam alone.

## Likely acute risks

The expected benzodiazepine hazard pattern includes:

- sedation;
- slowed reaction time;
- impaired balance;
- amnesia;
- disinhibition;
- blackouts;
- loss of consciousness in severe intoxication.

The risk rises substantially when other depressants are present.

## Why opioid combinations are especially concerning

Novel benzodiazepines increasingly appear in an opioid supply already complicated by fentanyl, nitazenes and newer synthetic opioids.

A person who believes they purchased only one drug can therefore be exposed to both respiratory depression and prolonged sedation.

Naloxone can reverse an opioid component but does not reverse phenazolam itself.

## Dependence and withdrawal

Even though phenazolam-specific dependence studies are lacking, its benzodiazepine-type pharmacology makes physical dependence a serious concern with repeated exposure.

Abrupt withdrawal from benzodiazepine dependence can be medically dangerous and may include seizures or delirium.

## Bottom line

Phenazolam is important not because a large clinical literature has established exactly how it behaves, but because **forensic evidence shows that it is increasingly present while the human evidence remains sparse**.

That is exactly the kind of mismatch that makes an emerging RC worth taking seriously.
