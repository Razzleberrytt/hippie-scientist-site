# 2-Methoxyqualone: Emerging Methaqualone-Analogue Masterclass

> **Emergency safety:** If an unknown “qualone” product causes profound sedation, abnormal breathing, repeated vomiting with reduced consciousness, collapse, seizure-like activity, severe confusion or dangerous agitation, seek emergency care. Because the human toxicology of 2-methoxyqualone is poorly characterized, an unexpected presentation should raise concern for substitution or a mixed product.

## Quick answer

**2-Methoxyqualone is an emerging 4-quinazolinone NPS structurally related to methaqualone.**

What makes this page unusual is not a large toxicology literature. It is the opposite: **real-world exposure is now analytically proven, while pharmacology and clinical toxicity remain largely unknown**.

The compound was first reported after police seized recreational tablets in China in 2022. A later forensic study detected and quantified 2-methoxyqualone in authentic human scalp-hair samples, establishing repeated real-world human exposure.

In April 2026, CFSRE reported two U.S. drug-material detections — a tablet from Pennsylvania and a white powder from California — and stated that no CFSRE toxicology specimens had yet contained the drug.

The biggest evidence mistake would be to fill those gaps by copying methaqualone's effects, potency or withdrawal profile onto 2-methoxyqualone. Structural analogy supports caution; it does not create human data.

## History and market emergence

2-Methoxyqualone entered the scientific record as a **new recreational drug discovered in seized tablets**.

In 2022, police in Hohhot, Inner Mongolia provided suspicious tablets for forensic analysis. Researchers identified a new methaqualone analogue, 2-methoxyqualone.

Follow-up work expanded the analytical characterization across mass-spectrometry platforms.

A 2023 human-hair study then demonstrated that the compound was not only present in drug material: it could be detected in authentic hair samples from people investigated for drug use. That finding established real human exposure over time.

By April 2026, CFSRE had identified 2-methoxyqualone in two U.S. drug materials from Pennsylvania and California.

## Chemistry and relationship to methaqualone

2-Methoxyqualone belongs to the same broad **4-quinazolinone** chemical family as methaqualone.

That relationship makes sedative-hypnotic activity plausible, but the following are **not established**:

- human potency relative to methaqualone;
- human dose-response;
- onset and duration;
- half-life;
- active metabolites;
- a therapeutic or recreational equivalence.

A structural analogue should therefore be treated as a distinct drug until compound-specific evidence exists.

## Pharmacodynamics

CFSRE's 2026 monograph states that no published pharmacological-effect or potency data were available at the time of compilation and that sedative-hypnotic effects were **hypothesized** from methaqualone similarity.

That wording matters.

For 2-methoxyqualone itself, there is not enough evidence to state a receptor potency, clinical sedative profile or intoxication syndrome as established fact.

Class-level discussion can explain *why concern exists*, but it must remain labeled as extrapolation.

## Pharmacokinetics and metabolism

Controlled human pharmacokinetic data are not established.

Important unknowns include:

- absorption;
- elimination half-life;
- metabolic pathways;
- active metabolites;
- renal versus hepatic clearance;
- duration of impairment;
- postmortem behavior.

The lack of data means precise onset, duration or detection-window claims from forums should not be presented as evidence.

## Human evidence: what is actually documented

### Human hair confirmation

A 2023 Forensic Science International study developed a GC-MS/MS method and applied it to authentic human scalp-hair samples.

Reported hair concentrations ranged from **35.1 to 116 pg/mg** in the study's authentic cases.

Hair analysis supports prior exposure over time; it does **not** establish dose, timing of individual use episodes, impairment, or toxicity severity.

### What has not yet been established

CFSRE stated in April 2026 that it had not identified 2-methoxyqualone in toxicology specimens in its own casework.

The peer-reviewed literature still lacks a well-characterized acute human poisoning series that defines a 2-methoxyqualone toxidrome.

## Acute toxicity: evidence boundary

There is no strong compound-specific human dataset from which to list a reliable 2-methoxyqualone toxicity frequency or concentration range.

Because the molecule is a methaqualone analogue, clinically relevant concerns **may** include CNS depression, impaired coordination, altered consciousness and other sedative-hypnotic complications.

But those are **class-inferred concerns**, not documented 2-methoxyqualone outcome rates.

In an emergency, symptoms matter more than the expected label.

## Interactions and polysubstance risk

There are no controlled interaction studies.

By sedative-hypnotic class reasoning, the combinations that deserve the greatest concern are:

- opioids;
- alcohol;
- benzodiazepines;
- barbiturates;
- Z-drugs;
- other sedative NPS.

Additive CNS depression could plausibly increase loss of consciousness, aspiration and respiratory compromise.

This remains a mechanistic/class-level warning rather than a quantified 2-methoxyqualone interaction study.

## Tolerance and dependence

Direct human tolerance or dependence studies do not exist.

Methaqualone and other sedative-hypnotics can produce tolerance and dependence, so repeated 2-methoxyqualone exposure should not be assumed free of that risk.

However, the site does not claim that the rate, severity or timeline is the same as methaqualone.

## Withdrawal

A compound-specific 2-methoxyqualone withdrawal syndrome has not been characterized.

Potential sedative-withdrawal concerns are therefore **class-inferred**, not directly demonstrated.

There is no evidence basis for publishing:

- a day-by-day withdrawal timeline;
- a methaqualone conversion;
- a benzodiazepine conversion;
- a DIY taper schedule.

People using an unknown sedative regularly should seek medical assessment before abrupt discontinuation if dependence is possible, particularly when other depressants are also involved.

## Addiction / substance-use-disorder education

Even when the pharmacology is poorly characterized, behavior can reveal clinically important risk.

Warning signs include:

- escalating frequency;
- inability to cut down;
- craving;
- continued use despite blackouts, accidents or health problems;
- using despite work or relationship consequences;
- repeatedly combining unknown sedatives with opioids or alcohol.

Physical dependence and compulsive use are related but not identical concepts.

## Treatment and support

There is no 2-methoxyqualone-specific antidote, detox medication or evidence-based taper protocol.

For severe sedation, abnormal breathing, collapse, seizure or major confusion, emergency care is appropriate.

For regular use with concern about dependence, clinicians can evaluate the pattern of sedative exposure, co-drugs and withdrawal risk without pretending the exact product has a validated conversion ratio.

U.S. readers can use **FindTreatment.gov** or the **SAMHSA National Helpline (1-800-662-HELP)** to locate treatment.

## Drug checking, toxicology and detection

The strongest literature on 2-methoxyqualone is analytical.

Researchers have characterized the compound using:

- GC-MS/MS;
- high-resolution mass spectrometry;
- multiple MS platforms;
- hair toxicology methods.

The 2023 hair paper shows that authentic human exposure can be documented analytically.

Routine hospital drug screens are not designed to comprehensively identify novel quinazolinone sedatives.

## Counterfeit and product-identity risk

2-Methoxyqualone first entered the literature through suspicious recreational tablets.

That alone makes product identity a central part of the safety story.

A tablet or powder label does not establish:

- exact molecule;
- purity;
- concentration;
- absence of opioids or other sedatives.

CFSRE's U.S. detections were analytically confirmed in drug materials, which is stronger evidence than seller labeling.

## Forensic interpretation

A positive hair result proves exposure occurred during the period represented by the hair segment. It does not prove:

- intoxication at a specific moment;
- a particular dose;
- a clinical effect;
- causation of an event.

Because blood and postmortem toxicology data are sparse, there is no validated impairment threshold or lethal concentration.

## Epidemiology and current trend

The current signal is best described as **emerging analytical confirmation**, not established population prevalence.

Key milestones include:

- 2022: first published seized-tablet identification in China;
- 2023: authentic human hair confirmation;
- 2026: CFSRE reports U.S. drug-material detections in Pennsylvania and California.

Those data demonstrate geographic spread and real exposure while leaving prevalence unknown.

## Legal and regulatory status — dated October 3, 2026

CFSRE's April 2026 U.S. monograph states that **2-methoxyqualone was not currently scheduled in the United States** at the time of compilation.

That is a dated statement and should not be generalized to every state or future date.

Methaqualone's own controlled status does not automatically prove that 2-methoxyqualone has the same legal classification.

Given likely sedative-hypnotic properties, people with respiratory vulnerability or exposure to other CNS depressants should be considered potentially higher risk, but exact risk magnitude is unknown.

## Myths and common misconceptions

### “It is basically methaqualone.”

No. It is structurally related, but human potency and clinical effects are not established.

### “If it has shown up in human hair, we know its effects.”

No. Hair proves exposure, not intoxication severity or pharmacology.

### “No published poisoning case means it is safe.”

No. Sparse surveillance is not evidence of safety.

### “A seller's tablet label establishes identity.”

No. The scientific story of 2-methoxyqualone began with forensic analysis of suspicious tablets precisely because appearance and labeling were insufficient.

### “Methaqualone's withdrawal schedule can be copied over.”

No. A compound-specific withdrawal timeline has not been established.

## Evidence boundaries that matter in practice

The central safety problem with 2-methoxyqualone is not that a particular toxic effect has already been quantified; it is that **basic human pharmacology is still missing while real drug-material detections already exist**. That mismatch changes how the evidence should be read. Structural similarity can justify hypotheses about a sedative profile, but it cannot establish a human half-life, a therapeutic window, a reliable potency comparison, or a predictable interaction magnitude.

A second limitation is the difference between **drug-material evidence** and **clinical evidence**. An analytically confirmed powder proves that the compound reached the supply. It does not tell us how often people are exposed, whether material sold under the same name is chemically consistent, or which symptoms would occur at a given exposure. Until toxicology casework accumulates, claims about a characteristic human syndrome should remain provisional.

### Dependence and withdrawal

There is not enough direct human evidence to publish a 2-methoxyqualone-specific withdrawal timeline. If the compound produces sustained sedative-hypnotic activity, repeated exposure could plausibly create tolerance or physical dependence, but that remains a class- and mechanism-informed concern rather than a measured incidence estimate for this molecule. The site therefore should not turn anecdotal withdrawal reports into a fixed day-by-day schedule.

### Testing and product identity

Routine point-of-care drug screens are not designed to identify every novel quinazolinone. Confirming an unusual compound generally requires a laboratory method capable of separating and identifying the analyte with an appropriate reference standard or high-resolution spectral evidence. A negative routine screen therefore cannot establish that an unknown sedative product is free of 2-methoxyqualone or related analogues.

The safest evidence summary is deliberately conservative: the compound is **analytically real, human effects are poorly characterized, and uncertainty itself is part of the risk**. That is more useful than filling the gaps with borrowed claims from methaqualone or online experience reports.

## Substance use disorder and recovery support

Dependence and a **sedative use disorder** are related but distinct. A harmful pattern can involve impaired control, hazardous use, or continued use despite cognitive, respiratory, psychiatric, work, or relationship consequences.

Treatment evidence is exceptionally weak: a Cochrane review found no qualifying randomized trials for methaqualone dependence, and modern qualone analogues have even less direct evidence. That means there is no validated analogue-specific detox medication, conversion ratio, or self-taper protocol.

U.S. readers can use [FindTreatment.gov](https://findtreatment.gov/) or [SAMHSA treatment resources](https://www.samhsa.gov/substance-use/treatment). Seizure, delirium, unresponsiveness, or abnormal breathing requires urgent medical evaluation.
