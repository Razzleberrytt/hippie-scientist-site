# Orphine Opioids: Cychlorphine, Chlorphine, Spirochlorphine & the Post-Nitazene Shift

> **Overdose warning:** These are opioid-receptor agonists. If someone is unresponsive or breathing slowly or abnormally, call emergency services and give naloxone if available.

## Quick answer

**Orphines are a newer family of synthetic opioids built around a benzimidazol-2-one scaffold.**

The class includes compounds such as:

- chlorphine;
- N-propionitrile chlorphine, often called **cychlorphine**;
- spirochlorphine;
- spirobrorphine;
- dichloro desmethylchlorphine;
- dichloro brorphine analogues.

They matter because forensic programs began seeing them expand as the nitazene market changed.

## Why they appeared after nitazenes

CFSRE's 2026 alert described a decline in nitazene availability after new Chinese core-structure controls and a simultaneous rise in orphine-type opioids.

That does not mean one policy mechanically caused one replacement.

It does show a familiar NPS-market pattern: **when one potent opioid family becomes harder to supply, a chemically different family can occupy the same market niche.**

## Cychlorphine became an early high-risk signal

N-propionitrile chlorphine, or cychlorphine, was first detected by CFSRE in 2024.

By January 2026, the program was warning about increasing fatal-overdose detections.

That is a remarkably short timeline from first forensic confirmation to a meaningful death signal.

## How are orphines different from nitazenes?

Both groups can act as potent mu-opioid receptor agonists, but they are chemically distinct families.

Nitazenes are generally 2-benzylbenzimidazole opioids.

Orphines are benzimidazol-2-one derivatives related historically to compounds such as brorphine and older pharmaceutical discovery programs.

The overlap is pharmacological—not chemical identity.

## Do all orphines have the same potency?

No.

The family name is not a dose-equivalence category.

Different analogues can have different receptor activity, metabolism and duration. Laboratory potency estimates are also assay-dependent and should not be converted into unsupervised human dose calculations.

## Why detection is difficult

When a new family emerges, routine toxicology panels may not include the newest analogues.

Reference standards can also lag behind first market appearance.

That creates a period where a novel opioid can be clinically important before many laboratories can identify it routinely.

## Naloxone

Because these compounds act as opioids, naloxone is appropriate when an orphine exposure is suspected and a person has signs of opioid overdose.

Repeated treatment and medical observation may be needed depending on the exposure.

## Bottom line

Orphines show why the modern opioid market has to be tracked by **chemical families and forensic surveillance**, not just by familiar drug names.

The transition from nitazenes toward cychlorphine and related analogues happened quickly enough that public awareness can easily lag behind the drug supply.
