# ADB-BUTINACA (ADB-BINACA): Fatal Poisonings, Mislabeling & Synthetic Cannabinoid Risk

## Quick answer

**ADB-BUTINACA, also called ADB-BINACA, is a potent synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonist.**

It now has unusually compelling evidence for one of the central dangers in the RC market: **a product can be chemically nothing like what its label says.**

## The mislabeled fatality

A 2026 forensic report investigated a powder found at a fatality scene.

The package was labeled **MDPHP freebase**, a synthetic cathinone stimulant.

Advanced chemical analysis found that the powder was instead **greater than 98% ADB-BUTINACA**, a potent synthetic cannabinoid.

The authors assigned a high toxicological significance to ADB-BUTINACA and described the case as a fatal monointoxication.

This is a near-perfect example of why vendor labels cannot safely substitute for drug checking.

## Other fatal cases

A separate 2026 study examined three fatalities in which ADB-BUTINACA was detected across blood, urine, bile, vitreous humor, brain, liver, lung, and other tissues.

Those cases involved other substances, so the contribution of ADB-BUTINACA had to be interpreted in context.

## Prison and infused-paper detections

Earlier forensic work identified ADB-BUTINACA in toxicology specimens and in synthetic-cannabinoid-infused papers seized in prisons.

That demonstrates multiple exposure formats beyond traditional sprayed herbal “Spice.”

## Why stimulant vs cannabinoid mislabeling is especially dangerous

Someone expecting a cathinone stimulant may interpret early symptoms and make decisions based on the wrong drug family.

A potent synthetic cannabinoid can instead produce profound impairment, collapse, seizures, abnormal behavior, and cardiovascular instability.

## Bottom line

ADB-BUTINACA is one of the clearest modern examples of the RC identity problem.

A powder labeled as one drug class was analytically shown to be a high-purity compound from an entirely different—and highly potent—class.

That is why this site treats **identity uncertainty as a primary risk, not a footnote.**
