# 2F-2oxo-PCE (CanKet): The “FXE” Identity Mix-Up, Toxicology & Safety Evidence

## Quick answer

**2F-2oxo-PCE is a novel dissociative structurally related to ketamine and other arylcyclohexylamines.**

It became especially important because drug-checking and forensic work showed that material circulating under or around the name **FXE** did not always correspond to the compound originally meant by fluorexetamine.

That makes this an unusually clear example of why **vendor names are not chemical identification**.

## What is CanKet?

Australian drug checking first encountered an unknown ketamine-like substance in 2022 and informally called it “CanKet.”

Subsequent structural analysis identified the material as **2F-2oxo-PCE**.

2F-2oxo-PCE is a positional isomer of fluorexetamine, sometimes written 3F-2oxo-PCE.

The two compounds have the same molecular formula but differ in where the fluorine substitution sits on the aromatic ring.

## Why the FXE name became confusing

Early analytical techniques could identify a compound as a fluorexetamine-like isomer without necessarily resolving the exact positional isomer.

As more reference standards became available, labs could distinguish the compounds more confidently.

This means historical or vendor references to “FXE” may not always describe the same molecule.

## North American toxicology

By May 2024, CFSRE reported 2F-2oxo-PCE in more than 20 drug materials and 35 toxicology specimens spanning multiple U.S. states and Canadian provinces.

The alert also discussed postmortem cases and emphasized that formal toxicity data were still limited.

## The opioid co-occurrence is a major safety signal

More than 60% of the drug materials containing 2F-2oxo-PCE in the CFSRE alert also contained opioids, primarily fentanyl.

Other co-occurring substances included xylazine, novel benzodiazepines and nitazenes.

That means an unexpected loss of consciousness or slowed breathing after a supposed dissociative product should not automatically be attributed to “too much dissociation.”

## Expected dissociative risks

Based on its drug class and available reports, concerns include marked impairment, confusion, loss of coordination, altered perception, agitation, accidental injury and unconsciousness.

Controlled human pharmacokinetic and dose-response data remain sparse.

## Bottom line

2F-2oxo-PCE matters for two reasons: it is a poorly characterized dissociative **and** it demonstrates how a popular RC name can drift away from a precise chemical identity.

The safest interpretation of an “FXE” label is therefore not certainty—it is a question that analytical testing has to answer.
