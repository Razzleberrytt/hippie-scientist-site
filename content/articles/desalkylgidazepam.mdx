# Desalkylgidazepam (Bromonordiazepam): Designer Benzo Evidence & Safety

## Quick answer

**Desalkylgidazepam is an active benzodiazepine compound that is also a metabolite of the prodrug gidazepam.**

It is sometimes called bromonordiazepam and has emerged in recreational-drug markets as a substance in its own right.

CFSRE has identified it in forensic toxicology, but the RC-market human evidence base remains limited.

## Why the metabolite story matters

A metabolite can be pharmacologically active.

Desalkylgidazepam illustrates this clearly: a compound originally known largely through gidazepam metabolism can itself circulate as a drug product.

That means a lab finding does not necessarily prove that the person took gidazepam.

## Expected benzodiazepine effects

Based on its drug class, effects can include sedation, reduced anxiety, slowed reaction time, impaired balance, amnesia and muscle relaxation.

Long-lasting compounds or metabolites can create prolonged impairment even after the most obvious subjective effects fade.

## Accumulation and repeated exposure

If a benzodiazepine has a long elimination profile, repeated exposure can lead to accumulation.

That can make a product feel unexpectedly stronger on later days even when the apparent amount has not changed.

Precise kinetics for gray-market desalkylgidazepam products remain poorly characterized.

## Dependence and withdrawal

Repeated benzodiazepine exposure can produce physical dependence.

Abrupt withdrawal after substantial dependence can cause seizures, delirium and severe autonomic symptoms.

A long-acting RC can also make withdrawal timing less obvious than a user expects.

## Opioid combinations

As with other benzodiazepines, combining desalkylgidazepam with opioids, alcohol or other sedatives can increase the risk of severe central nervous system depression.

## Bottom line

Desalkylgidazepam is a good example of how RC markets repurpose compounds from pharmaceutical and metabolic literature.

The name may sound obscure, but the underlying hazard pattern—**benzodiazepine impairment, accumulation, dependence and dangerous depressant combinations**—is familiar.
