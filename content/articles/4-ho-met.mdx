# 4-HO-MET (Metocin): Masterclass Monograph

> **Emergency safety:** Seizure, collapse, chest pain, severe overheating, persistent unconsciousness, major breathing abnormalities, or dangerous agitation/confusion require urgent medical assessment. If the presentation is unexpectedly severe or atypical, a substituted or mixed product should be considered.

## Quick answer

**4-HO-MET is a synthetic 4-hydroxy tryptamine psychedelic** commonly called **Metocin** or **Methylcybin**.

It is structurally related to psilocin but is **not the same molecule**. Its human evidence base is small but stronger than a purely anecdotal RC page would suggest: researchers have characterized its metabolism using human liver microsomes and authentic urine, and a later non-fatal clinical toxicology case confirmed 4-HO-MET in plasma using LC-based mass spectrometry.

The biggest misconception is that similarity to psilocin means its potency, safety, metabolism and interaction profile can simply be copied from psilocybin mushrooms. They cannot.

## History and market emergence

4-HO-MET is part of the modern synthetic tryptamine NPS market.

UNODC lists it as **4-hydroxy-N-methyl-N-ethyltryptamine** within the tryptamine/classic-hallucinogen group and records international reporting.

Its online familiarity can create the impression of a well-characterized drug, but most of the strong evidence remains **analytical and forensic**, not prospective clinical pharmacology.

## Chemistry and relationship to psilocin

Psilocin is 4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine.

4-HO-MET replaces one of psilocin's N-methyl groups with an **ethyl group**.

That relatively small change is enough to make it a distinct chemical with its own metabolism and receptor profile.

Similarity supports a serotonergic psychedelic mechanism; it does not establish equal potency, duration or safety.

## Pharmacodynamics

Comparative receptor profiling shows that 4-HO-MET interacts with **5-HT2A receptors** and acts as a serotonergic hallucinogen.

Novel tryptamines can also differ in activity at other serotonin receptors and monoamine targets.

The clinically useful conclusion is that 4-HO-MET belongs in the classic-psychedelic pharmacology family.

The non-useful conclusion would be turning receptor affinity into a human dose-conversion chart.

## Metabolism

A 2018 study combined pooled human liver microsomes with **three authentic urine samples**.

Researchers identified:

- 12 in-vitro metabolites;
- four in-vivo metabolites.

Proposed pathways included:

- mono- and dihydroxylation;
- demethylation;
- combined demethylation/hydroxylation;
- deethylation;
- oxidative deamination;
- carboxylic-acid formation;
- glucuronidation.

The authors recommended the N-oxide and hydroxy-alkyl metabolites, glucuronides and parent 4-HO-MET as useful analytical targets.

## Human toxicology evidence

A 2022 Journal of Analytical Toxicology report described a **non-fatal clinical toxicology case** involving 4-HO-MET.

Only blood plasma was available.

Important analytical findings:

- LC-based screening detected 4-HO-MET;
- GC-MS did not detect the parent compound;
- sensitive LC-HRMS-MS detected multiple metabolites;
- the measured plasma concentration was 193 ng/mL.

That concentration is a case observation, **not a toxicity threshold or safe range**.

## Why the testing lesson matters

The 2022 case demonstrates a broader NPS problem: a negative result on one analytical platform does not necessarily rule out exposure.

Different methods have different sensitivity and target coverage.

For 4-HO-MET, LC-HRMS-MS detected metabolites that less sensitive approaches missed.

This is why routine clinical drug screens can be falsely reassuring for novel tryptamines.

## Acute psychedelic risks

Human outcome data are too sparse to assign incidence rates, but clinically important psychedelic/NPS risks include:

- severe anxiety or panic;
- disorientation;
- impaired judgment;
- accidental injury;
- agitation;
- seizure;
- hyperthermia;
- chest pain;
- collapse;
- persistent altered consciousness.

A severe presentation may reflect 4-HO-MET, an interaction, an adulterant or a different drug entirely.

## Interactions and polysubstance risk

### MAO inhibitors

No controlled 4-HO-MET/MAOI safety studies exist. Serotonergic pharmacology makes strong monoamine interactions a significant uncertainty.

### Other serotonergic drugs

Entactogens, serotonergic stimulants and other psychedelics can increase both physiological and psychological complexity.

### Lithium and psychiatric medications

Classic-psychedelic interactions with some psychiatric medications can be clinically important, but 4-HO-MET-specific evidence is inadequate.

### Alcohol and sedatives

Depressants may worsen judgment and injury risk without reliably preventing psychedelic distress.

## Tolerance

Controlled repeated-dose human studies are lacking.

Rapid serotonergic psychedelic tolerance is biologically plausible, but the exact 4-HO-MET pattern has not been established clinically.

Tolerance should not be used as a rationale to escalate exposure.

## Dependence and withdrawal

A classic physical-dependence syndrome has not been established for 4-HO-MET.

There is no validated withdrawal timeline or taper protocol.

Repeated problematic use can still occur without physical withdrawal, especially if use continues despite psychiatric, social or safety harms.

## Treatment and support

There is no 4-HO-MET-specific antidote.

Acute management is based on the clinical syndrome: safe environment, monitoring, treatment of severe agitation or seizures, temperature management, and evaluation for co-drugs.

Persistent psychosis, mania, severe anxiety, derealization, perceptual disturbance or functional decline after use deserves professional evaluation.

For U.S. readers, **FindTreatment.gov** and the **SAMHSA National Helpline (1-800-662-HELP)** can help locate services.

## Drug checking, toxicology and detection

Useful methods include:

- LC-HRMS-MS;
- LC-MS/MS;
- validated multi-analyte tryptamine panels.

The 2022 case showed that GC-MS may fail where more sensitive LC-based approaches succeed.

Metabolite-aware testing can strengthen exposure confirmation.

## Counterfeit and product-identity risk

A powder, capsule, liquid or gummy sold as 4-HO-MET does not prove identity.

Possible issues include:

- another substituted tryptamine;
- a mixture of psychedelics;
- stimulant or sedative admixtures;
- incorrect concentration.

Subjective “psilocin-like” effects cannot identify the molecule.

## Forensic interpretation

A positive 4-HO-MET result proves exposure, not automatically causation.

A measured plasma concentration from one case should not be turned into a universal impairment or toxicity threshold.

Interpretation should include:

- co-drugs;
- specimen type;
- timing;
- analytical method;
- metabolites;
- clinical context.

## Epidemiology and current trend

UNODC records 4-HO-MET as a reported tryptamine NPS.

That establishes international market presence but not general-population prevalence or severe-event frequency.

The current evidence is best described as **established NPS circulation with sparse clinical outcome data**.

## Legal and regulatory status — dated October 3, 2026

### International

UNODC's tryptamine overview states that DMT, etryptamine, DET, psilocin and psilocybin are the tryptamines internationally controlled under the 1971 Convention.

4-HO-MET is not in that listed group.

### United States federal law

The current federal Schedule I list does not specifically name 4-HO-MET.

That does not guarantee legality; federal analogue-law questions can depend on chemical relationship, intended use and case facts.

### State law

Virginia specifically lists **4-hydroxy-N-methyl-N-ethyltryptamine (4-HO-MET/Metocin)** in Schedule I.

Other states differ.

## Special populations

There are inadequate compound-specific data for:

- pregnancy and breastfeeding;
- adolescents;
- older adults;
- cardiovascular disease;
- seizure disorders;
- bipolar disorder;
- psychotic disorders.

Evidence absence should not be interpreted as low risk.

## Myths and common misconceptions

### “4-HO-MET is basically psilocin.”

No. It is structurally related but chemically distinct.

### “A negative GC-MS result rules it out.”

No. A published clinical case detected 4-HO-MET with LC-based methods after GC-MS failed to detect it.

### “One measured plasma concentration defines a toxic level.”

No. One case cannot establish a universal threshold.

### “No physical withdrawal means no misuse risk.”

No. Problematic use can occur without classic physical dependence.

### “A familiar psychedelic feeling confirms the product.”

No. Subjective effects do not establish chemical identity.

## FAQ

### Is 4-HO-MET the same as psilocin?

No. It is a structurally related tryptamine with one different N-alkyl substituent.

### Has 4-HO-MET been detected in humans?

Yes. Authentic urine metabolism and a non-fatal plasma toxicology case have been published.

### Can routine toxicology miss it?

Yes. A published case illustrates that sensitive LC-based mass spectrometry may succeed when GC-MS does not.

### Does it cause withdrawal?

A specific physical-withdrawal syndrome has not been established.

### Is it internationally controlled?

It is not among the tryptamines UNODC lists as internationally controlled under the 1971 Convention.

## Dependence, tolerance, withdrawal and recovery

No prospective study has defined **4-HO-MET dependence, tolerance progression, or a withdrawal timeline** in humans.

As a serotonergic psychedelic, the closest evidence comes from the broader classic-psychedelic literature. That class is not typically associated with a dangerous physical-withdrawal syndrome resembling alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal. Rapid tolerance and less typical daily compulsive use are often discussed at the class level, but those observations should not be converted into a guarantee for 4-HO-MET.

A person can still develop a harmful pattern of use without classic physical withdrawal. Repeated exposure despite panic, persistent perceptual changes, psychiatric destabilization, injuries, financial or relationship harm, or inability to cut down can justify a substance-use assessment.

Persistent visual changes, flashbacks, derealization, or anxiety after stopping should be evaluated on their own merits rather than automatically labeled withdrawal.

### Recovery and support

Support can focus on stopping further unverified exposure, stabilizing sleep and mental health, and evaluating persistent psychiatric or perceptual symptoms. U.S. readers can use [FindTreatment.gov](https://findtreatment.gov/) or [SAMHSA treatment resources](https://www.samhsa.gov/substance-use/treatment). Acute suicidality, psychosis with inability to remain safe, seizure, or severe medical symptoms requires urgent care.


## Bottom line

4-HO-MET has enough human analytical evidence to move beyond a purely anecdotal “psilocin-like RC” description.

What the evidence supports is **real human exposure, defined metabolic pathways, serotonergic psychedelic pharmacology and important testing limitations**.

What it does not support is a precise safety margin, potency conversion or casual assumption that psilocin evidence transfers directly.
