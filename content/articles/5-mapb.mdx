# 5-MAPB: Benzofuran Entactogen Masterclass

> **Emergency safety:** Severe overheating, seizure, chest pain, collapse, extreme agitation, rapidly worsening confusion, persistent unconsciousness, clonus with muscle rigidity, or a very abnormal heartbeat require emergency medical care. An unexpected syndrome should not be assumed to come from 5-MAPB alone because NPS products can be substituted or mixed.

## Quick answer

**5-MAPB is a benzofuran stimulant-entactogen** whose systematic name is **1-(benzofuran-5-yl)-N-methylpropan-2-amine**. It is the N-methyl analogue of 5-APB and is often described as MDMA-like because of its monoamine pharmacology and entactogenic effects.

That comparison has limits. 5-MAPB is not MDMA, and controlled comparative human trials are sparse.

The strongest human toxicity report documented hypertension, tachycardia, diaphoresis, mild hyperthermia, tremor, hyperreflexia, clonus, agitation, hallucinations, convulsions and reduced consciousness after analytically confirmed exposure. The same case estimated first-order elimination with a half-life of about **6.5 hours**, but one case should not be treated as a universal pharmacokinetic value.

A 2024 forensic report also identified 5-MAPB in a fatal **polydrug** intoxication. It supports real-world fatal-risk context without defining a stand-alone lethal 5-MAPB concentration.

## History and market emergence

Aminoalkylbenzofurans emerged in NPS markets as alternatives to established entactogens and stimulants. 5-MAPB entered scientific toxicology literature in the early 2010s.

WHO added 5-MAPB to its **surveillance list in 2017**. The current ECDD repository states that it has been reported as misused in multiple Member States and identified in seized products, but it has **not been pre-reviewed or critically reviewed** by the ECDD.

That distinction matters: surveillance is not the same thing as an international scheduling recommendation.

## Chemistry and relationship to 5-APB, 6-APB and MDMA

5-MAPB has a benzofuran ring system rather than MDMA's methylenedioxyphenyl ring.

The most direct relationship is:

- **5-APB:** primary amine;
- **5-MAPB:** N-methyl analogue.

The “5” and “6” compounds are positional isomers. 5-MAPB and 6-MAPB are therefore distinct molecules and may require analytical separation.

Structural similarity does not establish equal dose, duration or toxicity.

## Pharmacodynamics

### Monoamine transporters

Preclinical studies support activity at the **serotonin, dopamine and norepinephrine transport systems**.

5-MAPB can promote monoamine release and shows an MDMA-like neurochemical profile in animal and in-vitro studies. That provides a plausible mechanism for stimulant effects, entactogenic effects, autonomic activation, hyperthermia and serotonin toxicity.

### Serotonin receptors

Binding studies also show serotonergic receptor activity.

These findings help explain risk, but receptor affinity must not be turned into a human potency ranking or dose-equivalence table.

### MAO-A signal

In-vitro profiling has reported monoamine oxidase-A inhibitory activity. That is a mechanistic interaction concern, especially with other monoaminergic drugs, but it does not establish the magnitude of MAO inhibition during typical human exposure.

## Pharmacokinetics and metabolism

The best human elimination estimate comes from a **single confirmed poisoning case**, which found an apparent half-life of about **6.5 hours**.

Metabolism research describes:

- **N-demethylation to 5-APB**;
- aromatic oxidation;
- downstream carboxylated/hydroxylated products;
- conjugated metabolites.

In authentic human urine, parent 5-MAPB and metabolites were detected. A 2015 analytical study also reported clinical plasma concentrations in six human cases, demonstrating that both parent drug and N-demethyl metabolite can be measured in real-world exposure.

Those values are analytical observations, not “safe” or “toxic” thresholds.

## Human effects: what is actually documented

### Confirmed acute poisoning

The 2017 case involved a previously healthy 24-year-old with analytically confirmed 5-MAPB exposure.

Observed findings included:

- hypertension and tachycardia;
- ECG change and elevated cardiac biomarker;
- sweating and mild hyperthermia;
- mydriasis and tremor;
- hyperreflexia and clonus;
- agitation, disorientation and hallucinations;
- convulsions;
- reduced consciousness.

Symptoms substantially improved with intensive supportive treatment.

This is direct evidence that severe toxicity can involve cardiovascular, neurological and thermoregulatory systems at the same time.

### Fatal co-intoxication

A 2024 case report described fatal co-intoxication involving **alpha-methyltryptamine, 5-MAPB, benzodiazepines, ephedrine and norephedrine**.

Because multiple active drugs were present, the case should not be used to define a lethal 5-MAPB concentration or to claim 5-MAPB alone caused the death.

## Acute toxicity and serotonin-toxicity features

Potential severe findings include:

- rapid heart rate;
- hypertension;
- vasoconstriction;
- sweating;
- hyperthermia;
- agitation;
- confusion;
- hallucinations;
- tremor;
- hyperreflexia;
- clonus;
- seizure;
- reduced consciousness.

The combination of **clonus, hyperreflexia, agitation and hyperthermia** is particularly concerning for serotonin toxicity.

## Cardiovascular risk

The confirmed poisoning case included hypertension, tachycardia, ECG change and elevated high-sensitivity troponin T.

That does not establish the frequency of cardiac injury, but it demonstrates that clinically important cardiovascular stress can occur.

Risk can be amplified by other stimulants, dehydration, overheating, prolonged exertion and underlying cardiovascular disease.

## Interactions and polysubstance risk

### MAO inhibitors

Combining monoaminergic stimulants/entactogens with MAO inhibitors can produce dangerous toxicity. There are no controlled 5-MAPB interaction trials that define a safe combination.

### Other serotonergic drugs

Other entactogens, serotonergic stimulants and serotonergic medications can add to serotonin load.

### Other stimulants

Amphetamine-type stimulants, cocaine and cathinones can add cardiovascular, thermal and psychiatric stress.

### Alcohol and sedatives

Sedatives can impair judgment and obscure deterioration. Polydrug use also makes toxicology interpretation more difficult.

## Tolerance, dependence and compulsive use

Direct longitudinal human studies of 5-MAPB dependence are lacking.

Preclinical reward and drug-discrimination studies support **abuse-liability potential** for the class, but they do not tell us how often people develop a substance use disorder.

Warning signs include escalating frequency, inability to stick to intended limits, sacrificing sleep or responsibilities, craving, or continuing despite cardiovascular, psychiatric or relationship harms.

Physical dependence is not the same thing as compulsive use or a substance use disorder.

## Withdrawal and post-use after-effects

A validated 5-MAPB-specific withdrawal syndrome or day-by-day timeline has not been established.

Possible stimulant-entactogen after-effects include:

- fatigue;
- sleep disruption;
- low mood;
- irritability;
- reduced motivation;
- concentration difficulty;
- appetite changes.

These should be described as possible class-level after-effects rather than a proven unique 5-MAPB withdrawal syndrome.

Severe depression, suicidal thoughts, persistent psychosis or medical instability warrants professional evaluation.

## Treatment and recovery support

There is no approved 5-MAPB-specific antidote, detox medication or withdrawal protocol.

Acute poisoning is managed according to the clinical syndrome, including airway/breathing support, temperature management, seizure/agitation control, cardiovascular monitoring and evaluation for complications.

For persistent compulsive stimulant use, evidence-based substance-use treatment and mental-health support are more appropriate than improvised detox regimens.

U.S. readers can use **FindTreatment.gov** or the **SAMHSA National Helpline (1-800-662-HELP)** to locate services.

## Drug checking, toxicology and detection

Routine immunoassays are not designed to comprehensively identify benzofuran NPS.

Definitive testing may require:

- LC-MS/MS;
- LC-HRMS;
- GC-MS with suitable methods;
- current reference standards.

A major analytical issue is **positional-isomer differentiation**. 5-MAPB and 6-MAPB may require chromatographic methods capable of separating the isomers.

Metabolite-aware interpretation is useful because 5-MAPB can form **5-APB** by N-demethylation.

## Counterfeit and product-identity risk

Powder appearance, crystal form, tablet branding or seller description cannot confirm 5-MAPB identity.

Potential problems include:

- another benzofuran sold as 5-MAPB;
- 5-MAPB sold as a more familiar entactogen;
- mixtures of related compounds;
- stimulant/cathinone substitution;
- variable purity or concentration.

A certificate of analysis only speaks to the specific sample and analytes actually tested.

## Forensic interpretation

A positive result proves exposure, not automatically causation.

Interpretation should consider:

- co-detected substances;
- timing;
- specimen type;
- concentration;
- tolerance;
- metabolites;
- scene and clinical evidence.

The 2024 fatal co-intoxication is a strong example of why one postmortem number should not be presented as a universal “fatal level.”

## Epidemiology and current trends

WHO's surveillance record confirms international misuse reports and identification in seized products.

However, there is no high-quality estimate of general-population prevalence from those data.

Surveillance counts, poisonings and seizure data should be described as **dated populations**, not as proof of global popularity.

## Legal and regulatory status — dated October 3, 2026

### International

WHO's ECDD repository lists 5-MAPB under surveillance and states that it has **not** undergone ECDD pre-review or critical review. No ECDD scheduling recommendation is listed.

### United States federal law

The current federal Schedule I list in 21 CFR 1308.11, reviewed in the version current through October 1, 2026, does not specifically name 5-MAPB.

That does **not** create a blanket legality conclusion. Federal analogue law and other provisions can depend on chemical similarity, intent and factual circumstances.

### U.S. state law

State law can be more explicit. For example, Florida specifically lists **5-MAPB**, and Virginia lists **(2-methylaminopropyl)benzofuran (MAPB)** in Schedule I.

Legal status therefore has to be checked by exact structure, jurisdiction and date.

## Special populations

Compound-specific evidence is inadequate for:

- pregnancy and breastfeeding;
- adolescents;
- older adults;
- cardiovascular disease;
- seizure disorders;
- liver or kidney disease;
- people taking psychiatric medications.

Class-level pharmacology supports extra caution in cardiovascular disease, seizure vulnerability and with medications that alter monoamine or serotonin signaling, but exact risk magnitudes are unknown.

## Myths and common misconceptions

### “5-MAPB is basically MDMA.”

It is related in pharmacology and subjective-effect category, but it is a distinct molecule with a much thinner human evidence base.

### “A smoother experience means lower medical risk.”

No. Subjective smoothness does not rule out hyperthermia, cardiovascular stress, seizure or serotonin toxicity.

### “A fatal blood concentration tells us the lethal dose.”

No. Co-drugs, timing, specimen type, tolerance and postmortem changes prevent that conversion.

### “A negative routine drug screen rules it out.”

No. Specialized mass-spectrometry methods may be required.

### “WHO surveillance means WHO scheduled it.”

No. The current WHO repository says 5-MAPB has not undergone pre-review or critical review.

## What we know vs what remains unknown

| Evidence level | Current conclusion |
| --- | --- |
| **Established** | 5-MAPB is the N-methyl analogue of 5-APB and a benzofuran stimulant-entactogen. |
| **Established** | Analytically confirmed human exposure can produce severe cardiovascular, neurological and serotonergic toxicity. |
| **Established** | 5-MAPB is metabolized in part to 5-APB and can be identified by mass-spectrometry methods. |
| **Probable / class-supported** | Repeated exposure can contribute to tolerance, compulsive use and stimulant-entactogen after-effects. |
| **Uncertain** | Frequency of severe toxicity, long-term effects, dependence incidence and exact comparative risk versus MDMA or other benzofurans. |
| **Not established** | A safe recreational dose, universal potency conversion, predictable lethal concentration or validated self-directed withdrawal protocol. |

## FAQ

### Is 5-MAPB the same as 5-APB?

No. 5-MAPB is the **N-methyl analogue** of 5-APB, although 5-APB can also appear as a metabolite after 5-MAPB exposure.

### Is 5-MAPB the same as MDMA?

No. It has MDMA-like monoamine effects but is chemically distinct and much less studied in humans.

### Can 5-MAPB cause serotonin toxicity?

A confirmed human poisoning included clonus, hyperreflexia, agitation and hyperthermia, which are compatible with serotonergic toxicity.

### Can it cause seizures?

Yes. Convulsions occurred in the confirmed acute poisoning case.

### How is it detected?

Definitive testing generally relies on chromatographic mass spectrometry and may need to distinguish 5-MAPB from positional isomers such as 6-MAPB.

### Is it internationally scheduled?

WHO currently lists 5-MAPB under surveillance but reports no ECDD pre-review or critical review and no scheduling recommendation.

## Bottom line

5-MAPB has enough direct human evidence to move beyond a generic “MDMA-like RC” description.

The strongest evidence establishes **real severe poisoning, human metabolism, forensic detectability and polydrug fatality context**, while major gaps remain in controlled human pharmacology, dependence incidence and long-term outcomes.

For harm reduction, the central message is not a potency comparison. It is that 5-MAPB is a **serotonergic stimulant-entactogen capable of serious hyperthermic, cardiovascular and neurologic toxicity**, with identity and interaction uncertainty layered on top.
