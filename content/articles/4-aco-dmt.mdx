# 4-AcO-DMT (Psilacetin): Masterclass Monograph

> **Emergency safety:** Seizure, collapse, severe overheating, chest pain, persistent loss of consciousness, major breathing abnormalities, or dangerous agitation/confusion require urgent medical assessment. If the product behaves very differently from an expected psychedelic, substitution or a mixture should be considered.

## Quick answer

**4-AcO-DMT is a synthetic psychedelic tryptamine** also called **psilacetin** or **O-acetylpsilocin**.

It is chemically close to psilocin and is widely discussed as a possible **psilocin prodrug**. That idea has meaningful support from metabolism and animal pharmacology, but it is still not backed by the kind of controlled human pharmacokinetic program available for psilocybin or 1P-LSD.

A 2022 human-liver-microsome study identified **hydrolysis as the most prominent metabolic pathway** and found multiple phase-I and phase-II metabolites. Preclinical receptor/behavior studies also support psychedelic 5-HT2A activity and are consistent with in-vivo deacetylation.

The biggest evidence problem is therefore not whether the compound is plausibly psychedelic. It is that **human clinical safety, pharmacokinetics, interaction risk, and uncommon adverse-event rates remain poorly characterized**.

## History and market emergence

Synthetic 4-substituted tryptamines entered NPS markets as alternatives to more familiar psychedelics.

A 10-year drug-checking follow-up published in 2017 found 4-AcO-DMT to be among the most frequently submitted non-regulated tryptamines in that dataset.

That popularity created an unusual mismatch: **substantial community familiarity but little controlled human science**.

UNODC now includes 4-AcO-DMT among reported tryptamine NPS, confirming that it is part of the international NPS landscape rather than only a niche online compound.

## Chemistry and relationship to psilocin and psilocybin

4-AcO-DMT is the **O-acetyl derivative of psilocin**.

That relationship is analogous in concept to how a chemical modification can create a precursor that is converted back to an active metabolite in the body.

Psilocybin, however, is a **phosphorylated** prodrug of psilocin, while 4-AcO-DMT is **acetylated**. They are not interchangeable molecules and should not automatically be given the same pharmacokinetic or safety profile.

## Pharmacodynamics

### 5-HT2A receptor activity

Preclinical structure-activity studies of psilocybin analogues show that 4-acetoxy tryptamines can activate 5-HT2 receptor systems and produce psychedelic-like behavior in animals.

O-acetylation reduced direct in-vitro 5-HT2A potency for some analogues, while in-vivo psychedelic-like potency was less affected. That pattern is consistent with **metabolic deacetylation before or during activity**.

### Evidence boundary

These findings do not provide a human potency ratio.

A receptor assay or animal head-twitch response cannot be converted into a human-equivalent dose or a statement that 4-AcO-DMT is “the same strength” as psilocin or psilocybin.

## Metabolism

The 2022 in-vitro metabolism study used pooled human liver microsomes and identified **15 proposed metabolites**.

Reported transformations included:

- hydrolysis;
- hydroxylation;
- N-demethylation;
- oxidation;
- glucuronidation.

The **hydrolysis product was the most abundant**, supporting the idea that psilocin formation is a major metabolic route.

The authors also proposed a beta-hydroxylated metabolite as a potentially useful biomarker for identifying exposure.

Important limitation: this was an **in-vitro human-liver-microsome study**, not a controlled human pharmacokinetic trial.

## Human evidence: what is actually established

There is no robust controlled human trial program defining:

- bioavailability;
- half-life;
- dose-response;
- cardiovascular effects;
- interaction risk;
- psychiatric adverse-event frequency;
- repeated-use outcomes.

Real-world drug-checking and forensic identification establish market presence and exposure, but not a complete toxidrome.

This is why the page keeps an evidence grade of **Very Low** despite the compound's popularity.

## Acute psychedelic risks

Potential harms should be framed from the broader serotonergic-psychedelic context and product uncertainty, not presented as precise 4-AcO-DMT incidence rates.

Clinically concerning findings include:

- severe panic or agitation;
- dangerous confusion;
- accidental injury;
- seizure;
- hyperthermia;
- chest pain;
- collapse;
- persistent loss of consciousness.

A severe or atypical presentation may indicate a different substance, mixture, or medical complication.

## Interactions and polysubstance risk

### MAO inhibitors

There are no controlled 4-AcO-DMT/MAOI interaction studies. Strong monoamine-system interactions should therefore be treated conservatively.

### Other serotonergic drugs

Entactogens, serotonergic stimulants and serotonergic medications can increase physiological complexity and may raise risk for agitation, hyperthermia or serotonin toxicity.

### Lithium and psychiatric medications

Classic psychedelics have important interaction concerns with some psychiatric medications, but compound-specific 4-AcO-DMT data are inadequate.

The absence of an interaction trial is not evidence of safety.

## Tolerance

Direct human tolerance studies are not established.

Because 4-AcO-DMT likely produces psilocin-like serotonergic signaling, rapid psychedelic tolerance is biologically plausible, but the exact degree and cross-tolerance profile have not been quantified clinically.

Tolerance should never be used as a rationale for exposure escalation.

## Dependence and withdrawal

A physical-dependence syndrome resembling opioids, alcohol or benzodiazepines has **not** been established for 4-AcO-DMT.

There is no validated withdrawal timeline and no reason to publish a taper schedule.

Psychological overuse, compulsive patterns or repeated risky use can still occur even without a classic physical-withdrawal syndrome.

## Treatment and support

There is no 4-AcO-DMT-specific antidote.

Acute management is guided by the syndrome: safe containment, monitoring, treatment of severe agitation or seizures, temperature management, and evaluation for substituted drugs when findings are atypical.

Persistent anxiety, psychosis, mania, derealization, perceptual disturbance or functional decline after psychedelic use deserves professional assessment.

For U.S. readers, **FindTreatment.gov** and the **SAMHSA National Helpline (1-800-662-HELP)** can help locate substance-use services.

## Drug checking, toxicology and detection

Mass-spectrometry-based methods can identify parent 4-AcO-DMT and proposed metabolites.

Because hydrolysis can generate psilocin, toxicology interpretation may need to consider whether detected psilocin reflects:

- direct psilocin exposure;
- psilocybin metabolism;
- 4-AcO-DMT metabolism.

That attribution problem is clinically and forensically important.

Routine screening panels are not designed to comprehensively identify every substituted tryptamine.

## Counterfeit and product-identity risk

A powder, capsule, gummy or liquid sold as 4-AcO-DMT should be considered chemically uncertain unless tested.

Possible problems include:

- another 4-substituted tryptamine;
- psilocin/psilocybin-related material;
- multiple psychedelics;
- stimulant or sedative admixtures;
- inaccurate concentration.

Subjective similarity cannot establish identity.

## Forensic interpretation

Analytical confirmation answers **what was present**, not automatically what caused every symptom.

If psilocin is detected, the original exposure may still be ambiguous because several compounds can generate psilocin or contain it directly.

Interpretation should incorporate:

- parent analytes;
- metabolites;
- product testing;
- timing;
- specimen type;
- co-drugs;
- clinical circumstances.

## Epidemiology and current trends

The strongest trend evidence is drug-checking and international NPS reporting.

Those sources establish that 4-AcO-DMT has circulated broadly enough to warrant surveillance, but they do not provide a denominator-based estimate of how often it is used or how often severe toxicity occurs.

## Legal and regulatory status — dated October 3, 2026

### International

UNODC lists 4-AcO-DMT among tryptamine NPS.

UNODC states that **DMT, etryptamine, DET, psilocin and psilocybin** are the tryptamines controlled under Schedule I of the 1971 Convention. 4-AcO-DMT is not in that listed group.

### United States federal law

The current federal Schedule I list does not specifically name 4-AcO-DMT or psilacetin.

That does not guarantee legality. Federal analogue-law questions can depend on chemical relationship, intent for human consumption and case-specific facts.

### State law

Some states explicitly control it. Virginia's Schedule I list includes **acetoxydimethyltryptamine (AcO-DMT, psilacetin)**.

## Special populations

There are inadequate compound-specific data for:

- pregnancy and breastfeeding;
- adolescents;
- older adults;
- cardiovascular disease;
- seizure disorders;
- bipolar disorder;
- psychotic disorders.

Risk in those groups should not be inferred as low simply because evidence is absent.

## Myths and common misconceptions

### “4-AcO-DMT is just synthetic mushrooms.”

No. It is a distinct synthetic tryptamine with a plausible psilocin-prodrug relationship, not a mushroom preparation.

### “Because it likely becomes psilocin, all psilocybin safety data transfer directly.”

No. Metabolic similarity does not establish identical absorption, formulation, interaction, or product-quality risk.

### “People have used it for years, so the human safety profile is settled.”

No. Community experience is much larger than the controlled clinical literature.

### “Psilocin in toxicology proves psilocybin was taken.”

Not necessarily. 4-AcO-DMT can plausibly generate psilocin as well.

### “A negative routine screen rules it out.”

No. Specialized mass-spectrometry testing may be needed.

## FAQ

### Does 4-AcO-DMT turn into psilocin?

In-vitro human metabolism strongly supports hydrolysis to psilocin-related products, but controlled human PK confirmation remains limited.

### Has 4-AcO-DMT been studied in humans?

It has been detected and characterized analytically, but controlled clinical pharmacology remains sparse.

### Is it the same as psilocybin?

No. Both can plausibly lead toward psilocin-related pharmacology, but they are distinct molecules.

### Can routine testing identify it?

Not reliably. Specialized chromatographic mass spectrometry may be required.

### Is it internationally controlled?

UNODC lists it as a tryptamine NPS, but it is not among the tryptamines UNODC identifies as internationally controlled under the 1971 Convention.

## Dependence, tolerance, withdrawal and recovery

There is **no established 4-AcO-DMT-specific physical-withdrawal syndrome** in the published human literature, and prospective dependence studies are lacking.

Because 4-AcO-DMT is a serotonergic psychedelic related to psilocin, classic-psychedelic evidence is the most reasonable comparison. That literature generally does not show the kind of medically dangerous withdrawal seen with alcohol, benzodiazepines, or opioids. However, translating that class pattern to an unapproved NPS should remain explicitly cautious.

Problematic use can still occur without a stereotyped physical withdrawal syndrome. Clinically relevant patterns include repeated use despite psychiatric or social harm, escalating risk-taking, inability to cut down, or using the drug compulsively to escape distress.

Persistent anxiety, depersonalization/derealization, flashbacks, or HPPD-like perceptual symptoms are **not proof of withdrawal** and deserve separate assessment.

### Recovery and support

If use is becoming difficult to control, or if mood, anxiety, sleep, perception, work, or relationships remain impaired after stopping, substance-use or mental-health care is appropriate even when no detoxification syndrome is present. U.S. readers can use [FindTreatment.gov](https://findtreatment.gov/) or [SAMHSA treatment resources](https://www.samhsa.gov/substance-use/treatment).


## Bottom line

4-AcO-DMT has a **credible psilocin-prodrug story and solid analytical chemistry**, but the internet's familiarity with the compound dramatically exceeds its controlled human evidence base.

The responsible conclusion is narrow: it is a serotonergic psychedelic with plausible conversion to psilocin, real product-identity uncertainty, and too little human pharmacology to justify precise safety, potency or interaction claims.
