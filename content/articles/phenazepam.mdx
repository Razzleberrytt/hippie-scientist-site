# Phenazepam: Long-Acting Benzodiazepine, Prolonged Toxicity & Forensic Evidence

> **Safety note:** Phenazepam is a long-acting benzodiazepine. Combining benzodiazepines with opioids, alcohol, or other sedatives can cause profound sedation and substantially increase overdose risk. Abrupt cessation after dependence can cause severe withdrawal, including seizures.

## Quick answer

**Phenazepam is a long-acting 1,4-benzodiazepine developed in the former Soviet Union.** It has legitimate medical history in Russia and neighboring countries but later became an internet-sold drug and research-chemical concern in Western Europe and North America.

Unlike some newer RC benzos, phenazepam has substantial forensic case literature.

## Prolonged intoxication

A UK case described analytically confirmed phenazepam toxicity with confusion and disorientation lasting roughly **60 hours**.

That prolonged course matters because impairment can continue far beyond the period a person expected.

## Impaired driving

A published impaired-driving case described slurred speech and profound psychomotor impairment with phenazepam detected in blood and no other drugs identified.

This is a useful real-world reminder that subjective wakefulness does not equal safe driving performance.

## Fatality evidence

Phenazepam has appeared in fatal investigations, particularly in mixed-drug exposures. Reviews of UK cases documented serious adverse events and deaths associated with nonmedical use.

Postmortem detection alone does not prove that phenazepam was the sole cause, but it places the compound firmly in clinically important toxicology rather than internet speculation.

## Why bulk powder was especially risky

Phenazepam was historically sold online as bulk crystalline powder.

For a potent drug, uncertain concentration or inaccurate measurement can turn a small error into a much larger exposure. That market context contributed to accidental intoxications and prolonged blackouts.

## Dependence and withdrawal

Repeated phenazepam exposure can produce tolerance and physical dependence.

Long-acting benzodiazepines can produce delayed withdrawal, including:

- severe anxiety;
- insomnia;
- tremor;
- confusion;
- perceptual disturbance;
- seizures.

A delayed onset can falsely suggest that dependence is mild during the first day or two after stopping.

## Reddit signal

Phenazepam remains a reference point in older RC-benzo discussions because of its unusually long duration and history as an internet-sold powder.

Those accounts are useful for understanding why the drug gained notoriety, but they do not establish a safe dose or a reliable equivalence to another benzodiazepine.

## Why long duration magnifies ordinary benzodiazepine risks

Phenazepam has a longer clinical and forensic history than many newer RC benzodiazepines, which gives the evidence base more depth. Its prolonged action, however, creates a special risk of accumulation and delayed impairment. A person can feel partly recovered while clinically relevant drug remains in the body.

### Dependence and withdrawal

Repeated phenazepam exposure can produce tolerance and physical dependence. Because elimination may be prolonged, withdrawal can be delayed after the last use. Severe benzodiazepine withdrawal may include marked anxiety, insomnia, tremor, confusion, perceptual disturbance, and seizures.

That does not justify a universal day-by-day phenazepam withdrawal schedule. Duration and severity depend on exposure history, co-use, individual metabolism, and the actual product involved.

### Impairment and mixed depressants

Phenazepam can impair coordination, memory, judgment, and driving ability. Alcohol, opioids, sedative-hypnotics, and other benzodiazepines can add to CNS depression. When more than one depressant is present, the resulting risk cannot be estimated reliably from a simple “equivalent dose” calculation.

### Testing and forensic interpretation

Phenazepam and metabolites can be measured with chromatographic mass-spectrometric methods, but toxicology still requires context. A concentration does not automatically reveal the degree of impairment at an earlier time, especially when tolerance or long elimination is involved. In deaths, co-detected drugs and medical circumstances remain essential to causal interpretation.

Phenazepam therefore belongs in a different evidentiary category from newly detected RC benzos: human experience is more substantial, yet unregulated products, long persistence, dependence, and polysubstance exposure still make simplistic potency comparisons unsafe.

## Bottom line

Phenazepam is not just an obscure historical RC. It has documented prolonged intoxication, impaired driving, and serious forensic cases. Its long duration makes accumulation and delayed withdrawal central safety concerns.
