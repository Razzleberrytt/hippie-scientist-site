# 5-MeO-MiPT (Moxy): Masterclass Monograph

> **Emergency safety:** Seizure, severe overheating, chest pain, collapse, major breathing abnormalities, persistent loss of consciousness, severe agitation/aggression, or rapidly worsening confusion require urgent medical assessment. If the product identity is uncertain or symptoms are atypical, substituted or mixed drugs should be considered.

## Quick answer

**5-MeO-MiPT, commonly called Moxy, is a synthetic 5-methoxy tryptamine psychedelic.**

Its pharmacology includes **5-HT2A receptor agonism**, and preclinical work shows broader serotonin-receptor activity and cardiorespiratory effects. Human evidence, however, is sparse and dominated by intoxication/forensic cases rather than controlled clinical trials.

One important 2018 forensic study described an agitated, aggressive man found naked in public. 5-MeO-MiPT was analytically confirmed in blood and urine, and seized powders contained the compound. However, cocaine, cocaethylene, methylphenidate and metabolites were also detected, so the case cannot be treated as a pure 5-MeO-MiPT toxidrome.

The same study, plus newer 2024 work, mapped multiple human-relevant metabolites. That makes the **analytical and forensic evidence considerably stronger than the clinical dose-response evidence**.

## History and market emergence

5-MeO-MiPT emerged in the broader market for synthetic tryptamine NPS and has appeared in forensic casework in Europe, North America and elsewhere.

UNODC includes 5-MeO-MiPT within the international tryptamine NPS landscape.

Its online reputation often emphasizes a distinctive mix of psychedelic, stimulating and tactile/sexual effects. Those descriptions may help explain search demand, but they are **community observations, not safety endpoints**.

## Chemistry and relationship to other tryptamines

5-MeO-MiPT is a **5-methoxy tryptamine** with methyl and isopropyl substitution on the terminal amine.

Small structural changes among tryptamines can alter:

- receptor selectivity;
- transporter activity;
- metabolism;
- duration;
- cardiovascular effects;
- interaction risk.

That means comparisons with 5-MeO-DMT, 5-MeO-DiPT, DMT, psilocin or LSD can be useful mechanistically but should not become equivalence claims.

## Pharmacodynamics

### Serotonin receptors

In-vitro comparative work shows that 5-MeO-MiPT interacts with **5-HT2A receptors** and acts as an agonist.

Newer structural work on 5-methoxytryptamines also reinforces the importance of both **5-HT2A and 5-HT1A** signaling across this chemical family.

### Transporter and broader monoamine effects

Comparative receptor/transporter profiling suggests that novel tryptamines can differ meaningfully in transporter activity and receptor efficacy.

These laboratory findings support plausible mechanisms for psychedelic effects, stimulation, autonomic activation and interaction risk.

They do not establish a human safe dose or potency ranking.

## Metabolism

A 2018 study using pooled human liver microsomes plus authentic blood and urine from a real intoxication case identified multiple phase-I metabolites.

Important pathways included:

- N-demethylation;
- hydroxylation;
- N-oxidation.

Proposed useful biomarkers included:

- 5-MeO-NiPT;
- 5-OH-MiPT;
- 5-MeO-MiPT-N-oxide;
- hydroxylated 5-MeO-MiPT species.

A 2024 human-liver-microsome study expanded the proposed pathway map, including N-dealkylation, O-demethylation, N-oxidation and conjugated metabolites.

This is useful forensic science, not a substitute for controlled human pharmacokinetics.

## Human intoxication evidence

### 2016 forensic intoxication case

Police encountered a man who was naked, agitated and behaving aggressively.

5-MeO-MiPT was identified in blood, urine and seized material.

However, the urine also contained:

- cocaine;
- cocaethylene;
- methylphenidate;
- ritalinic acid.

The case therefore proves **real 5-MeO-MiPT exposure and provides human metabolism data**, but it cannot isolate every symptom to 5-MeO-MiPT.

### Combined methylone + 5-MeO-MiPT intoxication

An earlier published case described combined exposure to methylone and 5-MeO-MiPT.

Again, this supports real-world toxicity and interaction concern while limiting single-drug attribution.

## Preclinical toxicology

A 2024 study linked a human poisoning case with systematic mouse toxicology.

The animal work found dose-dependent changes in:

- sensorimotor responses;
- prepulse inhibition;
- stimulated motor activity;
- thermoregulation;
- cardiorespiratory parameters.

These findings support biologic plausibility for human neurologic and cardiovascular toxicity, but **animal exposure levels cannot be converted into human recreational thresholds**.

## Acute toxicity and emergency signs

Plausible and documented concerns include:

- severe agitation;
- behavioral disorganization;
- tachycardia or blood-pressure changes;
- hyperthermia;
- tremor;
- confusion;
- seizure;
- collapse.

A psychedelic label should not cause objective medical danger signs to be dismissed as “just a bad trip.”

## Interactions and polysubstance risk

### MAO inhibitors

There are no controlled 5-MeO-MiPT/MAOI interaction trials. Because 5-methoxy tryptamines strongly engage serotonin systems, MAOI combinations should be treated as high-uncertainty and potentially dangerous.

### Serotonergic stimulants and entactogens

MDMA-like compounds, stimulant cathinones and other serotonergic drugs may increase autonomic and serotonergic stress.

### Other psychedelics

Stacking serotonergic psychedelics makes both psychological and physiologic response harder to predict.

### Alcohol and depressants

Depressants can impair judgment and obscure deterioration without removing stimulant/serotonergic toxicity.

## Tolerance

Controlled repeated-dose human tolerance studies are lacking.

Serotonergic psychedelic tolerance is biologically plausible through receptor adaptation, but 5-MeO-MiPT-specific magnitude and time course are not established.

No tolerance discussion should be used as a rationale for increasing exposure.

## Dependence and withdrawal

A classic physical-dependence syndrome has not been established for 5-MeO-MiPT.

There is no validated withdrawal timeline or taper protocol.

Repeated problematic use can still occur without physical withdrawal. Warning signs include:

- inability to cut down;
- escalating frequency;
- use despite psychiatric or cardiovascular symptoms;
- repeated use in unsafe settings;
- interference with work, relationships or sleep.

## Treatment and support

There is no 5-MeO-MiPT-specific antidote.

Acute care is syndrome-based: safety, airway/breathing assessment, cardiovascular and temperature monitoring, treatment of severe agitation or seizures, and investigation for co-drugs.

Persistent psychosis, mania, severe anxiety, derealization, perceptual disturbance or functional decline after exposure deserves professional evaluation.

U.S. readers can use **FindTreatment.gov** or the **SAMHSA National Helpline (1-800-662-HELP)** for substance-use support.

## Drug checking, toxicology and detection

5-MeO-MiPT and its metabolites can be identified using:

- LC-HRMS/MS;
- GC-MS;
- FTIR and NMR for seized material;
- targeted mass-spectrometry workflows.

Routine hospital screens are not designed to comprehensively identify 5-MeO-MiPT.

Metabolite-aware methods improve confidence when parent concentrations are low or timing is uncertain.

## Counterfeit and product-identity risk

A powder, capsule, tablet or liquid marketed as “Moxy” does not establish chemical identity.

The product may contain:

- another tryptamine;
- multiple psychedelics;
- stimulants;
- unexpected depressants;
- inaccurate concentration.

Subjective effects cannot confirm identity.

## Forensic interpretation

A positive 5-MeO-MiPT result proves exposure.

It does not by itself establish:

- that 5-MeO-MiPT caused every symptom;
- the amount consumed;
- the exact time of exposure;
- an impairment threshold;
- a lethal concentration.

The 2018 case is particularly instructive because multiple stimulants were co-detected.

## Epidemiology and current trend

UNODC reporting confirms that 5-MeO-MiPT has circulated internationally as a tryptamine NPS.

The available surveillance does not provide a reliable denominator for prevalence or severe-toxicity rates.

The strongest current conclusion is that 5-MeO-MiPT is an **established but relatively uncommon NPS with real forensic and poisoning evidence**.

## Legal and regulatory status — dated October 3, 2026

### International

UNODC's tryptamine overview states that the internationally controlled tryptamines under the 1971 Convention include DMT, etryptamine, DET, psilocin and psilocybin.

5-MeO-MiPT is not in that listed internationally controlled group.

### United States federal law

The current federal Schedule I list does not specifically name 5-MeO-MiPT.

That does not guarantee legality; federal analogue-law analysis can depend on chemical similarity, intended use and case facts.

### State law

Virginia specifically lists **5-methoxy-N,N-methylisopropyltryptamine (5-MeO-MiPT)** in Schedule I.

Other jurisdictions differ.

## Special populations

There are inadequate compound-specific data for:

- pregnancy and breastfeeding;
- adolescents;
- older adults;
- cardiovascular disease;
- seizure disorders;
- bipolar disorder;
- psychotic disorders.

Preclinical cardiorespiratory findings and serotonergic pharmacology support extra caution in vulnerable groups, but exact risk magnitude is unknown.

## Myths and common misconceptions

### “Moxy is a functional or gentle psychedelic.”

Those are subjective descriptors, not toxicology findings.

### “A psychedelic cannot cause dangerous cardiovascular effects.”

Preclinical 5-MeO-MiPT work found cardiorespiratory changes, and real intoxications include severe behavioral toxicity.

### “One poisoning case proves 5-MeO-MiPT alone caused everything.”

No. Important co-drugs were present.

### “No withdrawal syndrome means it cannot be misused.”

No. Problematic or compulsive use can occur without classic physical withdrawal.

### “A negative routine screen rules it out.”

No. Specialized analytical methods may be needed.

## FAQ

### What is Moxy?

Moxy is the common name for **5-MeO-MiPT**, a synthetic 5-methoxy tryptamine psychedelic.

### Has it caused human intoxication?

Yes. Published intoxication cases exist, although some involved additional substances.

### How is it detected?

Mass-spectrometry methods can identify parent 5-MeO-MiPT and characteristic metabolites in blood and urine.

### Does it cause physical withdrawal?

A specific physical-withdrawal syndrome has not been established.

### Is it internationally controlled?

It is not among the tryptamines UNODC lists as internationally controlled under the 1971 Convention.

### Is it federally Schedule I in the U.S.?

It is not specifically named in the current federal Schedule I list, but federal analogue law and state controls may still apply. Virginia specifically schedules it.

## Bottom line

5-MeO-MiPT has enough evidence to reject the idea that it is merely an internet nickname with no toxicology behind it.

There are **real human intoxication cases, real-case metabolism data, receptor pharmacology and preclinical cardiorespiratory findings**. What remains missing is the broad controlled human evidence needed for precise safety, potency, interaction or long-term claims.

That gap should stay visible.
