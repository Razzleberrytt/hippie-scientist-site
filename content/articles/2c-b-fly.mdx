# 2C-B-FLY

## Quick answer

**2C-B-FLY is a FLY-series psychedelic phenethylamine related to 2C-B, but its direct human safety evidence is extremely limited.**

Laboratory studies show strong serotonergic psychedelic pharmacology, while modern U.S. forensic monitoring has only recently confirmed it in drug material.

## What is actually known?

CFSRE reported its first identification of 2C-B-FLY in a U.S. drug material in February 2025.

At the time of the June 2025 monograph:

- one drug material had been confirmed;
- the sample came from California;
- PCP was also present;
- CFSRE had not detected 2C-B-FLY in toxicology cases.

That is important because online familiarity can make a compound look better studied than it really is.

## Pharmacology

Preclinical research shows that 2C-B-FLY has potent activity at serotonin 5-HT2 receptors and hallucinogen-like effects in animal models.

That supports psychedelic activity.

It does not establish a human safety margin.

## The 2009 Bromo-DragonFLY mislabeling lesson

A notorious 2009 incident involved a product sold as “2C-B-FLY” that was actually **Bromo-DragonFLY**.

Deaths from that incident should not be cited as confirmed 2C-B-FLY fatalities.

The real lesson is product identity: two similarly named compounds can have dramatically different pharmacology and potency.

## Evidence gaps

Direct human data remain weak for:

- toxic concentrations;
- cardiovascular risk;
- interaction risk;
- duration;
- dependence;
- rare severe outcomes.

## Bottom line

2C-B-FLY is a useful harm-reduction page precisely because the market name is familiar while the human evidence is thin.

It should be presented as an under-characterized psychedelic with a major historical mislabeling lesson—not as a proven “safer 2C-B.”
