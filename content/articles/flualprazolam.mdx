# Flualprazolam: Counterfeit Xanax, Intoxication, Toxicology & Overdose Evidence

> **Safety note:** Flualprazolam is a potent unapproved benzodiazepine-type depressant. Counterfeit tablets can contain unexpected compounds or concentrations, and combining benzodiazepines with opioids or alcohol sharply increases overdose risk.

## Quick answer

**Flualprazolam is a fluorinated analogue of alprazolam** that emerged as a designer benzodiazepine and has repeatedly been identified in counterfeit “Xanax,” emergency intoxications, and forensic toxicology.

Human evidence is better than for many RC benzos because it includes an adolescent intoxication outbreak, metabolism studies using human specimens, and multiple postmortem case series.

The evidence still does not establish a safe recreational dose or a reliable alprazolam-equivalence ratio.

## Counterfeit Xanax outbreak

A 2020 Pediatrics report described six adolescents who presented to emergency departments after taking illegally obtained tablets believed to be alprazolam.

Clinical effects included:

- sedation;
- slurred speech;
- confusion;
- mild respiratory depression.

Testing confirmed flualprazolam in biological samples and tablet material from several patients.

That outbreak matters because it demonstrates a real-world product problem: **the name or imprint on a tablet did not reliably identify the active drug.**

## Metabolism and detection

A 2020 analytical toxicology study characterized flualprazolam metabolism using in-vitro systems and human biosamples from an intoxicated patient.

The research identified metabolic pathways and urinary products relevant to toxicology laboratories.

This matters clinically because routine benzodiazepine screens can be incomplete. A suspected benzodiazepine exposure may require targeted mass-spectrometric testing to identify a designer compound.

## Fatalities and polysubstance exposure

A case series quantified flualprazolam in nine deaths. All involved more than one drug, preventing a simple “fatal concentration” threshold.

Another report described a fatal multidrug intoxication in which flualprazolam was considered a likely contributor to central nervous system and respiratory depression.

The consistent pattern is **polysubstance risk**, not a clean single-drug toxicology curve.

## Effects

Expected benzodiazepine-like effects include:

- sedation;
- reduced anxiety;
- impaired coordination;
- slowed reaction time;
- slurred speech;
- amnesia;
- disinhibition;
- loss of consciousness.

These effects become much more dangerous when another depressant is present.

## Opioids and alcohol

Flualprazolam can intensify sedation and impaired arousal from:

- fentanyl;
- heroin;
- prescription opioids;
- methadone;
- alcohol;
- other benzodiazepines;
- sedative-hypnotics.

The risk is not just “feeling more sedated.” A heavily sedated person may not wake enough to protect their airway or respond to opioid-induced respiratory depression.

## Dependence and withdrawal

Repeated flualprazolam exposure can produce benzodiazepine-type tolerance and physical dependence.

Abrupt cessation after dependence can cause:

- severe anxiety;
- insomnia;
- tremor;
- agitation;
- perceptual disturbances;
- seizures.

Because illicit tablets may contain unknown amounts, self-directed taper calculations are especially uncertain.

## U.S. legal status

DEA permanently placed flualprazolam in **Schedule I**, effective **April 1, 2026**, together with clonazolam, diclazepam, etizolam, and flubromazolam.

## What Reddit gets right—and wrong

Online reports repeatedly describe flualprazolam as highly sedating, amnestic, and difficult to compare cleanly with alprazolam.

Those themes are consistent with benzodiazepine pharmacology.

But a forum statement that one milligram of one drug “equals” a certain amount of another cannot be validated when the product concentration, purity, tolerance, and co-exposures are unknown.

## Bottom line

Flualprazolam is one of the better-documented designer benzodiazepines.

It has been confirmed in counterfeit alprazolam tablets, adolescent intoxications, fatal polysubstance cases, and analytical toxicology research.

The evidence supports treating it as a **high-risk unregulated benzodiazepine-type depressant**, particularly in counterfeit-pill and opioid-use contexts.
