# Flunitrazolam: Complete Evidence, Metabolism, Detection, Dependence & Safety Monograph

> **Emergency safety:** A person who cannot be awakened, has slow or irregular breathing, blue/gray lips, choking or gurgling while unresponsive, a seizure, collapse, or severe confusion after an unknown sedative needs emergency care. Alcohol, opioids and other sedatives can greatly increase benzodiazepine-type CNS depression. Naloxone does not reverse flunitrazolam, but it should be given when opioid exposure is possible because counterfeit or mixed products can contain opioids.

## Quick answer

**Flunitrazolam is a nitro-substituted triazolobenzodiazepine sold in designer-drug markets.** It is chemically distinct from flunitrazepam despite the similar name.

Its evidence base has an unusual shape: laboratories have published **direct controlled human analytical studies** showing that flunitrazolam can be measured in oral fluid and urine after a single verified exposure, and metabolism has been characterized further in human liver microsomes. Yet molecule-specific clinical outcome evidence remains extremely sparse.

That distinction matters. Researchers can answer “what is this molecule and how can we detect it?” much more confidently than “what dose causes blackout, coma, dependence, or death?”

## Identity

**Canonical name:** Flunitrazolam  
**Class:** triazolobenzodiazepine / designer benzodiazepine  
**Molecular formula:** C17H12FN5O2  
**Key structural features:** triazolo ring, ortho-fluorophenyl group, nitro substitution  
**Approved medical use:** none established

UNODC classifies flunitrazolam within the benzodiazepine NPS group and the sedative/hypnotic effect group.

### Flunitrazolam is not flunitrazepam

The names are easy to confuse.

Flunitrazepam is an established pharmaceutical benzodiazepine in some jurisdictions. Flunitrazolam is a different triazolobenzodiazepine NPS.

Structural resemblance can support a benzodiazepine-type mechanistic framework, but it does not create a validated clinical dose conversion or guarantee similar kinetics.

## History and market emergence

Flunitrazolam appeared during the expansion of designer benzodiazepines in European internet markets in the 2010s.

UNODC records it as an NPS benzodiazepine, and Sweden placed it under national control during 2016-2017. UNODC has also documented flunitrazolam in regional synthetic-drug surveillance.

This history establishes real market circulation, but seizure or market detection does not by itself quantify human toxicity.

## Pharmacodynamics

Modern controlled receptor studies specifically tying flunitrazolam concentrations to human effects are limited.

Its triazolobenzodiazepine structure supports action at the **benzodiazepine site of GABA-A receptors**, where drugs in this class act as positive allosteric modulators of inhibitory GABA signaling.

Expected class effects include:
- sedation;
- anxiolysis;
- amnesia;
- psychomotor slowing;
- impaired coordination;
- muscle relaxation;
- anticonvulsant effects.

These are class-supported expectations, not measured incidence rates for flunitrazolam.

## Controlled human analytical evidence

Two publications used a **controlled single administration** to characterize flunitrazolam analytically in a human volunteer.

One focused on oral fluid. Another followed urinary excretion and metabolism.

These studies are unusually valuable because they prove:
- the compound was actually administered;
- sampling time was known;
- analytical identity was controlled;
- parent/metabolite patterns could be observed.

They were **not** designed to establish a therapeutic dose, recreational safety margin, impairment threshold, or population half-life.

This page therefore does not reproduce the administered amount as consumer guidance.

## Metabolism and urinary markers

The urine study found parent flunitrazolam and several products consistent with nitrobenzodiazepine metabolism.

Reported human urinary findings included:
- 7-amino-flunitrazolam;
- 7-acetamido-flunitrazolam;
- a desnitro product.

Human liver microsome work separately identified pathways including:
- monohydroxylation;
- dihydroxylation;
- nitro reduction.

Different experimental systems can emphasize different metabolites. That is why toxicology panels benefit from multiple parent/metabolite targets rather than relying on one marker.

## Oral-fluid detection

The controlled oral-fluid study demonstrated that flunitrazolam can be measured in oral fluid using sensitive chromatographic mass-spectrometric methods.

Oral fluid can be useful for documenting relatively recent exposure, but one single-volunteer study cannot define a universal detection window.

Detection time also should not be confused with duration of impairment.

## What human evidence does **not** establish

The published controlled analytical studies do not establish:
- a safe recreational dose;
- a therapeutic dose;
- a population elimination half-life;
- a validated flunitrazepam equivalence;
- a concentration that predicts coma;
- a concentration that predicts fatality;
- how quickly dependence develops;
- a withdrawal timeline.

Those remain major gaps.

## Acute toxicity

Based on benzodiazepine pharmacology, expected toxicity includes:
- somnolence;
- slurred speech;
- ataxia;
- impaired memory;
- confusion;
- reduced consciousness.

The 2021 designer-benzodiazepine toxicology review did **not** identify molecule-specific poisoning, impaired-driving or fatality reports for flunitrazolam in the literature it reviewed.

That finding should be interpreted carefully: **lack of published cases is lack of evidence, not evidence of low toxicity**.

## Interactions and polysubstance risk

### Opioids
Benzodiazepine-type drugs and opioids can produce additive CNS and respiratory depression.

### Alcohol
Alcohol can magnify sedation, amnesia, impaired judgment and loss of coordination.

### Other sedatives
Other benzodiazepines, Z-drugs, gabapentinoids and sedating medications can compound impairment.

### Stimulants
A stimulant does not reliably “cancel out” a benzodiazepine. Mixed pharmacology can mask subjective sedation while leaving impaired judgment and adding cardiovascular stress.

## Tolerance

Repeated benzodiazepine-site exposure can produce tolerance, although tolerance does not necessarily develop equally to memory impairment, psychomotor impairment and respiratory interaction risk.

There are no prospective flunitrazolam-specific tolerance studies.

## Physical dependence

Physical dependence is biologically plausible and expected with repeated benzodiazepine-type exposure.

Physical dependence means the nervous system has adapted such that withdrawal can occur when exposure falls.

It is not the same thing as addiction or a benzodiazepine use disorder.

## Withdrawal

A flunitrazolam-specific withdrawal timeline is **not established**.

Benzodiazepine-class withdrawal can include:
- rebound anxiety;
- insomnia;
- tremor;
- autonomic activation;
- perceptual disturbance;
- confusion;
- seizures;
- delirium.

The 2025 multisociety benzodiazepine tapering guideline advises against abrupt discontinuation in people likely to be physically dependent.

That safety principle can be applied at the class level without pretending that a flunitrazolam-specific taper or conversion has been validated.

## Addiction / benzodiazepine use disorder

A broader use disorder may involve:
- inability to reduce use;
- craving;
- repeated blackouts;
- hazardous use;
- escalating exposure;
- continued use despite work, relationship or health harm;
- substantial time spent obtaining or recovering from the drug.

Tolerance or withdrawal alone does not establish a use disorder.

## Treatment and support

Acute intoxication management is primarily supportive:
- airway and breathing assessment;
- circulation;
- mental status;
- trauma evaluation;
- identification of co-exposures.

Flumazenil is a medical antidote used selectively in specific circumstances, not a universal community reversal agent, because it can precipitate seizures or withdrawal in susceptible patients.

For regular exposure with likely dependence, medical assessment is safer than abrupt cessation or an internet-derived conversion.

U.S. readers can use **FindTreatment.gov** and SAMHSA treatment resources.

## Drug testing and detection

Routine benzodiazepine immunoassays are not designed to identify every designer analogue.

Definitive testing may use:
- LC-MS/MS;
- LC-HRMS;
- parent flunitrazolam;
- nitro-reduced/acetylated or hydroxylated metabolites.

A negative routine benzodiazepine screen therefore does not reliably exclude flunitrazolam.

## Counterfeit and product-identity risk

A research-chemical label does not prove:
- identity;
- concentration;
- purity;
- absence of another benzodiazepine;
- absence of an opioid.

This matters especially when online potency discussions assume that every product labeled “flunitrazolam” contains the same substance at a known concentration.

## Forensic interpretation

A positive result establishes exposure, not automatically:
- the dose;
- the time of impairment;
- degree of impairment;
- sole causation of a death or event.

Interpretation should include specimen type, timing, co-drugs, tolerance and analytical coverage.

Because the molecule-specific clinical dataset is sparse, forensic language should be especially conservative.

## Epidemiology and current trends

UNODC continues to catalogue flunitrazolam as a benzodiazepine NPS, and regional reports have documented market detections.

For example, UNODC's 2025 East and Southeast Asia report identified flunitrazolam among designer benzodiazepines detected in Singapore in 2023.

Those are regional surveillance findings, not global prevalence estimates.

## Legal and regulatory history

**Sweden:** UNODC reports that flunitrazolam was placed under Swedish national control during 2016-2017.

**United States:** This October 3, 2026 review did not identify a specific DEA final scheduling rule naming flunitrazolam in the federal materials reviewed. That should not be treated as a guarantee of legality; state law and federal analogue provisions can differ.

**International:** UNODC catalogs flunitrazolam as an NPS. This review did not identify an individual CND decision placing it under the 1971 Convention in the reviewed current international-control materials.

## Special populations

Direct flunitrazolam data are not established for:
- pregnancy/breastfeeding;
- adolescents;
- older adults;
- liver/kidney impairment;
- chronic respiratory disease.

Benzodiazepine-class concerns still apply, including fetal/neonatal exposure, fall/cognitive risk in older adults and higher respiratory danger with opioids or pulmonary disease.

## Myths versus evidence

### “Flunitrazolam is basically flunitrazepam.”
**False.** They are distinct molecules and have no validated dose conversion.

### “A human study means a safe dose is known.”
**False.** The controlled studies were designed for analytical detection, not safety or efficacy.

### “No published deaths means it is safer than other RC benzos.”
**Unsupported.** Sparse case reporting cannot establish comparative safety.

### “If a routine benzodiazepine screen is negative, flunitrazolam was not present.”
**False.** Designer-benzodiazepine detection depends on assay coverage and confirmatory methods.

### “Dependence requires addiction.”
**False.** Physical dependence can occur without a substance use disorder.

## Major evidence gaps

Priority gaps include:
- controlled human pharmacodynamics;
- population pharmacokinetics;
- impairment and driving studies;
- prospective acute-intoxication cohorts;
- postmortem case series;
- dependence incidence;
- withdrawal timing and severity;
- pregnancy/organ-impairment data;
- current product-strength distributions.

## Bottom line

Flunitrazolam is a strong example of **analytical knowledge outrunning clinical knowledge**.

Scientists have directly characterized its detection and metabolism in a human volunteer and in laboratory systems. What they have *not* established is a safe dose, reliable potency equivalence, toxic threshold, typical withdrawal course, or comparative fatality risk.

The evidence-based safety frame is therefore **benzodiazepine-class risk + direct analytical evidence + severe uncertainty about molecule-specific clinical outcomes + unregulated product identity**.
