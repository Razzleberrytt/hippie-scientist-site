# Brorphine

> **Overdose warning:** Brorphine is a potent opioid. Unresponsiveness or slow/abnormal breathing is an emergency; call 911 and administer naloxone if available.

## Quick answer

**Brorphine is a potent novel synthetic opioid and mu-opioid receptor agonist.** It appeared in U.S. forensic casework around 2019–2020 as the illicit opioid market shifted away from earlier compounds.

## What forensic cases show

A 2021 series confirmed brorphine in 20 authentic cases. Fentanyl was present in every case and flualprazolam in most.

That is a powerful market signal: brorphine often appeared in already dangerous depressant mixtures.

It is not evidence that brorphine alone produced every death in that series.

## Pharmacology

Laboratory studies confirm potent mu-opioid receptor agonism. Later review literature describes preclinical respiratory depression and abuse liability consistent with that mechanism.

## Why co-detection matters

Combining potent opioids with fentanyl and benzodiazepines can make sedation and respiratory depression harder to recognize and reverse.

Users may not know the additional opioid is present.

## Detection

Brorphine may fall outside standard toxicology panels. Targeted LC-MS methods and updated NPS libraries are important in forensic and clinical confirmation.

## Bottom line

Brorphine illustrates how quickly one novel opioid can replace another in an illicit market. Its major practical hazard is not just receptor potency—it is hidden exposure inside a polysubstance supply.
