> **Bottom line:** Gastrodia elata, also called **Tianma**, now has a meaningful modern human cognition trial. The 2025 randomized study is encouraging, but it is still one 12-week, preparation-specific trial. That supports a **preliminary human evidence** label—not a dementia-treatment claim.

## The 2025 trial

Researchers randomized 100 adults aged 40 to 65 with normal or mildly impaired cognition to Gastrodia elata extract or placebo for 12 weeks.[1]

The trial was double-blind and placebo-controlled. The primary outcome used the Alzheimer's Disease Assessment Scale-cognitive subscale (ADAS-cog), while secondary outcomes included K-MMSE, BDNF, amyloid-beta, and total antioxidant status.

After 12 weeks, the Gastrodia group improved more on the ADAS-cog total score than placebo. The reported mean change was about -2.15 points with Gastrodia versus -0.92 with placebo.[1]

K-MMSE, BDNF, and total antioxidant status also favored the extract. Amyloid-beta did **not** significantly improve.

## Why this is meaningful

A positive primary cognitive outcome deserves more weight than a study that only reports scattered biomarker or post-hoc signals.

This moves Gastrodia beyond a purely mechanistic or animal-research story.

## Why it is still preliminary

One randomized trial is the start of a clinical evidence base, not the finish.

The study lasted only 12 weeks, tested one preparation, and enrolled a specific middle-aged population. It does not establish that every Tianma product is equivalent, that the effect persists long term, or that Gastrodia treats Alzheimer's disease or dementia.

Independent replication would substantially strengthen the case.

## Biomarkers do not prove the mechanism

The BDNF and antioxidant-status findings are useful clues, but they do not prove why the cognitive score changed.

Clinical outcomes and biomarkers should be kept separate rather than turned into a neat cause-and-effect story.

## Safety context

No serious adverse events were reported during the 12-week trial.[1] That is useful short-term information, but it cannot define rare adverse effects, long-term use, pregnancy safety, or every medication interaction.

Botanical authentication and extract standardization are especially important because a named herb is not one chemically identical product.

## Verdict

Gastrodia elata has crossed an important threshold: there is now a modern randomized human cognition signal worth following.

For now, **preliminary human evidence** is the most accurate description. Replication in independent populations and with clearly characterized preparations is the next step that would meaningfully increase confidence.
