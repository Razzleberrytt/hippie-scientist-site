# Research enrichment Waves 626–650 — 2026-10-03

Sixth pass of the Waves 501–1000 program.

## Waves 626–650
1. **creatine-beta-alanine — PMID 40647180** — 2025 seven-RCT/263-participant systematic review of combined creatine+beta-alanine; high-intensity performance signals are combination-specific and evidence is predominantly male.
2. **creatine-beta-alanine — PMID 42384726** — 2026 52-RCT network meta-analysis; creatine alone had the most consistent high-intensity performance effects while beta-alanine was task-specific and combined supplementation showed no added synergy.
3. **caffeine-nitrate — PMID 39408319** — Six-study/95-participant meta-analysis found caffeine+nitrate co-supplementation did not outperform caffeine or nitrate alone for time trials, heart rate or oxygen uptake.
4. **conditioning-supplements — PMID 41323837** — 2025 network meta-analysis of 30 RCTs/693 athletes comparing protein, creatine, beta-alanine, HMB, vitamin D and nitrate across energy-system outcomes; indirect rankings remain outcome-specific.
5. **dietary-nitrate — PMID 40085422** — 2025 umbrella review of 20 meta-analyses/180 primary studies/2,672 participants; benefits were domain-specific, several performance outcomes were null, and most underlying reviews were low/critically-low quality.
6. **probiotics — PMID 41291638** — 2025 meta-analysis of 34 RCTs/2,390 adults on cognitive outcomes and adverse events; cognition evidence remains strain-, population-, test-, and duration-specific.
7. **athlete-supplements-network — PMID 40717998** — 2025 network meta-analysis of 35 RCTs/991 athletes; supplement effects on strength, sprint, jump and muscle mass differed by outcome, with most trials having some risk-of-bias concerns.
8. **elite-athlete-supplements — PMID 41058995** — 2025 review of 46 studies/928 elite athletes; caffeine showed more consistent sport-performance effects than nitrate, while beta-alanine and recovery supplements were sport/context dependent.
9. **cycling-ergogenic-aids — PMID 38892701** — 2024 systematic review of 36 cycling studies/701 participants spanning caffeine, creatine, bicarbonate, beta-alanine, nitrate and combinations; sport- and intervention-specific evidence only.
10. **ergogenic-combinations — PMID 40619880** — 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis of 16 randomized studies testing performance-enhancing supplement combinations; combination effects must not be attributed to individual ingredients.
11. **probiotics — PMID 40740336** — 2025 six-study/424-patient insomnia meta-analysis; PSQI improved with moderate certainty while depression-score evidence was very-low certainty.
12. **probiotics — PMID 40966579** — 2025 umbrella review of probiotic meta-analyses for cognitive-related outcomes; strength/quality of evidence varies substantially by population and endpoint.
13. **probiotics — PMID 41958906** — 2026 13-study/890-participant adult sleep meta-analysis; modest PSQI/ISI signals with strain/product and baseline-sleep heterogeneity.
14. **vitamin-d — PMID 37418225** — 24-RCT/7,557-participant cognition meta-analysis; small global-cognition effect, no consistent specific-domain effect, with stronger signals in vulnerable/deficient subgroups.
15. **creatine — PMID 41062952** — 2025 20-study/1,093-participant meta-analysis of creatine plus exercise in older adults; strength/fat-percentage signals with total-body BMD null; combination/context specific.
16. **creatine — PMID 38582412** — 2024 systematic review found brain creatine increases but cognitive effects remain equivocal, providing a counterweight to positive cognition meta-analyses.
17. **vitamin-d — PMID 34806158** — Systematic review of 20 adult RCTs found mixed cognition results and did not support a consistent cognitive-enhancement effect—important counterweight to later positive syntheses.
18. **coenzyme-q10 — PMID 36091835** — RCT meta-analysis of fatigue; fatigue-reduction signal stronger for CoQ10-only formulations than compound products, with formulation and symptom-context boundaries.
19. **protein-resistance-training — PMID 38753309** — 2024 18-RCT/1,147-participant review of protein+resistance training and gait speed in older adults; exercise-combination and aging-population context retained.
20. **whey-protein — PMID 41228181** — 2025 older-adult meta-analysis of whey protein with/without resistance training; body-composition, strength and cardiometabolic outcomes remain context- and co-intervention-specific.
21. **sodium-bicarbonate — PMID 41416636** — Systematic review/meta-analysis found negligible benefit of a single oral dose for continuous running performance, providing a sport-task-specific null.
22. **bcaa — PMID 38625669** — 2024 18-study meta-analysis; BCAA reduced selected CK and muscle-soreness timepoints after exercise-induced damage but did not improve LDH; recovery biomarker/symptom evidence only.
23. **sodium-bicarbonate — PMID 39841595** — 2025 swimming meta-analysis; bicarbonate raised blood bicarbonate/lactate/pH but did not significantly improve 200-m time—physiology-performance dissociation retained.
24. **bcaa — PMID 38241335** — 2024 overview of 11 systematic reviews; CK/muscle-soreness recovery signals with no effect on muscle-performance recovery and notable review-quality limitations.
25. **hmb — PMID 41934514** — 2026 13-RCT/561-participant meta-analysis in adults >=50 found no meaningful added benefit of HMB+resistance training for fat mass, muscle mass or strength; strong counterweight to routine-adjunct claims.
