# Research enrichment Waves 1126–1150 — 2026-10-04

Sixth pass of the Waves 1001–1500 program, emphasizing PCOS, menopause, pregnancy, thyroid nutrition, fertility, and reproductive safety.

## Waves 1126–1150
1. **pcos-comparative-metabolic-therapies — PMID 42490840** — Network meta-analysis of 16 PCOS RCTs found GLP-1 receptor agonist plus metformin most effective for weight/BMI, while inositol-containing strategies were less prominent and none of the compared interventions significantly improved HOMA-IR; comparative pharmacologic context, not an inositol efficacy endorsement.
2. **soy-isoflavones — PMID 42032055** — Meta-analysis of 13 RCTs/1,325 postmenopausal women found soy isoflavones improved vaginal dryness and urogenital symptoms, while vasomotor and psychological outcomes were nonsignificant and heterogeneity exceeded 90%; formulation/population boundaries remain explicit.
3. **vitamin-d — PMID 41901143** — Systematic review of 11 PCOS studies found deficiency common and supplementation effects on insulin sensitivity and reproductive outcomes modest/inconsistent; correction of deficiency may complement care but vitamin D was not supported as stand-alone PCOS or fertility therapy.
4. **vitamin-d — PMID 42199796** — Meta-analysis of 11 RCTs/992 reproductive-age women found no overall effect of vitamin D supplementation on anti-Müllerian hormone, with substantial heterogeneity; open-label subgroup findings did not override the double-blind null.
5. **selenium-pregnancy — PMID 41596613** — Systematic review found lower maternal selenium concentrations associated with miscarriage in observational studies, but randomized interventional evidence was absent and confounding substantial; no causal supplementation claim is authorized.
6. **selenium-pregnancy — PMID 41683301** — Systematic review of selenium exposure and congenital anomalies found mixed observational associations by anomaly type, with only one randomized trial identified; evidence is confounded and insufficient to define an optimal supplemental selenium level in pregnancy.
7. **myo-inositol-selenium — PMID 42122912** — Meta-analysis of three studies/288 Hashimoto patients with subclinical hypothyroidism found myo-inositol plus selenium lowered TSH more than selenium alone, while T3/T4/TPOAb were not significantly different and the tiny evidence base limits confidence.
8. **phytoestrogens-menopause — PMID 42500528** — Meta-analysis/trial-sequential analysis of 16 phytoestrogen RCTs found improvement in selected menopausal psychological symptoms, but effects varied by symptom and menopausal stage and some significant outcomes failed sequential correction; category-level evidence only.
9. **menopause-dietary-interventions — PMID 42790873** — Systematic review of 15 RCTs found soy-based dietary patterns may reduce hot-flash frequency/severity, while overall vasomotor and psychological effects were inconsistent and certainty very low; whole-food/dietary evidence is not equivalent to supplement-only efficacy.
10. **omega-3-pregnancy — PMID 41773213** — Meta-analysis of 23 pregnancy studies found modest increases in gestational weight gain and infant birth size plus lower preterm-birth risk with omega-3 supplementation, but sensitivity analyses weakened some outcomes and effects remained outcome-specific.
11. **dha-pregnancy — PMID 42494300** — Meta-analysis of 21 prenatal DHA RCTs found no significant benefit across measured offspring neurodevelopmental domains, with only a modest birth-weight increase among growth outcomes; direct counterweight to broad prenatal cognition claims.
12. **omega-3-pregnancy-oxylipins — PMID 42715778** — Systematic review found only two pregnancy studies linking omega-3 interventions/exposure with LC-MS oxylipin profiling, leaving mechanistic relationships with gestational length and preterm birth insufficient and hypothesis-generating.
13. **astaxanthin — PMID 41714744** — Male-infertility meta-analysis found no significant improvement in human semen parameters with astaxanthin despite strong animal-model effects; human fertility outcomes such as pregnancy were not assessed, so clinical efficacy is unsupported.
14. **vitamin-e — PMID 42165159** — Meta-analysis of five studies after varicocelectomy found no consistent additional benefit of vitamin E on sperm concentration or motility, with heterogeneous morphology/pregnancy reporting; useful null evidence against routine postoperative vitamin-E claims.
15. **male-infertility-nutraceuticals — PMID 42206761** — Meta-analysis of 41 RCTs in idiopathic male infertility found antioxidant-associated improvements in semen parameters, but prediction intervals crossed the null, heterogeneity exceeded 80%, certainty was very low, and live-birth evidence was inadequate.
16. **pcos-mineral-supplements — PMID 41580698** — Meta-analysis of 11 RCTs/618 women with PCOS pooled magnesium, chromium, zinc, selenium and chromium-picolinate interventions and found improved glycemic/selected lipid measures, but the mixed-mineral design prevents attributing effects to any single mineral.
17. **micronutrients-immune-aging — PMID 42254024** — Nine-RCT meta-analysis in older adults found small pooled immune-function improvements across diverse micronutrients, with vitamin D, vitamin E and multivitamin subgroup signals; heterogeneous multi-nutrient evidence should not be reduced to a single supplement claim.
18. **female-infertility-supplements — PMID 42356328** — Systematic review/meta-analysis found no high-certainty evidence that dietary supplements improve female infertility outcomes versus placebo; secondary signals for NAC, myo-inositol, vitamin D/E and combinations were hypothesis-generating and at higher risk of bias.
19. **iodine-pregnancy — PMID 42359139** — Meta-analysis in iodine-deficient pregnant women found iodine supplementation lowered TSH and thyroglobulin, with subgroup differences by region, trimester, regimen and iodine form; biochemical improvement does not establish universal dosing outside deficiency context.
20. **prenatal-micronutrient-status — PMID 42359342** — Systematic review of 22 studies linked maternal B12, vitamin D and folate status with child neurodevelopment, but 18 of 22 studies were observational and heterogeneity precluded quantitative synthesis; strongest causal inference remains limited.
21. **preconception-nutrition-interventions — PMID 42684922** — Systematic review/meta-analysis of 24 preconception studies found selected maternal/neonatal benefits from lifestyle and nutritional interventions, but mixed study designs, heterogeneous interventions and outcome-specific pooling constrain causal supplement claims.
22. **gdm-nutrient-supplements — PMID 41921332** — Network meta-analysis of 11 GDM RCTs ranked different supplements favorably for different birth outcomes, but no single intervention improved all maternal/newborn outcomes and several rankings were based on wide credible intervals; network rankings remain exploratory.
23. **resveratrol — PMID 42150354** — ART meta-analysis found resveratrol improved fertilization rate but did not significantly improve biochemical/clinical pregnancy, live birth, oocyte count or embryo outcomes overall; fertility-establishment and live-birth nulls remain first-class.
24. **melatonin-pcos-art — PMID 42510627** — Systematic review reported favorable ART-related signals with melatonin in PCOS, including endometrial thickness, oocyte/embryo quality and pregnancy measures, but heterogeneous observational/clinical evidence and regimen variation limit confidence; no consumer dosing inference.
25. **antioxidants-art — PMID 42558317** — Meta-analysis of oral antioxidant RCTs in IVF/ICSI reported improved pregnancy and selected laboratory outcomes plus a live-birth signal, but regimens were heterogeneous, study count limited and certainty insufficient for a single antioxidant or standardized protocol recommendation.
