# Research enrichment Waves 1076–1100 — 2026-10-04

Fourth pass of the Waves 1001–1500 deep-enrichment program, emphasizing cognition, mood, nootropic claims, and gut-brain evidence.

## Waves 1076–1100
1. **milk-fat-globule-membrane — PMID 41599955** — Meta-analysis of three adult RCTs found small reductions in stress and anxiety with bovine milk-fat-globule-membrane supplementation, while depression effects were nonsignificant; the evidence base is only three trials and remains preliminary.
2. **scfa-gut-brain-depression — PMID 42021200** — Systematic review/meta-analysis found lower circulating short-chain fatty acids in depression and six RCTs suggested probiotic/prebiotic interventions can raise fecal SCFAs alongside symptom improvement; biomarker associations do not establish a specific supplement mechanism or strain effect.
3. **mitochondrial-nutraceuticals-psychiatry — PMID 42253799** — Systematic review of 122 psychiatric nutraceutical studies found heterogeneous signals across vitamin D, NAC, acetyl-L-carnitine, CoQ10, alpha-lipoic acid, magnesium and B vitamins, with high overall risk of bias and low methodological quality; category-level evidence only.
4. **probiotics — PMID 42374951** — Thirteen-RCT/710-participant meta-analysis in major depressive disorder found a small pooled symptom benefit favoring probiotics with moderate heterogeneity; strain/formulation uncertainty prevents generic probiotic equivalence.
5. **gotu-kola-phosphatidylcholine-taurine — PMID 42391143** — Scoping review found very limited human mood evidence for gotu kola, phosphatidylcholine and taurine, with most supporting data preclinical and no studies of their combination; evidence is insufficient for quantitative synthesis or product-level claims.
6. **microbiota-insomnia-interventions — PMID 42509727** — Systematic review of 56 controlled human/animal studies found sleep-related signals from probiotics, prebiotics, dietary patterns and botanicals, but major intervention heterogeneity and mixed preclinical/human evidence prevent attribution to a single compound or strain.
7. **curcumin — PMID 41108513** — Four-RCT schizophrenia meta-analysis found no significant effect on positive or total symptoms, a small low-certainty reduction in negative symptoms, and very uncertain effects on depression/general psychopathology; authors did not recommend clinical implementation.
8. **nrf2-activators-schizophrenia — PMID 42154264** — Meta-analysis of 16 RCTs across curcumin, ascorbic acid, resveratrol and sulforaphane found non-robust and compound-specific schizophrenia signals; formulation/co-intervention heterogeneity and sparse data for several agents require category-level handling.
9. **omega3-vitamin-e-pcos — PMID 41664652** — Network meta-analysis of 25 RCTs in PCOS found potential mood benefits for omega-3 plus vitamin E among several psychological/exercise/drug interventions, while vitamin D plus probiotics was not significantly beneficial; combined-intervention attribution firewall applies.
10. **probiotics-pregnancy-postpartum — PMID 41783815** — Four RCTs/1,342 women without diagnosed mental illness found limited strain-specific probiotic signals for perinatal depressive/anxiety symptoms; no prebiotic or synbiotic trials were available and risk of bias was moderate-to-high.
11. **probiotics-fibromyalgia — PMID 41789223** — Systematic review of four probiotic studies in fibromyalgia found inconsistent anxiety/depression effects and insufficient safety reporting, so no definitive recommendation for or against probiotics could be made.
12. **depression-augmentation-nutraceuticals — PMID 42281452** — Systematic review of non-pharmacological/non-psychological depression augmentation found lower levels of evidence for probiotics than for exercise or bright-light approaches; augmentation evidence remains category-specific and does not establish probiotic monotherapy efficacy.
13. **gut-microbiota-ibd-mood — PMID 42311388** — Systematic review of six prospective cohorts and four RCTs in IBD linked dysbiosis with anxiety/depression and found microbiota-targeted interventions sometimes improved symptoms, but interventions included probiotics and fecal microbiota transplantation and remained heterogeneous.
14. **scfa-modulation-depression — PMID 42674291** — Meta-analysis of seven intervention RCTs found pooled depressive-symptom improvement from diverse SCFA-modulating strategies, including probiotics, FMT, vagal stimulation and direct butyrate; between-class differences were not significant and the evidence cannot be assigned to one supplement.
15. **ginseng — PMID 41863264** — Nine-study/617-participant meta-analysis in MCI/Alzheimer/dementia found modest cognitive-score improvements with ginseng, but effects did not reach clear clinical significance and evidence was limited by small study count and heterogeneity.
16. **choline-dietary-intake — PMID 42735059** — Dose-response meta-analysis of observational studies associated higher dietary choline intake with lower risk of cognitive decline and Alzheimer-related outcomes, but this is non-randomized dietary evidence and cannot establish supplementation efficacy or an optimal supplemental dose.
17. **n-acetylcysteine — PMID 40988585** — Translational Parkinson review found consistent preclinical neuroprotection and small open-label human signals in UPDRS/DAT imaging, but randomized efficacy evidence was lacking; disease-modifying claims remain premature.
18. **n-acetylcysteine — PMID 42115982** — Seven placebo-controlled RCTs/566 participants found no consistent NAC benefit for alcohol craving or PTSD severity; a sensitivity-analysis signal emerged only after one study was removed, supporting an overall null/uncertain efficacy interpretation.
19. **anti-inflammatory-adjuncts-schizophrenia — PMID 41559638** — Meta-analysis of 74 RCTs found apparent adjunctive benefits for several anti-inflammatory agents including NAC and fatty acids, but publication bias, heterogeneity and sensitivity analyses weakened many effects; routine use was not robustly supported.
20. **vascular-dementia-cognition-interventions — PMID 42635820** — VaD-focused RCT meta-analysis found small cognitive effects for galantamine/memantine and a fragile very-low-certainty EGb 761 signal, while citicoline and several other interventions remained single-trial/non-poolable; patient-important benefits were uncertain.
21. **taurine — PMID 41750826** — Systematic review of eight acute RCTs/244 healthy participants found mostly null or small inconsistent taurine cognition/mood effects; more reliable benefits occurred in caffeine combinations that did not isolate taurine.
22. **creatine-hydrochloride — PMID 40854087** — Small four-arm RCT in 36 peri/postmenopausal women found medium-dose creatine hydrochloride improved selected reaction-time, brain-creatine and lipid outcomes versus placebo, but sample size, formulation specificity and exploratory endpoints sharply limit generalization to creatine monohydrate.
23. **creatine — PMID 41960895** — Parallel trial in 40 young active adults found creatine monohydrate improved Wingate mean/peak power but did not improve Stroop or Trail cognitive outcomes; useful direct null evidence against broad acute cognition claims in healthy active adults.
24. **creatine — PMID 42075005** — Sleep-deprivation crossover trial in 29 healthy adults found a single creatine dose attenuated deterioration in several cognitive tasks during 21 hours of sleep loss; acute sleep-deprivation context and unusually large studied exposure prevent routine cognition/dosing extrapolation.
25. **omega-3-pufa-adhd — PMID 42776218** — Meta-analysis of 16 pediatric ADHD RCTs found a small borderline overall PUFA benefit with stronger signals on performance-based cognition than subjective ratings; heterogeneity was substantial and PUFA supplementation was not supported as standalone treatment.
