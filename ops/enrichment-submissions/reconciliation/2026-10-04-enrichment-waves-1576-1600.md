# Research enrichment Waves 1576–1600 — 2026-10-04

Fourth pass of the Waves 1501–2000 program, filling under-covered exercise/performance evidence for beta-alanine, carnosine, beetroot/nitrate, stimulant combinations and sport-specific supplement reviews.

## Waves 1576–1600
1. **beta-alanine-older-adults-cognition-mood — PMID 36839281** — Ten-week double-blind RCT in 100 older adults found no overall MoCA advantage from beta-alanine, although participants with below-normal baseline cognition showed subgroup improvement; mood and physical-function outcomes were largely null.
2. **beta-alanine-combat-sports-review — PMID 37686787** — Systematic review of seven combat-sport clinical trials reported some improvements in strength, power, work capacity and sport-specific measures with beta-alanine, but the small heterogeneous evidence base and mixed perceptual/biochemical findings limit broad performance claims.
3. **beta-alanine-brain-mri-older-adults — PMID 38971131** — Small mechanistic trial in only 10 analyzed older adults with lower baseline cognition found beta-alanine increased fractional anisotropy in the hippocampus and amygdala without changing BDNF or inflammatory markers; MRI changes are hypothesis-generating, not clinical proof.
4. **beta-alanine-high-intensity-functional-training — PMID 39064783** — Four-week RCT in 27 high-intensity functional-training participants found beta-alanine improved vertical-jump and jumping-power measures but did not clearly alter fatigue, exercise metabolic intensity or perceived intensity.
5. **caffeine-beta-alanine-resistance-training — PMID 39438314** — Triple-blind crossover study in 21 resistance-trained men found caffeine, beta-alanine and their combination produced little consistent mechanical advantage during upper-body superset training; beta-alanine alone reduced last-repetition velocity versus placebo on one measure.
6. **combat-sport-supplements-network-meta — PMID 39747536** — Bayesian network meta-analysis of 67 RCTs involving 1,026 elite combat-sport athletes compared 26 supplements; rankings varied by outcome and discipline, so indirect SUCRA-style hierarchy should not be translated into a universal best supplement.
7. **swimming-supplements-network-meta — PMID 39796467** — Network meta-analysis of swimming interventions found outcome-specific signals for L-arginine, beta-alanine and creatine plus sodium bicarbonate, while several swimming-time outcomes were null; indirect comparisons and sparse networks constrain rankings.
8. **beta-alanine-overweight-feasibility — PMID 39800667** — Three-month randomized feasibility trial in 27 adults with overweight or obesity found sustained-release beta-alanine was tolerated and adhered to, but showed low probability of meaningful cardiometabolic, cardiovascular or routine biochemical effects.
9. **beta-alanine-hiit-bdnf-stroop — PMID 39832765** — Fourteen-week beta-alanine plus HIIT study in 13 active men found no improvement in serum BDNF or Stroop performance; training improved selected cardiorespiratory outcomes independently, providing direct null cognitive evidence.
10. **carnosine-neat-cognition — PMID 39919936** — Post-hoc randomized-trial analysis found carnosine improved selected cognition speed and efficiency outcomes mainly in the youngest adult age stratum; subgroup/post-hoc status means the finding needs prospective confirmation.
11. **creatine-beta-alanine-citrulline-basketball — PMID 40010353** — Four-week six-arm basketball trial found sprint-interval training improved performance across groups and supplement arms showed larger changes than placebo on selected outcomes, but small groups and combined creatine/beta-alanine/citrulline conditions complicate ingredient attribution.
12. **beta-alanine-distance-runners — PMID 40528157** — Four-week randomized trial in competitive adolescent middle- and long-distance runners found beta-alanine improved time to exhaustion but not aerobic capacity, reinforcing that endurance-test effects do not equal VO2max improvement.
13. **beta-alanine-futsal-null — PMID 40981477** — Two-week high-dose beta-alanine RCT in 16 trained futsal players found no significant supplementation-by-time benefit for intermittent endurance, 30-m sprint, lactate or perceived exertion despite overall training/time improvements.
14. **beta-alanine-strength-power-dosing-review — PMID 40995761** — Systematic review of nine studies in trained participants suggested beta-alanine strength/power findings may vary with exposure pattern and training metabolic demand, but this literature synthesis is heterogeneous and studied protocols must not become dosing recommendations.
15. **betalain-rich-concentrate-running — PMID 39994008** — Triple-blind crossover trial in 17 recreational male runners found an acute betalain-rich concentrate did not improve running economy or VO2max, although heart rate, perceived exertion and post-exercise muscle oxygenation showed selected favorable changes.
16. **beetroot-caffeine-resistance-performance — PMID 40369047** — Four-condition crossover trial in 13 resistance-trained men found caffeine improved several squat strength/power measures while beetroot juice and combination conditions affected selected endurance outcomes; no uniform additive synergy was established.
17. **beetroot-short-term-strength-recovery — PMID 40431460** — Small crossover trial in 12 resistance-trained men reported selected strength, fatigue and recovery benefits after short-term beetroot-juice supplementation, but sample size and multiple endpoints make the magnitude and reproducibility uncertain.
18. **beetroot-performance-umbrella-review — PMID 40573069** — Umbrella review of 15 meta-analyses found statistically significant but generally negligible-to-small beetroot-juice effects on strength, VO2max and time-to-exhaustion; effect size and population differences argue against broad performance promises.
19. **beta-alanine-caffeine-ocular-pressure — PMID 39732141** — Triple-blind crossover study in 20 young men found caffeine, with or without beta-alanine, acutely raised intraocular pressure at rest while beta-alanine alone did not; exercise subsequently reduced ocular-pressure measures regardless of supplement.
20. **carnosine-beta-alanine-glycemia-meta — PMID 40999397** — Meta-analysis of eight RCTs and 377 participants with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes found carnosine/beta-alanine interventions reduced fasting glucose and HbA1c but not several lipid, insulin or anthropometric outcomes; interventions and populations were heterogeneous.
21. **beta-alanine-sodium-bicarbonate-female-basketball — PMID 41838453** — Randomized double-blind trial in 68 highly trained female basketball players found sodium-bicarbonate-containing arms improved initial Wingate peak power, whereas beta-alanine alone did not improve measured physical-capacity outcomes; combination effects require separation.
22. **beta-alanine-kickboxing — PMID 42127019** — Four-week double-blind RCT in 28 trained male kickboxers found beta-alanine improved total kickboxing anaerobic work and push-up/pull-up endurance but not jump power, best effort or performance-decrement index.
23. **beetroot-endothelial-function-resistance-exercise — PMID 40457872** — Crossover study in 12 young men found beetroot juice prevented an early post-resistance-exercise reduction in flow-mediated dilation without clear differences in blood pressure or several hemodynamic measures; this is an acute vascular surrogate finding.
24. **oral-nitrite-older-adults-null — PMID 41691463** — Twelve-week double-blind RCT in 64 sedentary adults age 70 and older found oral nitrite did not improve skeletal-muscle mitochondrial respiration, exercise capacity or physical function despite acute platelet bioenergetic effects.
25. **water-polo-ergonutrition-review — PMID 40284184** — Systematic review of water-polo ergonutrition described possible roles for whey protein, beta-alanine, L-arginine, spirulina and other supplements, but the sport-specific evidence base is sparse and heterogeneous and should remain category-level context.
