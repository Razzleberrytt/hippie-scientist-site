# Research enrichment Waves 1551–1575 — 2026-10-04

Third pass of the Waves 1501–2000 program, focused on cognition, attention, stimulants, choline/phospholipids, creatine and Ginkgo evidence with explicit product, combination and safety boundaries.

## Waves 1551–1575
1. **energy-drinks-cardiovascular-safety — PMID 41236610** — Systematic review of 37 studies involving 1,597 mostly young participants found energy-drink exposure frequently increased heart rate and blood pressure and was associated with QTc and other ECG changes; multi-ingredient products and heterogeneous protocols prevent caffeine-only attribution.
2. **caffeine-neuroinflammation-preclinical-mood — PMID 41249138** — Systematic review of 17 rodent studies found caffeine often reduced stress-related behavioral, oxidative and neuroinflammatory changes, but dose, route and model heterogeneity were substantial and animal findings cannot establish antidepressant or anxiolytic efficacy in humans.
3. **caffeine-mouth-rinse-tennis — PMID 41269093** — Randomized crossover study in 16 recreational male tennis players found caffeine mouth rinsing improved selected groundstroke, volley and total targeting scores versus placebo/no-rinse control; tiny sport-specific study does not establish general cognitive enhancement.
4. **phosphatidylserine-healthy-children-cognition — PMID 41318468** — Twelve-week randomized placebo-controlled trial in healthy children age 8 to 12 found no primary or secondary cognitive, mood or sleep benefit from sunflower phosphatidylserine in the full cohort; a predefined low-performing subgroup showed a visuospatial-memory signal only.
5. **caffeine-energy-drink-cognition-metabolism — PMID 41374082** — Twenty-eight-day randomized trial found a caffeine-based energy drink acutely increased energy expenditure and produced selected reaction-time, focus and energy effects, but the beverage matrix and repeated-exposure design prevent clean caffeine-versus-other-ingredient attribution.
6. **choline-alphoscerate-citicoline-dementia — PMID 41426989** — Meta-analysis of only three RCTs and 358 dementia patients found choline alphoscerate favored citicoline on a geriatric clinical-assessment scale and several subdomains, while memory and word-fluency differences were null; the very small evidence base limits comparative claims.
7. **creatine-loading-sleep-cognition-recovery — PMID 41470776** — Small randomized crossover trial in 14 physically active men tested a one-week creatine loading phase across sleep, cognition, physical performance and recovery; findings are acute-loading and male-athlete specific, so they should not be generalized to chronic cognitive use.
8. **choline-postmenopausal-working-memory-fmri — PMID 41683281** — Pilot crossover study in 20 postmenopausal women found oral choline altered working-memory-related brain activation and connectivity without improving task performance; neuroimaging changes alone do not establish cognitive benefit.
9. **egg-phosphatidylcholine-tmao — PMID 41742272** — Secondary analysis of a 12-week randomized trial in 32 healthy middle-aged and older adults found within-group TMAO elevation at the highest egg-phosphatidylcholine intake but no significant between-group difference versus placebo; values remained below high-risk thresholds.
10. **caffeine-morning-cognition-strength — PMID 41901129** — Randomized crossover study in recreationally active men found morning caffeine improved selected strength-related measures, while cognitive outcomes varied by test; a single standardized morning protocol does not imply uniform cognitive enhancement.
11. **creatine-resistance-training-older-adults — PMID 41941966** — Six-arm 16-week RCT in 103 older adults found high-load resistance training improved multiple physical and biomarker outcomes and creatine supplied some additional benefits, but the exercise-by-supplement factorial context prevents assigning all gains to creatine alone.
12. **caffeine-preterm-neurodevelopment — PMID 41945073** — Systematic review/meta-analysis of nine studies and more than 7,000 assessments associated neonatal caffeine treatment with better motor and cognitive outcomes and lower cerebral-palsy odds but no clear language benefit; this is a neonatal medical-treatment context, not consumer stimulant evidence.
13. **theacrine-high-dose-cognition-stress — PMID 41950524** — Randomized crossover study in 19 habitual caffeine users found higher theacrine exposures increased salivary cortisol without clear heart-rate, blood-pressure or cognitive benefit; this provides a useful stress-response and null-efficacy counterweight to stimulant marketing.
14. **phosphatidylserine-adhd-behavior — PMID 41960234** — Randomized open-label comparison in 56 children with ADHD found phosphatidylserine did not significantly improve core ADHD symptoms, while atomoxetine did; phosphatidylserine showed some internalizing/externalizing behavior changes, but open-label active-comparator design limits inference.
15. **ginkgo-biloba-extract-review — PMID 42000006** — Broad 2026 Ginkgo biloba review integrates extraction, delivery, mechanistic, clinical and toxicology literature; useful as a map of formulation and safety issues, but mixed evidence types and technologies should not be treated as a single efficacy estimate.
16. **ginkgo-traumatic-brain-injury — PMID 42081645** — Systematic review spanning 42 preclinical studies and 23 clinical trials found possible cognitive and functional signals for Ginkgo after traumatic brain injury, while highlighting reported bleeding events especially with anticoagulants or NSAIDs; clinical certainty remains limited.
17. **caffeine-schizophrenia — PMID 42138706** — Systematic review/meta-analysis of 11 studies and 1,406 adults with schizophrenia found no significant overall symptom effect from caffeine, with mixed signals for negative symptoms, cognition and possible positive-symptom worsening; medication interactions and heterogeneity matter.
18. **caffeinated-gum-special-forces — PMID 42142184** — Randomized crossover trial in 16 male special-forces soldiers found caffeinated gum improved selected reaction-time, strength, jump, shooting and close-quarters-battle measures versus placebo; small elite-military sample sharply limits generalization.
19. **caffeine-theanine-breathing-esports — PMID 42176572** — Three-arm crossover trial in 15 professional male esports athletes found caffeine plus L-theanine and caffeine plus diaphragmatic breathing improved auditory reaction time and Aim Lab performance versus caffeine alone; ingredient-specific attribution is impossible for the combined arms.
20. **caffeine-p300-meta-analysis — PMID 42176899** — Meta-analysis found caffeine increased P300 amplitude at moderate studied exposures but did not significantly change P300 latency, with high between-study heterogeneity; electrophysiological changes should remain separate from claims of meaningful cognitive improvement.
21. **energy-drink-sprint-cognitive-fatigue — PMID 42180830** — Small randomized crossover study in 12 college athletes found a multi-ingredient energy drink improved repeated-sprint output and attenuated aspects of cognitive fatigue versus placebo, but caffeine, anserine, elderberry and micronutrients were co-administered.
22. **caffeine-female-team-sport-meta — PMID 42588052** — Three-level meta-analysis of 26 randomized crossover trials in female team-sport athletes found small favorable physical-performance effects from acute caffeine, while sport-specific and cognitive estimates were less certain; domain-specific effects and evidence certainty should remain explicit.
23. **dietary-choline-dementia-risk — PMID 42380861** — Prospective cohort plus dose-response meta-analysis found a nonlinear association between dietary choline and incident dementia, with moderate—not highest—intakes associated with lower risk in some analyses; observational nutrition data cannot establish supplementation benefit.
24. **caffeinated-beverages-attention-alertness — PMID 42559121** — Randomized double-masked study in 116 adults found both an energy drink and caffeine-matched control improved sustained attention and alertness similarly, with no strength or static-endurance advantage from the energy drink; results point to caffeine rather than proprietary extras.
25. **caffeine-morning-mood-agility-cognition — PMID 42738944** — Large crossover study of 51 recreationally active men found morning caffeine did not significantly improve the tested cognitive battery or reactive-agility outcomes versus placebo/no-pill control, providing a useful direct null against blanket morning-performance claims.
