# Research enrichment Waves 1901–1925 — 2026-10-04

Seventeenth pass of the Waves 1501–2000 program, emphasizing kratom/7-OH and kava harm-reduction evidence, product evolution, toxicology, withdrawal, interactions and surveillance while preserving case-level and observational limits.

## Waves 1901–1925
1. **7oh-severe-neurocardiac-toxicity — PMID 42534824** — Case report of large 7-hydroxymitragynine ingestion described profound CNS depression, marked QTc prolongation and prolonged encephalopathy requiring intensive care; polysubstance and psychiatric/infectious contributors make causality multifactorial but the toxicity signal is serious.
2. **kratom-evidence-gap-review — PMID 42547284** — Focused clinical review emphasizes that expanding US kratom use is occurring amid unregulated product variability, limited high-quality safety/efficacy data and increasing need for clinician recognition of use, dependence and toxicity.
3. **kratom-human-metabolomics — PMID 42562516** — Human cross-sectional metabolomics study of 37 regular kratom users versus 12 controls found distinct plasma metabolite/pathway profiles involving amino-acid, pyruvate, TCA, glycolytic and glutathione metabolism; association does not establish harm or benefit.
4. **kratom-global-prevalence-meta — PMID 42565818** — Meta-analysis of 21 studies estimated low general-population but substantially higher high-risk/substance-using kratom prevalence, with major regional and methodological heterogeneity; surveillance evidence, not efficacy or toxicity.
5. **kratom-product-evolution-review — PMID 42572688** — Review distinguishes native leaf from increasingly concentrated extracts, isolates and 7-OH-enriched or chemically altered products and argues dependence/overdose risk likely rises as alkaloid concentration and product modification increase.
6. **kratom-use-disorder-pregnancy — PMID 42576700** — Perinatal review/case discussion describes maternal and neonatal withdrawal risk and very limited evidence for buprenorphine management of kratom use disorder in pregnancy; only a few pregnancy cases support current practice.
7. **kratom-alkaloid-human-pk-method — PMID 42605611** — Validated human plasma assay quantified 12 kratom alkaloids and five mitragynine metabolites, showing rapid absorption and identifying major circulating alkaloids/metabolites in regular users; high-value source-identity and PK evidence.
8. **kratom-methamphetamine-craving-crosssectional — PMID 42607660** — Cross-sectional treatment sample of 403 men found kratom-use indicators were not overall risk factors for methamphetamine craving; only a subgroup association with age at first kratom use emerged in those with heroin history.
9. **kratom-toxicity-clinical-review — PMID 42634663** — Narrative toxicology review describes opioid-like toxidromes, variable naloxone responsiveness, possible delayed/recurrent respiratory depression, polysubstance amplification and substantial product heterogeneity; clinical management evidence remains case-heavy.
10. **kratom-cardiovascular-review — PMID 42636328** — Cardiovascular review integrates ion-channel mechanisms with reported tachycardia, hypertension, QTc prolongation, Brugada patterns, ventricular arrhythmias and cardiac arrest while emphasizing heterogeneous products and confounding co-exposures.
11. **acmt-7oh-position-statement — PMID 42645738** — American College of Medical Toxicology position statement identifies public-health risks from 7-hydroxymitragynine and other kratom-associated compounds; authoritative toxicology guidance rather than an efficacy study.
12. **kratom-cancer-consumer-survey — PMID 42655877** — Self-selected US survey of 136 kratom consumers found cancer and non-cancer users differed in motivations and patterns, with cancer respondents frequently using kratom for pain/mental-health concerns; perceptions cannot establish effectiveness or safety.
13. **kratom-pulmonary-toxicity-systematic — PMID 42661120** — Systematic review of 24 clinical/forensic reports classified kratom-associated diffuse alveolar hemorrhage, ARDS/lung injury, eosinophilic pneumonitis, respiratory depression and postmortem pulmonary edema; evidence remains case/forensic based but clinically important.
14. **kratom-hearing-loss-rhabdo-case — PMID 42662183** — Case report describes transient sensorineural hearing loss, rhabdomyolysis, severe AKI and dialysis in a chronic kratom user; sepsis and other clinical factors limit single-agent causal certainty.
15. **kratom-ventricular-tachycardia-ecmo — PMID 42377231** — Case report describes seizure-like activity and refractory pulseless ventricular tachycardia after concentrated kratom extract, requiring prolonged resuscitation and VA-ECMO; toxicology confirmed mitragynine.
16. **kava-kratom-liquid-shortterm-safety — PMID 39807458** — Six-day randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial of a commercial kava/kratom liquid blend found mostly mild-to-moderate adverse events and no major laboratory toxicity in healthy adults; short duration and combined-product design cannot establish long-term safety.
17. **kava-acute-kidney-injury-case — PMID 40343400** — Case report links very heavy kava tea ingestion with biopsy-proven severe acute tubulointerstitial nephritis, marked creatinine rise, bradycardia and QT prolongation, with recovery after corticosteroids; product analysis found kavalactones without other detected substances.
18. **kava-acute-skin-toxicity-letter — PMID 40378629** — Published report documents acute kava-associated skin toxicity; preserve as a rare safety signal while avoiding incidence estimates from case-level evidence.
19. **kava-serotonergic-interaction-pediatric — PMID 40510098** — Pediatric case report describes prolonged serotonin-syndrome features during kava use while switching serotonergic antidepressants, with plausible CYP2D6/MAO interaction mechanisms; single-case evidence supports interaction caution, not frequency estimates.
20. **kava-severe-withdrawal-case — PMID 40541460** — Case report describes hallucinations, anxiety, insomnia and myoclonus after stopping heavy kava/kratom beverage use, with GABAergic withdrawal suspected and improvement after phenobarbital/diazepam; mixed-product exposure complicates attribution.
21. **kava-global-systematic-review — PMID 40560778** — Narrative systematic review of 197 records found kava's cultural/economic importance clear but health benefits and harms—including anxiety and liver effects—remain inconsistent and methodologically limited.
22. **kava-acute-dermatitis-case-series — PMID 40757984** — Three-patient case series found acute kava reactions resembling DRESS with distinctive sebaceous adenitis/apoptosis on biopsy; rare dermatologic safety signal without incidence data.
23. **kava-us-use-patterns-survey — PMID 40962124** — Large US survey estimated 10.7% past-year kava use among respondents and common co-use with prescription/alternative products; perceived relaxation benefits and drowsiness/sweating are self-report observational findings.
24. **kava-kratom-sud-assessment — PMID 41322679** — Online survey of adults using kava/kratom found past-year SUD criteria much more common for kratom than kava and substantial co-use; self-selected sampling and DSM-criterion adaptation require cautious interpretation.
25. **tianeptine-poison-centers — PMID 39724478** — US poison-center analysis tracked rising tianeptine exposures from 2015–2023 and associated clinical outcomes, providing national toxicovigilance evidence for misuse and toxicity rather than controlled incidence estimates.
