# Research enrichment Waves 1926–1950 — 2026-10-04

Eighteenth pass of the Waves 1501–2000 program, emphasizing tianeptine dependence/toxicity and dietary-supplement adulteration, authentication, mislabeling and liver-injury evidence.

## Waves 1926–1950
1. **tianeptine-synthetic-cannabinoid-outbreak — PMID 40102319** — New Jersey poison-center outbreak identified 34 patients with severe toxicity after tianeptine products; product testing found variable composition including synthetic cannabinoids, kava alkaloids and cannabinoids, and many patients required intubation/ICU care.
2. **tianeptine-use-patterns-sud-survey — PMID 41084181** — Online survey found tianeptine use uncommon within a gray-market-product sample but frequently associated with rapid tolerance, adverse effects, severe past-year SUD criteria and strong perceived addictiveness/overdose risk; self-selected sample limits prevalence inference.
3. **tianeptine-alcohol-use-disorders-case — PMID 41127130** — Case report describes concomitant tianeptine and alcohol use disorders treated with buprenorphine-naloxone; single-patient management evidence supports clinical awareness without establishing an optimal protocol.
4. **tianeptine-use-disorder-case-review — PMID 41132482** — Case report and review describes severe tianeptine use disorder with psychiatric deterioration, dependence and withdrawal, emphasizing diagnostic barriers and need for addiction-treatment referral; case-level evidence only.
5. **tianeptine-misuse-systematic-review — PMID 41136982** — Systematic review of 25 studies found frequent habitual misuse, overdose, withdrawal and co-use, with naloxone and buprenorphine appearing in reported management; underlying literature is largely case-based and heterogeneous.
6. **tianeptine-three-cases-management — PMID 42016572** — Three-case series and review describes rapid tolerance, opioid-like intoxication/withdrawal and use of naloxone, buprenorphine, clonidine and supportive care; treatment conclusions remain limited by case evidence.
7. **tianeptine-addiction-systematic-review — PMID 42177839** — Systematic review of 53 publications found opioid-like dependence, withdrawal and overdose, with substantial ICU use in retrospective series and reported fatalities among detailed overdose cases; evidence is dominated by case reports/series.
8. **kratom-tianeptine-combined-overdose — PMID 42198932** — Case report describes severe hypoxemic respiratory failure, encephalopathy and pulmonary infiltrates after combined kratom/tianeptine ingestion, improving with naloxone and ICU supportive care; co-exposure prevents single-agent attribution.
9. **tianeptine-reddit-experiences — PMID 42412511** — NLP-assisted qualitative analysis of Reddit posts found increasing discussion of tianeptine dependence, withdrawal and co-ingestion; useful real-world signal but self-reported social-media content cannot estimate clinical incidence.
10. **emerging-substances-clinician-knowledge — PMID 42459031** — Survey of addiction-conference clinicians found substantial knowledge gaps around kratom, phenibut and tianeptine despite high concern and support for more research/regulation; clinician-practice context rather than toxicity incidence.
11. **tianeptine-methadone-case — PMID 42767626** — Case report describes sustained abstinence from tianeptine after methadone treatment when high-dose buprenorphine had not controlled cravings/withdrawal; illustrates a treatment gap but cannot establish comparative efficacy.
12. **eria-jarensis-supplement-mislabeling — PMID 40679500** — Laboratory analysis found none of 12 US sports supplements labeled with Eria jarensis were accurately labeled; several contained undeclared stimulants, including FDA-prohibited DMAA, and caffeine content varied dramatically.
13. **supplement-adulteration-lab-review — PMID 40700739** — Review summarizes laboratory risk-mitigation approaches for dietary-supplement adulteration, including identity/purity testing, supplier quality agreements and third-party verification; quality-system evidence rather than clinical efficacy.
14. **botanical-authentication-methods-review — PMID 40783800** — Review of authentication methods for botanicals in plant extracts and supplements highlights identity testing as a core defense against substitution and adulteration; methodological quality-control evidence.
15. **sports-supplement-anabolic-steroid-adulteration — PMID 41097223** — Review documents the problem of sports supplements adulterated with anabolic steroids and the anti-doping/health consequences of undeclared exposure; product-quality and harm-reduction evidence.
16. **rhodiola-us-market-quality — PMID 41544018** — US market analysis found wide deviations in Rhodiola rosavin/salidroside content, probable undisclosed synthetic salidroside in one product and trace metals in tested products; strong formulation/authenticity warning.
17. **illicit-supplement-steroid-screening — PMID 41937464** — Analytical study applies LC-MS/MS and LC-QTOF/MS screening for anabolic-androgenic steroids in illicit dietary supplements/counterfeit drugs; useful adulterant-detection and quality-control evidence.
18. **cordyceps-online-falsification — PMID 41947147** — Post-market analysis of 65 online Cordyceps products found more than half falsified, 11% unregistered and 6.2% adulterated with corticosteroids, alongside frequent illegal disease claims.
19. **weightloss-supplement-diuretic-screening — PMID 41955470** — Validated GC-MS method targets undeclared diuretics in adulterated weight-loss supplements, supporting forensic/product-quality surveillance rather than efficacy claims.
20. **green-coffee-supplement-mislabeling — PMID 42154619** — Analysis of 23 green-coffee-bean supplements found wide chlorogenic-acid/caffeine variability, products labeled 50% CGAs with none detected, and decaffeinated products exceeding caffeine thresholds.
21. **tadalafil-analogues-herbal-sex-supplements — PMID 42350094** — Analytical chemistry study isolated previously unreported tadalafil analogues illegally added to a male sexual herbal supplement, demonstrating pharmaceutical adulteration of purportedly herbal products.
22. **sibutramine-slimming-supplement-cases — PMID 42425829** — Toxicovigilance report documents multiple batches of a slimming supplement adulterated with sibutramine and associated clinical cases; high-value undeclared-drug safety signal.
23. **yellow-oleander-misrepresented-supplement — PMID 42647950** — Case report describes yellow-oleander toxicity from a misrepresented dietary supplement, underscoring that botanical/product identity failures can cause severe poisoning independent of labeled intent.
24. **turmeric-piperine-dili-case — PMID 41281037** — Case report attributes drug-induced liver injury to a turmeric supplement containing piperine after competing causes were evaluated; single-case evidence supports hepatotoxicity vigilance, especially with bioavailability-enhancing formulations.
25. **ashwagandha-reproductive-thyroid-immune-safety-review — PMID 41454545** — Safety review focuses on reproductive, thyroid and immune-system concerns relevant to ashwagandha use as a food/additive, emphasizing unresolved risk questions and the need for population-specific caution.
