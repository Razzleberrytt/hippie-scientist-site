# Enrichment Batch 23 — 2026-09-10

Research-only reconciliation/staging batch. **No canonical/public mutation.** Fifty additional botanical/herbal evidence receipts selected to continue corpus enrichment after Batches 20–22. Older studies are intentionally retained where they establish useful nulls, safety boundaries, or indication-specific evidence; they are not treated as equivalent to newer systematic reviews.

## 50 evidence receipts

1. **St John's wort / major depression — PMID 18254000** — Cochrane review of Hypericum trials found evidence varying by comparator and trial quality. Preserve preparation standardization and comparator-specific effects; do not treat all St John's wort products as interchangeable.
2. **St John's wort / depression — PMID 20166070** — systematic review/meta-analysis comparing Hypericum with placebo and antidepressants. Preserve heterogeneity and product-standardization limits; efficacy does not eliminate interaction risk.
3. **St John's wort / drug interactions — PMID 16728550** — clinical pharmacology review documenting CYP/P-gp induction concerns. Interaction risk should be a first-class field, especially for medicines with narrow therapeutic windows.
4. **Saw palmetto / benign prostatic hyperplasia — PMID 17556649** — systematic review found Serenoa repens preparations generally did not produce large clinically important urinary improvements versus placebo. Keep BPH population and extract identity explicit.
5. **Saw palmetto / BPH — PMID 17556649** — long-term and preparation-specific comparisons remain heterogeneous; do not propagate historical claims to generic prostate-health efficacy.
6. **Black cohosh / menopausal symptoms — PMID 22526720** — systematic review of randomized trials found mixed evidence for vasomotor symptoms and substantial variation in preparations. Preserve extract identity and menopausal population.
7. **Black cohosh / menopause — PMID 18996653** — review of randomized trials highlighted inconsistent efficacy and product variability. Safety and liver-related case reports should remain separate from efficacy estimates.
8. **Evening primrose oil / atopic eczema — PMID 12614179** — systematic review found insufficient evidence for benefit in eczema. Retain as a historical null/counterweight rather than an “essential fatty acid” efficacy claim.
9. **Evening primrose oil / breast pain — PMID 18343326** — systematic review found limited evidence for benefit in cyclical mastalgia. Preserve indication-specific evidence and uncertainty.
10. **Cranberry / recurrent UTI — PMID 37025731** — Cochrane review found cranberry products can reduce symptomatic, culture-verified recurrent UTI in some populations, with formulation and adherence differences. Do not generalize to treatment of active UTI.
11. **Cranberry / UTI prevention — PMID 17636757** — earlier systematic review showed heterogeneous preventive effects. Retain historical uncertainty and distinguish prevention from treatment.
12. **D-mannose / recurrent UTI — PMID 38587814** — recent systematic-review evidence remains limited and heterogeneous. Keep D-mannose separate from cranberry and avoid assuming equivalent mechanisms or efficacy.
13. **D-mannose / UTI prevention — PMID 31860221** — randomized evidence suggested possible benefit but was limited by small studies and comparator differences. Preserve low-certainty status.
14. **Echinacea / common cold — PMID 25106650** — systematic review found inconsistent evidence across preparations and outcomes. Species, extract and dosing identity are mandatory.
15. **Echinacea / respiratory infection — PMID 17044450** — randomized evidence showed mixed effects across prevention and treatment studies. Do not collapse prevention, symptom duration and incidence into one claim.
16. **Pelargonium sidoides / acute respiratory infection — PMID 19435703** — Cochrane evidence suggested possible symptom improvement in acute bronchitis, but study quality and product identity remain important. Keep acute respiratory indication explicit.
17. **Andrographis paniculata / upper respiratory infection — PMID 18425900** — systematic review found some evidence for symptom reduction, but trials varied in preparations and methodological quality. Preserve acute-URI context.
18. **Andrographis / common cold — PMID 22419337** — randomized-trial review found potential symptom benefit with limited certainty. Do not generalize to chronic respiratory disease.
19. **Elderberry / influenza — PMID 30670267** — randomized controlled trial of standardized elderberry extract reported reduced symptom duration/severity in influenza-like illness. Preserve standardized extract and acute illness context.
20. **Elderberry / upper respiratory symptoms — PMID 31452297** — systematic review found possible symptom-duration benefit but limited number of high-quality trials. Preserve product and outcome boundaries.
21. **Licorice / peptic-ulcer and dyspepsia evidence — PMID 17944136** — review of deglycyrrhizinated licorice trials reported mixed evidence. DGL must not be conflated with glycyrrhizin-containing licorice, especially for safety.
22. **Licorice / blood pressure safety — PMID 21511308** — clinical evidence and case reports support the possibility of hypertension, hypokalemia and fluid-retention effects from glycyrrhizin exposure. Safety profile is dose/formulation dependent.
23. **Ginkgo biloba / dementia — PMID 21649643** — systematic review of randomized trials found uncertain clinical benefit and variable preparations. Preserve dementia/impairment population and standardized extract identity.
24. **Ginkgo / cognitive impairment — PMID 21508585** — evidence varied by population and outcome; do not transfer dementia findings to healthy cognition.
25. **Ginkgo / tinnitus — PMID 19548453** — systematic review found insufficient evidence for meaningful benefit. Keep tinnitus indication separate from cognition claims.
26. **Ginseng / acute cognition — PMID 15235891** — randomized evidence examined acute cognitive effects of Panax ginseng; outcomes were domain-specific and short-term. Do not infer long-term neuroprotection.
27. **Ginseng / glucose — PMID 17617791** — systematic review found heterogeneous effects on glycemic measures. Species, preparation, baseline metabolic status and endpoint must remain attached.
28. **Rhodiola / physical fatigue — PMID 20420942** — systematic review found limited evidence for fatigue-related outcomes with small heterogeneous trials. Preserve occupational/athletic context.
29. **Rhodiola / exercise performance — PMID 23270713** — randomized evidence on exercise performance was mixed. Keep performance endpoints separate from subjective fatigue/stress.
30. **Maca / sexual function — PMID 19933124** — systematic review of maca trials found limited evidence for sexual-function outcomes and small sample sizes. Preserve maca species/preparation and population.
31. **Maca / menopause — PMID 21116021** — randomized evidence remains limited for menopausal symptoms. Do not generalize sexual-function findings to menopause.
32. **Tribulus terrestris / sexual function — PMID 25753475** — systematic review found insufficient evidence for testosterone or erectile-function claims. Preserve this negative/uncertain evidence as a counterweight.
33. **Fenugreek / lactation — PMID 23712493** — systematic review found limited evidence for increased milk volume, with methodological weaknesses and variable preparations. Pregnancy/lactation safety requires separate review.
34. **Fenugreek / glycemia — PMID 23483826** — systematic review found possible reductions in glucose but evidence quality and preparation varied. Keep T2DM endpoint specificity and avoid recommendation-level dosing.
35. **Moringa / glycemic control — PMID 32891299** — systematic review found limited human evidence for glycemic effects and substantial variability in preparations. Human evidence must remain separate from animal/mechanistic studies.
36. **Moringa / lipid profile — PMID 34217503** — human trial evidence remains limited and heterogeneous. Preserve product identity and avoid treating preclinical lipid findings as clinical efficacy.
37. **Nigella sativa / type 2 diabetes — PMID 31548990** — meta-analysis reported improvements in some glycemic outcomes but heterogeneous interventions and limited trial quality. Preserve seed/oil/extract distinctions.
38. **Nigella sativa / metabolic syndrome — PMID 28673472** — randomized-trial evidence suggested some metabolic-marker changes, but study numbers were small. Keep surrogate outcomes distinct from clinical events.
39. **Black seed / blood pressure — PMID 25248694** — clinical-trial synthesis reported possible BP reductions with substantial heterogeneity. Preserve preparation and baseline-hypertension status.
40. **Grape seed extract / blood pressure — PMID 23196448** — meta-analysis found modest BP effects in some populations. Preserve extract standardization and endpoint-specific certainty.
41. **Pycnogenol / endothelial function — PMID 19577412** — randomized evidence examined endothelial and cardiovascular biomarkers; surrogate outcomes should not be converted into event-prevention claims.
42. **Grape seed extract / metabolic outcomes — PMID 29205182** — systematic review found mixed metabolic effects across biomarkers. Preserve intervention identity and null endpoints.
43. **Hibiscus / hypertension — PMID 19877098** — randomized clinical evidence suggested modest BP reductions in adults with elevated BP. Keep beverage/preparation identity and short-duration evidence explicit.
44. **Hibiscus / cardiometabolic markers — PMID 24617892** — human trials suggested changes in BP and lipids, but populations and preparations varied. Do not generalize tea evidence to concentrated extracts.
45. **Green tea / body weight — PMID 21479771** — systematic review/meta-analysis found small weight-related effects with high variation across catechin/caffeine products. Preserve magnitude and avoid “fat burner” framing.
46. **Green tea extract / liver injury — PMID 28417035** — systematic review of hepatotoxicity evidence supports a safety warning for concentrated extracts; risk appears different from ordinary brewed tea. Keep safety field prominent.
47. **Turmeric/curcumin / osteoarthritis — PMID 30309580** — systematic review/meta-analysis reported symptom improvements in knee OA but emphasized formulation and trial limitations. Culinary turmeric must not be treated as equivalent to tested curcumin products.
48. **Curcumin / inflammatory markers — PMID 30709537** — meta-analysis found changes in selected inflammatory biomarkers, but surrogate outcomes and heterogeneous formulations limit clinical interpretation.
49. **Boswellia / osteoarthritis — PMID 25705405** — randomized evidence suggested possible pain/function improvement, but extracts and trial durations varied. Preserve standardized-extract identity.
50. **Devil's claw / osteoarthritis and low-back pain — PMID 17348898** — systematic review found some evidence for pain reduction with devil's claw preparations, but trial quality and extract standardization limit certainty. Preserve indication and formulation boundaries.

## Disposition

All 50 remain **reconciliation candidates/evidence receipts**, not promoted claims. Historical and contemporary evidence are deliberately separated where possible. No canonical herb profile, recommendation, monetization content, generated runtime evidence, indexability state, or public-facing scientific claim was mutated by this batch.

## Research integrity notes

- Preparation/formulation identity is retained whenever it changes interpretation.
- Prevention is not treated as treatment.
- Surrogate biomarkers are not treated as clinical outcomes.
- Animal/mechanistic evidence is not promoted to human efficacy.
- Safety signals remain independent of efficacy claims.
- Null and insufficient-evidence findings are retained as first-class counterweights.
- Studied doses are evidence metadata only, not consumer recommendations.
- Drug-interaction concerns remain attached to relevant botanicals, particularly St John's wort, licorice, ginkgo, green-tea extract and other concentrated products.
