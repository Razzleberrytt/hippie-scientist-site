# Research enrichment Waves 1401–1425 — 2026-10-04

Seventeenth pass of the Waves 1001–1500 program, emphasizing vitamin C/E efficacy, safety, deficiency risk, bioavailability, critical-care null/harm evidence, and biomarker-versus-clinical-outcome boundaries.

## Waves 1401–1425
1. **iv-vitamin-c-nasopharyngeal-chemotherapy — PMID 41973719** — Randomized double-blind trial in nasopharyngeal-cancer chemoradiotherapy found intravenous vitamin C did not reduce gastrointestinal treatment toxicities, although platelet-count decline was attenuated; supportive hematologic signal without broad toxicity reduction.
2. **vitamin-c-deficiency-scurvy-vulnerability — PMID 42514284** — Systematic review/meta-analysis of contemporary scurvy in high-income countries found cases clustered in vulnerable people with restrictive diets, neurodevelopmental disorders, medical complexity and psychosocial risk; useful deficiency-risk context rather than supplementation efficacy.
3. **ayurvedic-formulation-vitamin-ce-vascular — PMID 42195225** — Twelve-month randomized trial in high-cardiovascular-risk adults found an Ayurvedic herbal formulation improved endothelium-independent dilation versus placebo, while vitamin C/E did not clearly outperform placebo on the main vascular endpoints; multi-arm comparison does not support a C/E vascular benefit.
4. **high-dose-iv-vitamin-c-severe-burns — PMID 42267875** — Multicenter phase-3 VICTORY trial in severe burns was stopped early for futility/harm: high-dose intravenous vitamin C did not improve discharge or organ outcomes and the primary mortality/persistent-organ-dysfunction composite numerically favored placebo.
5. **high-dose-iv-vitamin-c-cardiac-arrest — PMID 42301311** — Multicenter randomized VITaCCA trial found intravenous vitamin C did not improve post-cardiac-arrest organ dysfunction; the 10-g dose produced worse R-SOFA change, renal function, troponin release and neurological outcomes than placebo.
6. **ascorbate-rich-fruit-iron-bioavailability — PMID 42349680** — Systematic review/meta-analysis of 15 interventions found ascorbate-rich fruits consumed with meals improved ferritin, hemoglobin and transferrin-receptor measures, while direct fractional iron-absorption evidence was not clearly significant; food-matrix evidence should not be reduced to isolated vitamin C dosing.
7. **vitamin-c-t2d-proteomics — PMID 42373594** — Small double-blind crossover T2D trial found four months of vitamin C altered multiple plasma proteins and pathways related to innate immunity and metabolism; proteomic changes were mechanistic and do not by themselves establish clinical benefit.
8. **iv-vitamin-c-sepsis-umbrella — PMID 42384633** — Umbrella review of 31 sepsis/septic-shock reviews found vitamin-C combination regimens did not reduce mortality and hemodynamic improvements were modest; monotherapy mortality signals were low-to-moderate certainty and highly context-dependent.
9. **iv-vitamin-c-kidney-biopsy — PMID 42399956** — Large randomized trial in 710 CKD patients undergoing kidney biopsy found perioperative intravenous vitamin C did not reduce perirenal hematoma size, pain or bleeding complications, arguing against routine prophylactic use in this setting.
10. **high-dose-vitamin-c-severe-covid-mortality — PMID 42446912** — Meta-analysis of eight randomized COVID-19 trials involving 2,104 patients found no significant mortality reduction with high-dose vitamin C overall; study-level heterogeneity prevents a definitive benefit claim.
11. **fruit-vegetable-berry-powder-cardiovascular — PMID 42738920** — Small open-label randomized trial in older adults found a commercial fruit/vegetable/berry powder was metabolically tolerated and improved selected oxidative and blood-pressure markers, but several effects attenuated after covariate adjustment and the formulation is proprietary.
12. **iv-vitamin-c-sepsis-meta-analysis — PMID 42761050** — Updated meta-analysis of 14 sepsis/septic-shock RCTs found a mortality signal for intravenous vitamin C, but evidence quality was low and the apparent benefit was driven by single-center rather than multicenter trials; larger low-bias trials remain inconclusive.
13. **oral-vitamin-c-clonal-cytopenia — PMID 42764731** — Phase-2 placebo-controlled EVITA trial found oral vitamin C did not change the primary clonal-growth endpoint in CCUS/lower-risk myeloid disease; exploratory cytokine, serious-adverse-event and survival signals require phase-3 confirmation.
14. **vitamin-e-diabetic-neuropathy — PMID 41528693** — Meta-analysis of five RCTs found tocotrienol-rich vitamin E produced small improvements in selected nerve-conduction velocities in diabetic neuropathy, while nerve amplitudes and HbA1c were unchanged; functional pain/gait outcomes remain insufficient.
15. **fermented-milk-fat-soluble-vitamin-bioavailability — PMID 41765411** — Randomized crossover study in 20 healthy men found co-ingestion with non-fat fermented milk increased short-term bioavailability markers for vitamins A, D, E and K versus water; this is a food-matrix absorption finding, not evidence of superior clinical outcomes.
16. **vitamin-e-oral-mucositis — PMID 41886966** — Systematic review/meta-analysis of 14 trials/715 patients found vitamin E was associated with less severe antineoplastic-treatment oral mucositis, with similar direction in pediatric and adult subgroups; formulation and administration differences across trials still matter.
17. **vitamin-e-cerebrovascular-function — PMID 41964896** — Systematic review found no qualifying human clinical studies of vitamin E on cerebrovascular function; positive effects came entirely from heterogeneous preclinical models, so human cerebrovascular benefit is unestablished.
18. **vitamin-e-nafld-active-comparator — PMID 42039690** — Randomized pilot comparing ketotifen with vitamin E in NAFLD found ketotifen produced greater improvements on several steatosis, glycemic and inflammatory measures; the active-comparator design does not establish vitamin E efficacy against placebo.
19. **vitamin-e-masld-lifestyle-comparison — PMID 42237829** — Three-arm randomized MASLD study found vitamin E and saroglitazar were not significantly better than lifestyle intervention alone for liver-stiffness, fibrosis-score or most secondary outcomes over 24 weeks.
20. **vitamin-e-meal-sequence-bioavailability — PMID 42435810** — Human pharmacokinetic study found vitamin E absorption depended on meal timing/second-meal fat exposure and was reduced in women with obesity/MASLD; evidence informs absorption physiology rather than supplement efficacy.
21. **vegan-multinutrient-omega3-vitd-status — PMID 42480243** — Four-month double-blind RCT in healthy vegans found a multinutrient plus omega-3 supplement increased omega-3 index and 25-OH-vitamin D, while doubling the omega-3/vitamin-D dose conferred no significant additional biomarker advantage; clinical endpoints were not tested.
22. **tocotrienol-influenza-vaccine-response — PMID 42579941** — Randomized placebo-controlled trial in 150 healthy adults found palm tocotrienol-rich fraction increased influenza-vaccine antibody and selected Th1/antioxidant responses across doses; immune-marker enhancement does not establish lower infection risk.
23. **nutritional-supplements-covid-network — PMID 42582094** — Network meta-analysis of COVID-19 nutrition RCTs found different supplements affected different inflammatory markers with mostly low or very-low certainty; heterogeneous interventions and surrogate outcomes preclude a single best-supplement recommendation.
24. **supplements-preeclampsia-network — PMID 42397815** — Network meta-analysis suggested calcium, vitamin D and several multi-nutrient combinations may reduce preeclampsia risk, but indirect ranking evidence and combination regimens prevent attribution to vitamin C/E or any single ingredient.
25. **fat-soluble-vitamins-breastfeeding-status — PMID 42176989** — Secondary analysis of a postpartum randomized cohort found maternal serum vitamins A, E and K and breastmilk A/E were generally adequate while vitamin-D sufficiency was less consistent; this informs postpartum status and milk transfer rather than a supplementation effect.
