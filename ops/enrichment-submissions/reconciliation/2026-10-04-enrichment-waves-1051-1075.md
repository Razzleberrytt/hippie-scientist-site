# Research enrichment Waves 1051–1075 — 2026-10-04

Third pass of the Waves 1001–1500 deep-enrichment program, with a deliberate safety/interactions emphasis.

## Operating rule
Every PMID was screened against merged enrichment waves, the pending 501–1000 branch, and the active 1001–1050 branch. Product-integrity, interaction, pediatric, high-dose, oncology and disease-specific safety evidence remains separated from direct efficacy evidence. Null and adverse findings remain first-class.

## Waves 1051–1075
1. **natural-health-product-drug-interactions — PMID 42105017** — New Zealand general-population analysis found substantial concurrent natural-health-product and medicine use, including potential interactions involving fish oil, coenzyme Q10, turmeric and glucosamine; one fish-oil/warfarin combination was rated potentially significant, and coding of supplement exposures was often incomplete.
2. **melatonin — PMID 41481289** — Systematic review of melatonin use in children age 0-6 found rising prescribing, extended use and overdose exposures; short trials in neurologic conditions suggested sleep-onset benefit with few adverse events, but long-term outcomes and efficacy in typically developing young children were absent.
3. **cancer-supportive-supplements — PMID 41482855** — Umbrella review covering 52 reviews, 250 RCTs and 18 supplements found most evidence low/critically-low quality, with only selected moderate-certainty supportive-care benefits and dose-related or serious adverse events for some vitamins/minerals; broad category evidence only.
4. **vitamin-d — PMID 41737411** — Twenty-study/1,737-participant GDM meta-analysis found improvements in glycemic markers and several pregnancy/neonatal outcomes with vitamin D, but substantial heterogeneity and methodological limitations make these exploratory disease-specific findings.
5. **vitamin-d — PMID 42021229** — Pulmonary-tuberculosis meta-analysis found higher sputum-smear conversion and fewer adverse events with adjunctive vitamin D but no significant culture-conversion benefit; outcome-specific certainty and route/duration heterogeneity prevent a broad TB-treatment claim.
6. **vitamin-d — PMID 42189368** — Critical ASD review found inconsistent observational status data and mixed RCT results; only one high-dose trial showed broad symptom improvements while other trials were marginal/null, leading the authors to reject vitamin D as a general ASD-targeted intervention.
7. **vitamin-d — PMID 42272678** — Umbrella review of 21 asthma syntheses found a possible exacerbation-risk reduction with vitamin D but inconsistent effects on symptom control, lung function and inflammatory outcomes; evidence certainty was frequently low/very low and primary-study overlap was substantial.
8. **vitamin-d — PMID 42293210** — Eight-RCT/617-participant hemodialysis meta-analysis found lower CRP and iPTH and slightly higher albumin with nutritional vitamin D, with no significant increase in overall adverse reactions, hypercalcemia or hyperphosphatemia; dialysis-specific evidence only.
9. **vitamin-d — PMID 42320168** — Systematic review of 11 high-dose vitamin-D RCTs in multiple sclerosis found serum-level and immunologic changes with rare transient calcium disturbances, but clinical and MRI benefits were inconsistent; high-dose safety conclusions require monitored trial context.
10. **vitamin-d — PMID 42389475** — Umbrella review of nine tuberculosis systematic reviews found consistently null primary outcomes for culture conversion, time to conversion and mortality, with subgroup signals considered hypothesis-generating; strong counterweight to routine adjunctive vitamin-D claims in unselected TB.
11. **vitamin-d — PMID 42528624** — Umbrella review in diabetic kidney disease found low-certainty reductions in surrogate urinary albumin/protein markers but no consistent benefit for eGFR, creatinine, kidney replacement therapy, mortality or cardiovascular events, with safety inadequately reported.
12. **omega3-vitamin-d-chemotherapy — PMID 42588106** — Systematic review of 10 prospective breast-cancer chemotherapy trials found inconsistent efficacy across DHA/EPA combinations with or without vitamin D/GLA, no consistent treatment-response or survival benefit, and low-to-very-low certainty; co-supplementation prevents ingredient-level attribution.
13. **supplement-doping-risk — PMID 42639281** — Structured sports-nutrition review identified highest contamination/ADRV risk in multi-ingredient pre-workouts, weight-loss, muscle-building and testosterone-boosting products and emphasized third-party certification, product verification and documentation; product-quality/safety infrastructure evidence only.
14. **vitamin-d — PMID 42704968** — Five-year randomized Finnish trial in 2,495 largely vitamin-D-sufficient older adults found neither 1,600 nor 3,200 IU/day reduced fracture risk; the lower-dose arm had more fractures than placebo, directly arguing against high-dose fracture-prevention claims in replete populations.
15. **vitamin-c — PMID 42246021** — Fourteen-study hemodialysis meta-analysis found modest hemoglobin/TSAT improvements and lower weight-adjusted ESA requirements with ascorbic acid, but certainty was low, heterogeneity high, and long-term safety/patient-centered outcomes unestablished.
16. **antioxidants-breast-cancer — PMID 42123930** — Systematic review of nutritional antioxidants in breast cancer highlighted a double-edged interaction with therapy: selected supplements may reduce toxicities, while antioxidant use during oxidative cytotoxic treatment may theoretically or observationally compromise treatment effects; broad mechanistic/clinical category evidence.
17. **supplement-contamination-sport — PMID 41829906** — Forensic systematic review/meta-analysis linked supplement contamination to a meaningful share of unintentional anti-doping violations and described cardiovascular risks from performance-enhancing substances; contamination estimates and nutrition alternatives remain sport-regulatory context rather than product efficacy evidence.
18. **supplement-adulteration — PMID 41908212** — Systematic review of 44 empirical studies estimated roughly 9-15% of tested supplements contained prohibited or undeclared pharmacological substances, especially stimulants/anabolic agents in pre-workout, weight-loss and muscle-building products; product-integrity safety evidence only.
19. **creatine — PMID 42124755** — Systematic review of five adolescent/youth creatine studies found no consistent short-term renal, hepatic or cardiometabolic safety signals or serious attributed adverse events, but the evidence base was small and lacked long standardized follow-up.
20. **caffeine — PMID 42033594** — Meta-analysis of 48 athlete studies found higher odds of headache, abdominal discomfort, tachycardia, insomnia, anxiety, nervousness and increased urination with acute caffeine, with several side effects increasing with dose; safety effects remain distinct from ergogenic benefit.
21. **caffeine — PMID 42588143** — Systematic review/meta-analysis in adolescent athletes found only a small uncertain acute physical-performance benefit, sparse cognitive evidence and inconsistent adverse-event reporting; current evidence did not support routine caffeine use in youth sport.
22. **omega-3 — PMID 41692069** — Thirteen-study hemodialysis review found selected CRP reductions with omega-3 formulations but substantial heterogeneity and poor adverse-event reporting; reported events were mostly mild, while optimal formulation/composition remains uncertain.
23. **saw-palmetto — PMID 42157952** — Systematic review of recent Serenoa repens evidence found symptom improvements in some BPH/LUTS studies and generally favorable tolerability, but extract/formulation, combination-treatment and study-design heterogeneity substantially limited comparability.
24. **antioxidants-dietary-therapies-epilepsy — PMID 41601892** — Umbrella meta-analysis found dietary therapies such as low-glycemic-index treatment reduced seizure frequency while antioxidant adjuncts did not show significant efficacy; adverse events increased overall, especially with ketogenic diets, so this remains broad category evidence.
25. **thiamin — PMID 41277404** — Six-RCT/438-participant septic-shock meta-analysis found lower in-hospital mortality and renal-replacement-therapy use with thiamin; critically ill population and treatment-context boundaries prevent extrapolation to general supplementation.
