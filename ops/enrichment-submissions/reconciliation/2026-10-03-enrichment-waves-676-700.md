# Research enrichment Waves 676–700 — 2026-10-03

Eighth pass of the Waves 501–1000 program.

## Waves 676–700
1. **herb-drug-interactions — PMID 38504455** — 2024 30-year historical assessment of clinically relevant botanical-drug interactions; mechanism and clinical-evidence strength remain interaction-specific.
2. **herb-drug-interactions — PMID 30660822** — Systematic review/meta-analysis of clinical herb-drug interaction studies; clinically important St John's wort, echinacea and green-tea interactions illustrate transporter/enzyme-specific risk.
3. **natural-product-drug-interactions — PMID 16175124** — Systematic review of 47 clinical pharmacokinetic interaction trials; strongest evidence centered on St John's wort, with limited inference for most botanicals.
4. **botanical-drug-interactions — PMID 26438626** — Clinical/pharmacokinetic review highlights major gaps between in-vitro predictions and human interaction trials; preclinical enzyme effects must not be treated as proven clinical interactions.
5. **green-tea-extract — PMID 32140423** — USP comprehensive hepatotoxicity review; concentrated extract, fasting/bolus exposure and interindividual susceptibility are key context, distinct from brewed tea.
6. **green-tea-extract — PMID 27188915** — Systematic review of 34 RCTs found few mostly mild liver-enzyme adverse events and no serious hepatic events; useful RCT counterweight to case-report hepatotoxicity evidence.
7. **turmeric-curcumin — PMID 32656820** — Italian phytovigilance plus systematic case review linked high-bioavailability turmeric/curcumin supplements with acute hepatitis; concomitant exposures and case-report causality limitations retained.
8. **green-tea-extract — PMID 29580974** — Systematic safety review of 159 human intervention studies; hepatic risk concentrated in high-bolus catechin-rich extracts and differed from brewed tea/food exposures.
9. **turmeric-curcumin — PMID 42364655** — 2026 USP safety review concluded clinically apparent hepatotoxicity is rare but real enough to justify cautionary labeling; idiosyncratic/genetic susceptibility and concomitant products retained.
10. **kava — PMID 16011454** — Systematic kava safety review covering hepatotoxicity, adverse effects, interactions and preparation issues; product/extract type and causality uncertainty remain explicit.
11. **kratom — PMID 41803068** — 2026 systematic review of 31 studies/32 liver-injury cases; predominantly cholestatic injury, frequent incomplete exposure data/polysubstance use, and several transplant cases; case evidence does not establish incidence.
12. **ashwagandha — PMID 42367407** — 2026 scoping review of 25 reported liver-injury patients; cholestatic/mixed patterns predominated, with case-series evidence and product/concomitant-exposure limitations.
13. **st-johns-wort — PMID 15231618** — Systematic review of prospective clinical trials found repeated reductions in systemic bioavailability of conventional drugs during St John's wort exposure; interaction evidence is extract/hyperforin dependent.
14. **st-johns-wort — PMID 17010103** — 31-study CYP3A systematic review; high-hyperforin extracts consistently induced CYP3A while low-hyperforin extracts showed less/no effect, making constituent standardization central.
15. **ginkgo-biloba — PMID 21923430** — 18-RCT/1,985-participant meta-analysis of hemostasis outcomes found no clear increased bleeding-risk signal with standardized Ginkgo extract—counterweight to bleeding case reports.
16. **licorice — PMID 28660884** — 18-study meta-analysis linked chronic glycyrrhizic-acid-containing licorice exposure with increased BP and reduced potassium/renin/aldosterone; cardiovascular-risk context retained.
17. **ginkgo-biloba — PMID 40198642** — 2025 clinical interaction/coagulation analysis found associations between Ginkgo-drug interactions, bleeding symptoms and abnormal coagulation, with medication-specific patterns.
18. **black-cohosh — PMID 21228727** — Meta-analysis of five randomized trials of isopropanolic black-cohosh extract found no adverse liver-function effect, an important formulation-specific counterweight to spontaneous reports.
19. **herb-drug-interactions — PMID 19719333** — Updated systematic review of seven popular herbs documented extensive St John's wort interactions and more limited evidence for ginkgo, ginseng, garlic, echinacea, saw palmetto and kava.
20. **black-cohosh — PMID 21677326** — Causality-focused survey of purported black-cohosh liver-injury cases found major case-quality limitations and no confirmed causality under liver-specific assessment methods.
21. **medicinal-edible-fruit-safety — PMID 38851470** — 2024 systematic review of medicinal/edible fruit adverse events; serious events were present and were often associated with excess exposure or young children; species identity preserved.
22. **adaptogen-heavy-metals — PMID 41300272** — 2025 analytical safety study documented lead/nickel contamination in tested adaptogenic supplements, demonstrating product-quality risk rather than intrinsic botanical pharmacology.
23. **herb-induced-liver-injury — PMID 34307603** — Systematic review/meta-analysis of 936 reported HILI cases across 79 herbs/products; causality and incidence limitations retained while high-frequency implicated products are mapped.
24. **supplement-liver-injury — PMID 36515339** — Pooled analysis of 428 herbal/dietary-supplement liver-injury cases; severe outcomes occurred, but case-report aggregation cannot estimate population incidence.
25. **black-cohosh — PMID 12544678** — Systematic safety review found clinical adverse events generally rare/mild/reversible while serious spontaneous reports existed with uncertain causality; historical safety context retained.
