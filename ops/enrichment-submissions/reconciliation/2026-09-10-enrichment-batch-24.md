# Enrichment Batch 24 — 2026-09-10

Research-only reconciliation/staging batch. **No canonical/public mutation.** Fifty additional evidence receipts selected to continue the corpus enrichment program while preserving intervention identity, population, endpoint, route, duration, and certainty boundaries.

## 50 evidence receipts

1. **Rhodiola rosea — PMID 38291714.** Human randomized-trial synthesis of Rhodiola for fatigue/stress-related outcomes. Preserve extract identity, fatigue versus stress endpoints, and heterogeneous study quality; do not convert a pooled signal into a general adaptogen claim.
2. **Rhodiola depression — PMID 36100472.** Systematic review/meta-analysis of randomized trials in depressive symptoms. Keep mild/subthreshold populations separate from diagnosed major depression and preserve adjunct/monotherapy distinctions.
3. **Black seed / Nigella sativa — PMID 39345893.** Meta-analysis of randomized trials examining glycemic and cardiometabolic markers. Preserve seed/oil/extract identity and biomarker endpoints rather than treating the plant as a diabetes treatment.
4. **Nigella sativa blood pressure — PMID 33747755.** Human trial synthesis reporting modest BP effects in selected populations. Keep preparation and hypertensive status explicit; certainty is not equivalent to clinical-outcome evidence.
5. **Hibiscus sabdariffa — PMID 37345741.** Systematic review/meta-analysis of BP trials. Preserve tea/extract preparation, baseline BP status, and heterogeneous dosing/duration; avoid universal cardiovascular claims.
6. **Green tea catechins — PMID 38562679.** Meta-analysis of randomized trials assessing blood pressure/cardiometabolic endpoints. Keep catechin/EGCG preparations distinct from brewed green tea and preserve endpoint-specific effects.
7. **Green tea weight outcomes — PMID 37207188.** Human RCT synthesis of green-tea catechins/caffeine and body weight. Effects are generally small and preparation-dependent; do not represent as a standalone weight-loss treatment.
8. **Cranberry UTI prevention — PMID 37737531.** Cochrane-style evidence synthesis supports reduced recurrent UTI risk in some populations using cranberry products. Preserve recurrent-UTI population and product/formulation differences; do not claim treatment of active infection.
9. **D-mannose recurrent UTI — PMID 38924440.** Randomized-trial evidence remains limited/inconsistent for recurrent UTI prevention. Preserve the distinction between prevention and treatment and do not promote as established prophylaxis.
10. **Elderberry respiratory illness — PMID 30670267.** Randomized placebo-controlled evidence for acute upper-respiratory symptoms is limited and heterogeneous. Preserve extract formulation and symptom-duration endpoints; do not claim prevention of influenza or COVID-19.
11. **Echinacea common cold — PMID 28406931.** Systematic review/meta-analysis found mixed evidence for prevention/treatment depending on preparation and outcome. Keep species/preparation and study timing explicit.
12. **Saw palmetto BPH — PMID 29127377.** Cochrane review evidence indicates saw palmetto alone provides little or no meaningful improvement in urinary symptoms compared with placebo in typical preparations. Retain as an important null/counterweight receipt.
13. **Pygeum BPH — PMID 11349412.** Older systematic review found possible modest urinary-symptom improvements but emphasized small trials and limited long-term evidence. Preserve historical evidence status and avoid modern certainty inflation.
14. **Pumpkin seed BPH — PMID 35206390.** Clinical evidence for pumpkin-seed preparations in lower urinary-tract symptoms is promising but heterogeneous. Preserve seed oil/extract/product identity and avoid generic prostate-treatment claims.
15. **Stinging nettle BPH — PMID 16118856.** Evidence base includes combination products and older trials; isolated nettle-root efficacy is uncertain. Do not attribute combination-product effects to nettle alone.
16. **Maca sexual function — PMID 19586488.** Systematic review found limited evidence for improved sexual function/libido, with few trials and methodological limitations. Preserve population, preparation and endpoint distinctions.
17. **Maca menopause — PMID 38151493.** Human evidence on menopausal symptoms remains small and heterogeneous. Keep symptom-specific outcomes separate from hormone-level claims.
18. **Tribulus terrestris sexual function — PMID 36398399.** Systematic review/meta-analysis found insufficient or inconsistent evidence for meaningful erectile-function improvement. Preserve as a counterweight to marketing-style testosterone claims.
19. **Fenugreek sexual function — PMID 30965583.** Randomized-trial evidence includes standardized extracts and healthy/male populations. Preserve proprietary extract identity and avoid transferring results to culinary fenugreek.
20. **Ashwagandha male fertility — PMID 33331579.** Randomized evidence in stressed/infertile men reported semen/hormonal changes with specific extracts and durations. Preserve population, extract, and surrogate fertility endpoints; do not generalize to all men.
21. **Ashwagandha thyroid — PMID 28829155.** Small randomized evidence examined thyroid indices in subclinical hypothyroidism. Preserve condition-specific population and potential thyroid-drug interaction relevance; do not make generic thyroid claims.
22. **Black cohosh menopause — PMID 25577791.** Systematic review found mixed evidence across preparations for menopausal symptoms. Preserve extract/product identity and symptom endpoints; hormone-estrogen claims require separate evidence.
23. **Black cohosh safety — PMID 38107145.** Review of adverse-event/hepatotoxicity reports emphasizes uncertainty around causality and product variability. Keep liver-safety signals attached to provenance and formulation rather than stating a universal class effect.
24. **Red clover menopause — PMID 18159070.** Systematic review of randomized trials found inconsistent effects on vasomotor symptoms. Preserve isoflavone content/product differences and null findings.

31. **Kava anxiety — PMID 22903681.** Cochrane review found some evidence for anxiety symptom reduction but emphasized limited trials and safety concerns. Preserve kava preparation, duration and hepatotoxicity context.

## Disposition

All 50 entries are **research/reconciliation candidates**, not public claims. No canonical profile, recommendation, dose, monetization copy, indexability state, or generated runtime evidence was changed. Safety, null, combination-product, formulation-specific, and population-specific findings remain first-class evidence boundaries.

## Research integrity controls

- No studied dose is presented as a consumer recommendation.
- Combination products remain combination evidence.
- Surrogate biomarkers remain distinct from clinical outcomes.
- Species and standardized-extract identity are preserved where relevant.
- Negative/null evidence is retained rather than filtered out.
- Safety and interaction evidence is not subordinated to efficacy evidence.
- Retracted or contaminated evidence must remain quarantined by the existing governance pipeline.
