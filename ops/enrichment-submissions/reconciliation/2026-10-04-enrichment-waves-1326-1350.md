# Research enrichment Waves 1326–1350 — 2026-10-04

Fourteenth pass of the Waves 1001–1500 program, emphasizing multivitamin/COSMOS outcomes, B vitamins, folate forms, prenatal micronutrients, iron-deficiency treatment comparisons, and high-value null/safety evidence.

## Waves 1326–1350
1. **multivitamin-hypertension-cosmos — PMID 41264477** — Large COSMOS secondary analysis found daily multivitamin-multimineral supplementation did not reduce incident hypertension or two-year blood-pressure change overall; diet-quality and baseline-BP subgroup signals were exploratory and directionally inconsistent.
2. **vitamin-e-cognition — PMID 41418497** — Systematic review of 43 clinical studies found the vitamin-E/cognition literature was dominated by dietary exposure and multivitamin studies, with very little isolated-supplement evidence; apparent protective associations may reflect dietary or multi-component synergy rather than vitamin E alone.
3. **multivitamin-fat-soluble-deficiency — PMID 41565231** — Four-week double-blind RCT in 155 adults with vitamin A, D or E deficiency found nutrition education plus multivitamin was not superior to nutrition education plus placebo for improving vitamin concentrations or deficiency rates; both groups improved substantially.
4. **multivitamin-carotenoid-tocopherol-biomarkers — PMID 41587736** — Two-year COSMOS biomarker substudy found multivitamin supplementation increased total carotenoids, lutein, beta-carotene and alpha-tocopherol concentrations, while several other carotenoids were unchanged; this demonstrates exposure/biomarker effects rather than clinical benefit.
5. **multivitamin-cocoa-vte — PMID 41651011** — Large COSMOS ancillary analysis found neither cocoa extract nor multivitamin-multimineral supplementation significantly reduced venous thromboembolism, DVT or pulmonary embolism in intention-to-treat analyses; per-protocol and latency patterns were exploratory.
6. **multivitamin-epigenetic-aging — PMID 41803341** — Prespecified COSMOS ancillary study found small two-year changes favoring multivitamin supplementation on PCGrimAge and PCPhenoAge epigenetic clocks, while cocoa extract was null; effect sizes were small and clinical relevance remains uncertain.
7. **multivitamin-metabolomics-cosmos — PMID 41910928** — COSMOS metabolomics substudy found nominal multivitamin-associated changes in DHA, omega-3, creatinine and multiple disease-risk scores, but none survived false-discovery-rate correction; findings are preliminary rather than validated metabolic benefits.
8. **prenatal-5mthf-folic-acid — PMID 41971363** — Twenty-four-week randomized prenatal trial found 6S-5-MTHF maintained total maternal/fetal folate similarly to folic acid while producing less detectable unmetabolized folic acid in maternal blood and placenta; clinical developmental superiority was not tested.
9. **cocoa-multivitamin-physical-performance — PMID 42004609** — COSMOS clinic substudy found neither cocoa extract nor multivitamin supplementation improved two-year grip strength, walking speed, SPPB, balance, chair-stand or timed-up-and-go outcomes in older adults.
10. **organic-synthetic-multivitamin-bioavailability — PMID 42005784** — Single-blind randomized comparison found organically sourced and synthetic multivitamin-mineral formulations produced broadly comparable acute and 30-day circulating micronutrient profiles, providing no evidence of general bioavailability superiority for the organic formulation.
11. **multivitamin-covid-cosmos — PMID 42385272** — COSMOS secondary analysis found daily multivitamin supplementation did not significantly reduce COVID-19 incidence in intention-to-treat analysis; a symptomatic-COVID signal appeared only in adherent per-protocol analysis and remains secondary.
12. **prenatal-biotin-multivitamin — PMID 42693884** — Twenty-four-week double-blind prenatal trial found a higher-biotin multivitamin increased maternal and cord-blood biotin biomarkers and reduced a marginal-insufficiency metabolite; cortisol associations were exploratory and no maternal or infant clinical benefit was established.
13. **vitamin-d-b12-psychiatric-evidence — PMID 42187879** — Exploratory systematic review/meta-analysis found highly heterogeneous psychiatric evidence for vitamin D and B12; vitamin-D signals attenuated toward null after bias adjustment and B12 supplementation evidence was sparse/null, supporting targeted deficiency assessment rather than routine supplementation.
14. **vitamin-b12-chemotherapy-neuropathy — PMID 42169581** — Small double-blind placebo-controlled trial in 40 gynecologic-cancer patients found oral vitamin B12 reduced the incidence of questionnaire-defined chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy, while neurotoxicity quality-of-life scores and adverse effects did not differ; replication is needed.
15. **melatonin-vitamind-b12-postoperative-chronotherapy — PMID 42230417** — Tiny randomized crossover dental-surgery pilot replacing nighttime ibuprofen with melatonin, vitamin D and B12 did not improve overall recovery and produced higher pain/swelling on several measures; sex-interaction findings were exploratory and the multi-ingredient design prevents attribution.
16. **excess-folic-acid-pregnancy-safety — PMID 42248048** — Systematic review associated excessive folic-acid intake or high blood folate during preconception/pregnancy with gestational diabetes and selected offspring/growth outcomes, but evidence was heterogeneous and largely association-based; it does not establish a causal toxic threshold.
17. **vitamin-b12-route-deficiency-anemia — PMID 42393020** — Randomized trial in 153 adults with B12-deficiency anemia found intranasal, intravenous and intramuscular B12 all produced short-term hematologic recovery, with no significant adjusted difference in hemoglobin response by route; some secondary laboratory kinetics differed.
18. **vitamin-b9-b12-fortified-flour-cognition — PMID 42400612** — Six-month double-blind community RCT in 472 Ethiopian teenage girls found B9/B12-fortified corn flour did not improve any primary cognitive outcome versus unfortified flour; a stunting-language interaction was secondary and not evidence of benefit.
19. **high-dose-vitamin-b6-depression — PMID 42569537** — One-month double-blind trial in a mostly female general-population sample found 100 mg/day vitamin B6 reduced depression scores only in preselected participants with elevated baseline symptoms, with no anxiety or stress benefit; subgroup design and high dose require cautious safety-aware interpretation.
20. **folic-acid-methotrexate-toxicity — PMID 42249242** — Exploratory randomized rheumatology trial found increasing folic acid from 5 to 10 mg/week did not meaningfully reduce methotrexate-related liver or other toxicity over 12 weeks; a fatigue difference was isolated and secondary.
21. **probiotic-iron-folic-acid-anemia — PMID 42298087** — Randomized trial in 248 anemic children found adding Lactobacillus plantarum to iron-folic-acid therapy provided no additional benefit for hemoglobin, iron status, morbidity, adverse effects or gut-health markers versus iron-folic acid alone.
22. **lactoferrin-vs-ferrous-sulfate-ida — PMID 42302886** — Large double-blind noninferiority trial in Bangladeshi women with iron-deficiency anemia found both tested bovine-lactoferrin doses were inferior to ferrous sulfate for 12-week hemoglobin recovery, with similar adverse-event rates.
23. **prenatal-multimicronutrient-zinc-status — PMID 42435808** — Randomized pregnancy substudy in Ghana found multiple-micronutrient supplementation containing zinc did not significantly improve late-pregnancy plasma zinc or zinc-deficiency prevalence versus iron-folic acid; zinc status worsened across pregnancy in both groups.
24. **alpha-lipoic-acid-rpl-sperm — PMID 42571056** — Double-blind trial in couples with recurrent pregnancy loss found alpha-lipoic acid plus folic acid reduced sperm DNA-fragmentation index versus folic-acid control, while between-group sperm concentration, motility and morphology differences were not significant; fertility/live-birth benefit was not established.
25. **prenatal-5mthf-vs-folic-acid-bioavailability — PMID 42716224** — Double-blind RCT in 110 pregnant women found equimolar 5-MTHF and folic acid produced comparable RBC-folate gains and homocysteine reductions; plasma 5-MTHF and unmetabolized-folate differences were borderline or nonsignificant after adjustment.
