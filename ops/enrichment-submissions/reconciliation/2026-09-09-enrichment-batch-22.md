# Enrichment Batch 22 — 2026-09-09

Research-only reconciliation/staging batch. **No canonical/public mutation.** Fifty evidence receipts selected to extend the prior enrichment work without reusing Batch 21's exact receipts. Evidence boundaries are preserved: species/identity, formulation, route, population, endpoint, duration, and certainty are not generalized beyond the cited study.

## 50 evidence receipts

1. **Saffron — PMID 41693488** — 34 RCTs/1,769 adults; pooled BDI and BAI improvements but high heterogeneity; clinician-rated HDRS/HARS/POMS outcomes were not significant. Keep self-report vs clinician-rated endpoints separate and preserve moderate GRADE certainty.
2. **Chamomile — PMID 31006899** — 12 randomized/quasi-randomized trials; evidence suggested benefit for GAD and sleep quality, while state-anxiety and insomnia evidence was sparse/null. Do not generalize to all anxiety or insomnia.
3. **Ashwagandha — PMID 41644067** — 22 RCTs; 2026 dose-response meta-analysis reported improvements in stress, depression, and anxiety but substantial heterogeneity and need for better trials. Keep extract identity and dose-response findings study-bound.
4. **Lavender — PMID 31655395** — 65 RCTs plus 25 nonrandomized studies; inhalation and oral Silexan analyses differed by route/product. Preserve route and standardized-extract identity; do not treat aromatherapy evidence as equivalent to oral extract evidence.
5. **Ashwagandha endocrine effects — PMID 41740946** — 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis examined hormonal modulation across randomized trials. Keep HPA/gonadal biomarkers distinct from clinical stress or fertility outcomes.
6. **Saffron depression — PMID 25384672** — clinical systematic review of randomized studies examined depression, extract source, standardization, duration, and safety. Preserve product-specific boundaries rather than treating saffron as a generic antidepressant.
7. **Cinnamon/T2DM — PMID 37818728** — 24 RCTs; updated meta-analysis reported reductions in fasting glucose, HOMA-IR and HbA1c, with insulin not significantly changed. Keep T2DM population and cinnamon preparation identity explicit.
8. **Lavender anxiety/depression — PMID 34775136** — meta-analysis found reductions in anxiety and depression versus placebo/no treatment, but route and participant health state contributed to heterogeneity. Avoid route-free claims.
9. **Saffron mild/moderate depression — PMID 32221179** — 12 studies; saffron improved depressive symptoms versus placebo and was broadly comparable to antidepressants in included trials; adverse-event incidence did not differ significantly. Preserve condition and trial limitations.
10. **Ashwagandha low-dose extract — PMID 42593642** — 2026 randomized double-blind trial in 60 stressed adults tested a proprietary 30 mg/day extract for 28 days. Treat as extract-specific and short-duration evidence; industry affiliation is part of provenance.
11. **Ginseng cognition — PMID 39474788** — systematic review/meta-analysis of RCTs through December 2023 evaluated human cognitive outcomes. Keep ginseng species/preparation and healthy-vs-impaired cognition distinctions explicit; do not infer broad nootropic efficacy from the review title.
12. **Ginger pregnancy nausea — PMID 22951628** — systematic review found four RCTs reporting reduced nausea/vomiting with oral ginger. Pregnancy-specific evidence must not be transferred to other nausea populations without separate evidence.
13. **Garlic hypertension — PMID 25837272** — seven randomized placebo-controlled trials showed reductions in systolic and diastolic BP, while authors called for better-designed long-term trials. Preserve formulation and hypertension population boundaries.
14. **Cocoa flavanols/BP — PMID 19910929** — meta-analysis of 10 RCTs/297 participants examined flavanol-rich cocoa in normotensive, prehypertensive and stage-1 hypertensive adults. Keep cocoa-product identity distinct from isolated flavanol supplements.
15. **Beetroot juice/BP — PMID 29141968** — 22 trials with 47 intervention and 43 control groups; pooled beetroot juice reduced SBP and DBP, with duration/dose subgroup differences. Preserve juice intervention rather than generalizing to beetroot foods.
16. **Echinacea pediatric respiratory infections — PMID 41234257** — 2026 systematic review included five pediatric studies; evidence suggested shorter symptoms and lower antibiotic use but with risk-of-bias concerns. Keep pediatric acute RTI context separate from adult prevention/treatment.
17. **Bacopa cognition — PMID 22747190** — six randomized controlled human trials, all 12 weeks, used three Bacopa extracts at 300–450 mg/day and mainly assessed memory. Do not turn studied doses into recommendations; preserve extract and duration.
18. **Curcumin osteoarthritis — PMID 34331696** — systematic review evaluated randomized trials of curcumin compounds in osteoarthritis, especially knee OA. Preserve compound/formulation and OA-specific symptom endpoints.
19. **Garlic hypertension — PMID 25239480** — nine double-blind trials/482 participants; BP reductions favored garlic but heterogeneity was high. This is a separate evidence receipt from the earlier garlic review and should retain its own study provenance.
20. **Ginger pregnancy nausea umbrella review — PMID 42322087** — 2026 umbrella review/meta-synthesis focused specifically on Zingiber officinale for pregnancy nausea/vomiting. Keep pregnancy population and review-level evidence distinct from general antiemetic claims.
21. **Beetroot nitrate hypertension — PMID 35369064** — seven RCTs/218 hypertensive participants; nitrate-rich beetroot juice reduced SBP but not DBP, with moderate GRADE certainty. Preserve nitrate-rich juice formulation and hypertensive population.
22. **Boswellia osteoarthritis — PMID 38365549** — nine RCTs/712 participants; Boswellia extracts improved pain, stiffness and function measures, with subgroup analysis for standardized Aflapin extract. Do not collapse standardized extracts into all Boswellia products.
23. **Ginger broad clinical evidence — PMID 31935866** — systematic review of 109 RCT papers found comparatively consistent support for pregnancy nausea/vomiting, inflammation, metabolic syndrome, digestive function and colorectal-cancer markers, while other indications were more controversial. Preserve indication-specific boundaries.
24. **Ginger chemotherapy nausea — PMID 38625733** — systematic review/meta-analysis of RCTs examined chemotherapy-induced nausea/vomiting. Keep oncology treatment context separate from pregnancy or postoperative nausea.
25. **Ginseng fatigue — PMID 36730693** — 19 RCTs; pooled overall fatigue reduction was not significant, while ginseng herbal formulas and chronic/general fatigue subgroups showed small effects. Preserve the overall null result and subgroup limitations.
26. **Panax ginseng fatigue — PMID 29624410** — systematic review found 10 eligible studies and modest evidence of efficacy with low reported adverse-event burden. Do not treat “promising” evidence as established treatment efficacy.
27. **Lemon balm — PMID 34449930** — meta-analysis of randomized clinical trials reported improvements in anxiety and depression scores versus placebo without serious adverse effects. Preserve acute-setting emphasis and heterogeneity.
28. **Valerian sleep — PMID 17145239** — 16 randomized placebo-controlled studies/1,093 patients; sleep-quality benefit appeared in a subset, but methodological problems, varying preparations/doses and publication bias limited certainty. Do not make preparation-free sleep claims.
29. **Red ginseng/T2DM — PMID 22139546** — four RCTs; meta-analysis did not convincingly favor red ginseng for fasting glucose or insulin, and evidence was limited by few heterogeneous trials. Retain this as a counterweight to generic ginseng glucose claims.
30. **Curcumin metabolic syndrome — PMID 30941814** — seven RCTs; pooled analyses reported improvements in fasting glucose, triglycerides, HDL and diastolic BP. Keep small evidence base and metabolic-syndrome population boundaries.
31. **Ginseng fatigue/physical performance — PMID 27822924** — meta-analysis examined RCT evidence for fatigue and physical performance. Preserve the distinction between subjective fatigue and objective performance outcomes; no performance recommendation should be inferred from the review alone.
32. **Curcumin glycemic/lipid outcomes — PMID 30156145** — meta-analysis included 26 trials/1,890 participants with metabolic syndrome and related disorders. Keep formulation, disease phenotype and endpoint specificity; do not equate with culinary turmeric.
33. **Turmeric glucose/metabolic syndrome umbrella review — PMID 37471428** — umbrella review plus updated meta-analysis evaluated Curcuma longa and related compounds in T2DM, prediabetes and metabolic syndrome, with preparation-form subgrouping. Preserve preparation identity and overlap among underlying reviews.
34. **Pomegranate juice/BP — PMID 37461211** — 14 clinical trials/573 participants; pooled SBP reduction, with effects varying by duration and dose. Preserve juice-specific intervention and the finding that benefit was not uniform across longer durations.
35. **Centella asiatica cognition/mood — PMID 28878245** — five RCTs of Centella alone plus six of Centella-containing products; no significant cognitive-domain benefit overall, but some short-term alertness/anger signals. Preserve product variation and avoid strong cognition claims.
36. **Flaxseed hypertension — PMID 39526211** — 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis evaluated randomized trials in hypertensive patients. Preserve product type and population; do not transfer results to flaxseed oil/lignan extract without identity matching.
37. **Psyllium lipids — PMID 18985059** — 21 controlled studies/1,717 total participants; psyllium lowered total and LDL cholesterol with dose/time relationships. Keep historical study limitations and dietary-adjunct context.
38. **Peppermint oil/IBS — PMID 9672344** — older meta-analysis of eight RCTs found possible symptom relief but emphasized methodological flaws and insufficient certainty. Preserve this as historical evidence rather than definitive efficacy.
39. **Peppermint oil/IBS updated meta-analysis — PMID 35942669** — 10 RCTs/1,030 patients; peppermint oil improved global IBS symptoms and abdominal pain but adverse events were more frequent and evidence quality was very low. Preserve safety counterweight.
40. **Pomegranate consumption/BP — PMID 38410857** — 22 RCTs; pooled SBP/DBP reductions were accompanied by very high heterogeneity. Keep the heterogeneity explicit and avoid presenting the pooled estimate as a uniform effect.
41. **Fenugreek/T2DM — PMID 37762302** — 10 RCTs/706 participants; fenugreek improved several glycemic and lipid outcomes, while HOMA-IR, LDL and BMI were not significantly changed. Mild GI effects were reported; preserve outcome-specific boundaries.
42. **Psyllium lipids 2025 — PMID 41366295** — 41 RCT articles/2,049 participants; newer meta-analysis found significant LDL and total-cholesterol reductions, with triglyceride and HDL changes not significant. This newer receipt should supersede broad older summaries when appropriate but retain endpoint specificity.
43. **Holy basil stress/mood/sleep — PMID 36185698** — randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial of standardized Ocimum tenuiflorum extract in 100 stressed adults over eight weeks. Preserve standardized extract, dose and adult stressed-population boundaries.
44. **Hawthorn chronic heart failure — PMID 12798455** — 13 qualifying randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trials; eight trials/632 patients contributed to meta-analysis, showing some functional and symptom improvements as adjunctive therapy. Preserve NYHA I–III context and adjunctive-use boundary.
45. **Artichoke lipid profile — PMID 34569671** — 14 studies/15 effect sizes; artichoke extract/juice showed lipid changes, but juice and extract effects differed by outcome. Keep preparation-specific estimates separate.
46. **Aloe vera glycemic control — PMID 27009750** — eight trials/470 patients in prediabetes/T2DM; some glucose and HbA1c improvements were reported, but heterogeneity and preparation standardization limited certainty. Preserve disease-specific evidence and avoid general “blood sugar” claims.
47. **Rosemary cognition — PMID 21877951** — randomized placebo-controlled double-blind crossover study assessed acute cognitive effects of dried rosemary leaf powder in older adults. Treat as a small acute trial, not evidence of long-term cognitive protection.
48. **Olive leaf/pomace cardiometabolic markers — PMID 42303499** — 2026 GRADE-assessed review/meta-analysis included 30 RCTs/1,726 participants and evaluated 21 biomarkers. Preserve olive-leaf versus olive-pomace identity and biomarker-vs-clinical-outcome distinction.
49. **Olive leaf cardiovascular risk — PMID 36271405** — 12 studies/819 participants; olive leaf extract lowered triglycerides and SBP overall, with stronger subgroup effects in hypertension; evidence for glucose, liver, kidney and inflammatory markers was low/very low. Preserve certainty differences.
50. **Olive leaf hypertension RCT — PMID 40990594** — multicenter randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial/621 participants; 12-week olive leaf extract reduced 24-h SBP versus placebo modestly and improved several secondary metabolic markers. Preserve the specific extract, trial duration, population and secondary-endpoint status.

## Disposition

All 50 remain **reconciliation candidates/evidence receipts**, not promoted claims. No canonical herb profile, recommendation, monetization content, generated runtime evidence, indexability state, or public-facing scientific claim was changed by this batch. Receipts should enter the existing governed review/reconciliation path before any canonical promotion.

## Research integrity notes

- Exact species/product identity is retained where the source is formulation-specific.
- Combination interventions are not attributed to single ingredients.
- Surrogate/biomarker outcomes are not promoted to clinical outcomes.
- Population-specific findings remain population-specific.
- Studied doses are recorded only as evidence metadata and are not presented as user recommendations.
- Industry affiliation/proprietary-extract provenance should remain attached during downstream review when applicable.
- Older evidence is retained when useful as a counterweight, but newer systematic reviews should be checked for overlap before promotion.
