# Research enrichment Waves 2076–2100 — deep pass

Fourth abstract-verified pass of the Waves 2001–2500 program.

## Waves 2076–2100
1. **curcumin-turmeric — PMID 41761990** — Curcumin/turmeric supplementation significantly reduced urea but did not significantly change creatinine, BUN, uric acid, or albumin; a creatinine publication-bias signal was detected. Boundary: Evidence certainty ranged from moderate to low and does not establish broad renoprotection. State: `reviewed_receipt_pending_final_admission`.
2. **probiotics — PMID 41066046** — Probiotic supplementation reduced bilirubin at several early time points and shortened phototherapy and hospital duration in pooled analyses. Boundary: Neonatal, short-term evidence requires strain- and population-specific interpretation; long-term efficacy and safety remain uncertain. State: `research_only_population_firewall`.
3. **vitamin-d — PMID 42746921** — Vitamin D supplementation reduced deficiency risk; higher versus lower dosing further reduced deficiency but did not clearly improve osteopenia or bone mineral content. Boundary: Higher-dose regimens probably increased vitamin-D excess, creating a clear dose-safety tradeoff in a vulnerable population. State: `research_only_population_dose_firewall`.
4. **magnesium — PMID 38812090** — Higher dietary magnesium intake was associated with lower depression risk, with an inverse dose-response pattern across observational data. Boundary: Association does not establish antidepressant efficacy of magnesium supplements and remains vulnerable to dietary and lifestyle confounding. State: `research_only_observational`.
5. **curcumin — PMID 40977174** — Curcumin reduced pain more than corticosteroids at one week, but later pain outcomes and clinical lesion improvement were generally comparable. Boundary: High risk of bias and substantial heterogeneity weaken conclusions; delivery formulations and comparator regimens varied. State: `research_only_formulation_evidence_firewall`.
6. **glycine — PMID 41189445** — Five days of oral glycine raised serum glycine but did not reduce alcohol craving, laboratory alcohol consumption, or most subjective alcohol effects versus placebo. Boundary: Direct human null evidence does not support glycine as a stand-alone anti-craving treatment; exploratory craving–consumption dissociation requires replication. State: `reviewed_receipt_pending_final_admission`.
7. **vitamin-b12 — PMID 42144864** — Vitamin B12 levels declined numerically after 6 and 12 months of metformin, but changes were neither statistically nor clinically significant; deficiency estimates differed greatly between RCT and observational data. Boundary: Evidence is sparse and heterogeneous; this informs monitoring risk rather than vitamin-B12 supplementation efficacy. State: `research_only_drug_nutrient_context`.
8. **vitamin-d — PMID 42004696** — Oral vitamin D was associated with small improvements in semen volume, sperm concentration, progressive motility, and testosterone, but not clinical pregnancy or live birth. Boundary: Adverse-event reporting was limited; surrogate semen improvements do not establish improved fertility outcomes, and higher doses did not show added benefit. State: `research_only_population_firewall`.
9. **vitamin-d — PMID 41744130** — Preoperative vitamin D was associated with lower postoperative and symptomatic hypocalcemia and a modestly shorter hospital stay. Boundary: Overall evidence quality was low; this is perioperative treatment-context evidence, not general supplementation guidance. State: `research_only_population_firewall`.
10. **omega-3-rich-grass-finished-lamb — PMID 40690019** — Eating grass-finished versus concentrate-finished lamb increased several plasma omega-3 fatty acids over four weeks, without differences in measured cardiovascular risk factors. Boundary: This is a whole-food matrix intervention and should not be treated as evidence for omega-3 supplement efficacy. State: `research_only_food_matrix_firewall`.
11. **zinc — PMID 42346271** — Overall zinc reduced severe oral-mucositis risk, but route analysis showed the signal was driven by topical mouthwash; systemic oral zinc produced borderline, inconsistent benefit. Boundary: The authors explicitly conclude oral systemic zinc does not provide reliable prophylactic benefit; topical results remain hypothesis-generating due to very limited trials. State: `research_only_route_firewall`.
12. **panax-ginseng — PMID 39428934** — The review catalogued ginsenosides and other constituents alongside broad anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, metabolic, and anticancer mechanisms, but did not provide a focused high-certainty clinical efficacy synthesis. Boundary: Broad mechanistic reviews are useful for identity and mechanism mapping but should not be promoted as proof of clinical benefit. State: `research_only_evidence_quality`.
13. **areds-nutrients — PMID 41984164** — Defined AREDS-style supplementation has evidence for reducing progression from intermediate to late AMD, while broader nutrient and dietary associations remain more mixed. Boundary: Benefits apply to specific AMD risk groups and formulations, not indiscriminate use of lutein, zinc, omega-3, or other nutrients in the general population. State: `research_only_formulation_population_firewall`.
14. **nutraceuticals — PMID 41683750** — Multiple dietary bioactives can interfere with alpha-synuclein aggregation or clearance in experimental systems. Boundary: Poor bioavailability, blood-brain-barrier limitations, and overwhelmingly preclinical evidence prevent clinical neuroprotection claims. State: `research_only_preclinical`.
15. **melatonin — PMID 42556907** — Adjunctive melatonin improved some attachment, pocket-depth, gingival, plaque, and short-term glycemic outcomes at selected time points. Boundary: Prediction intervals crossed the null for key periodontal outcomes and heterogeneity was substantial, so consistent clinical benefit is uncertain. State: `research_only_combination_firewall`.
16. **probiotics — PMID 41274661** — Probiotics did not significantly improve anxiety overall; subgroup benefit appeared in neurodevelopmental disorders, especially autism, but was based on only a few trials. Boundary: Sparse subgroup evidence and strain/population heterogeneity preclude a general pediatric anxiolytic claim. State: `research_only_population_category_firewall`.
17. **protein-supplementation — PMID 40312971** — Protein supplementation modestly improved weight, muscle mass, fat-free mass, and fat mass, but did not significantly change BMI or lean body mass. Boundary: Certainty was moderate to low and findings are surgery-specific; the review itself judged evidence insufficient for firm recommendations. State: `research_only_population_firewall`.
18. **omega-3 — PMID 41515289** — Omega-3 interventions improved several nutritional, immune, inflammatory, and postoperative recovery measures and shortened hospitalization, but did not improve mortality. Boundary: Clinical nutrition regimens and oncology context limit translation to routine supplement use; post-hoc dose-response estimates are not consumer dosing guidance. State: `research_only_population_firewall`.
19. **probiotics — PMID 41630163** — Probiotics showed modest cognitive improvements and changes in several BDNF, inflammatory, and oxidative-stress markers. Boundary: Substantial heterogeneity and limited biomarker evidence reduce certainty; multi-strain apparent advantages are not proof of class-wide efficacy. State: `research_only_category_firewall`.
20. **creatine — PMID 42765482** — Creatine plus resistance training improved upper- and lower-body strength and lean mass versus resistance training alone, with a small rise in body mass and small reduction in body-fat percentage; fat mass itself was not significantly different. Boundary: Moderator findings, including larger strength effects in younger groups, were exploratory; diet and training backgrounds were heterogeneously reported. State: `reviewed_receipt_pending_final_admission`.
21. **probiotics-synbiotics — PMID 41961895** — Probiotic/synbiotic supplementation reduced total cholesterol, LDL-C, and ALT, but did not improve BMI-z, waist circumference, triglycerides, HDL-C, or fasting glucose; HOMA-IR increased. Boundary: Mixed metabolic effects, substantial heterogeneity, and low/very-low certainty for many outcomes require caution. State: `research_only_population_category_firewall`.
22. **probiotics — PMID 41328641** — Probiotics reduced ALT and produced a borderline AST reduction, but did not significantly improve bilirubin, albumin, or GGT. Boundary: Liver-disease stage, geography, strains, and treatment duration varied; enzyme changes do not establish improved clinical outcomes. State: `research_only_category_population_firewall`.
23. **probiotics — PMID 40859213** — Probiotics did not significantly improve adverse pregnancy outcomes overall; a subgroup using Bifidobacterium plus Lactobacillus showed reduced small-for-gestational-age risk. Boundary: The positive subgroup is hypothesis-generating, and computational mechanism work does not validate clinical efficacy. State: `research_only_population_strain_firewall`.
24. **probiotics — PMID 40669008** — Probiotics produced a modest pooled reduction in depressive symptoms, with substantial heterogeneity and stronger signals reported for certain strains or multi-strain products. Boundary: Strain-specific findings and heterogeneous scales/mechanisms prevent generalizing benefit to all probiotic products. State: `research_only_category_firewall`.
25. **curcumin — PMID 41115177** — Topical curcumin delivered as mouthwash, ointment, or photodynamic solution reduced Candida albicans colony counts and clinical signs across included trials. Boundary: Evidence quality was moderate with inconsistency and risk of bias; topical delivery findings do not establish oral curcumin supplement efficacy. State: `research_only_route_formulation_firewall`.
