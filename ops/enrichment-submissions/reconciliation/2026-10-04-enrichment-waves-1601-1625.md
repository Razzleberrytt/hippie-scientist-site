# Research enrichment Waves 1601–1625 — 2026-10-04

Fifth pass of the Waves 1501–2000 program, focused on cardiometabolic biomarkers, glucose, lipids, blood pressure and formulation-specific botanical evidence.

## Waves 1601–1625
1. **culinary-spices-metabolic-syndrome — PMID 38892617** — Twelve-week double-blind RCT in 120 people with or at risk for metabolic syndrome found ginger, cinnamon and black-seed interventions improved selected glucose and lipid markers versus placebo, but the parallel spice arms should not be collapsed into a single ingredient effect.
2. **garlic-glucose-lipids-meta — PMID 38892625** — Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized trials evaluated garlic across glycemic and lipid outcomes; formulation, duration and population heterogeneity mean pooled biomarker effects should remain separate from cardiovascular-event claims.
3. **purple-garlic-oil-metabolic-syndrome — PMID 38892688** — Randomized placebo-controlled ENDOTALLIUM study tested encapsulated purple-garlic oil on microvascular function and metabolic-syndrome components; product-specific vascular and metabolic findings should not be generalized to all garlic preparations.
4. **cinnamon-type2-diabetes-meta — PMID 38917435** — Systematic review/meta-analysis of randomized trials found cinnamon associated with changes in selected metabolic biomarkers in type 2 diabetes, but substantial variation in species, preparations, exposures and baseline treatment limits product-level certainty.
5. **freeze-dried-garlic-prehypertension — PMID 39001718** — Eight-week double-blind RCT in adults at risk for hypertension found freeze-dried garlic extract lowered systolic, diastolic and mean arterial pressure and improved several lipid markers versus placebo while increasing nitric-oxide levels.
6. **berberine-mafld-metabolic — PMID 39042390** — Twelve-week double-blind RCT in 70 people with MAFLD found berberine improved ALT, de Ritis ratio and total-cholesterol change, while most other glucose and lipid outcomes were not significantly different from placebo.
7. **gymnema-versus-berberine-obesity — PMID 39064727** — Randomized comparative study in 50 adults with obesity found berberine and Gymnema sylvestre produced different body-composition, glucose, blood-pressure and adipokine-expression changes; active-comparator design does not establish superiority against placebo.
8. **garlic-dyslipidemia-meta — PMID 39113105** — Meta-analysis of 21 RCTs in dyslipidemia reported reductions in total cholesterol, triglycerides and LDL with a small HDL increase from garlic, but product forms and study quality vary across the pooled evidence.
9. **onion-garlic-extract-ldl — PMID 39203947** — Eight-week double-blind RCT in mildly hypercholesterolemic adults found a standardized onion-plus-garlic extract lowered LDL and total cholesterol with selected blood-pressure and inflammatory changes; the combined formulation prevents garlic-only attribution.
10. **cinnamon-blood-pressure-inflammation-umbrella — PMID 39299867** — Umbrella review of cinnamon meta-analyses found small average reductions in systolic and diastolic pressure and selected antioxidant/IL-6 signals, while CRP, MDA and ICAM-1 were not consistently improved.
11. **ceylon-cinnamon-metabolic-safety — PMID 39854533** — Randomized double-blind controlled trial evaluated Ceylon-cinnamon extract on glucose, lipids and safety in adults; species-specific extract evidence should not be transferred to cassia cinnamon or culinary cinnamon generally.
12. **berberine-cinnamon-type2-diabetes — PMID 39998703** — Twelve-week randomized double-blind trial found combined berberine plus cinnamon lowered fasting glucose, HbA1c and selected lipid measures versus placebo in type 2 diabetes; the combination design cannot identify the contribution of either ingredient.
13. **berberine-ursodeoxycholate-type2-diabetes — PMID 40029660** — Phase-2 randomized trial of berberine ursodeoxycholate in inadequately controlled type 2 diabetes assessed glycemic, hepatic and cardiometabolic outcomes; this is a distinct pharmaceutical conjugate and must not be treated as ordinary berberine-supplement evidence.
14. **botanicals-type2-diabetes-network-meta — PMID 40439602** — Bayesian network meta-analysis compared curcumin, resveratrol, silymarin and berberine for cardiometabolic risk markers in type 2 diabetes; indirect rankings across heterogeneous products and trials are category evidence, not a universal botanical hierarchy.
15. **berberine-nafld-clinical-preclinical-meta — PMID 40949129** — Mixed clinical/preclinical meta-analysis found berberine lowered fasting glucose in the small clinical evidence base and showed broader biochemical effects in animal models; the 4-clinical-trial subset is too limited to inherit the stronger preclinical signals.
16. **cinnamon-metabolic-disease-umbrella — PMID 41256917** — Umbrella review of 21 meta-analyses reported cinnamon-associated improvements in fasting glucose and lipid profiles in metabolic disease, but methodological quality, overlapping trials and formulation heterogeneity temper certainty.
17. **berberine-mafld-vascular-function — PMID 41305635** — Double-blind RCT in 70 overweight or obese adults with MAFLD examined berberine effects on VEGF, arterial stiffness and vascular measures; vascular-surrogate findings should remain distinct from demonstrated clinical cardiovascular benefit.
18. **garlic-hypertension-long-term-meta — PMID 41393949** — Meta-analysis of 10 RCTs found adjunctive garlic lowered blood pressure in hypertension, while effect sizes varied by formulation, duration and population; findings support a biomarker-level signal rather than replacement of antihypertensive therapy.
19. **ceylon-cinnamon-diabetes-rct — PMID 41412108** — Randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial evaluated Ceylon cinnamon in diabetes; the receipt remains species- and preparation-specific and should not be merged with cassia-cinnamon evidence without formulation verification.
20. **berberine-obesity-masld-null-adiposity — PMID 41543854** — Large multicenter six-month RCT in 337 diabetes-free adults with obesity and MASLD found berberine did not significantly reduce visceral-adipose area or liver-fat content versus placebo, providing a high-value null against weight/fat-loss claims.
21. **green-tea-extract-gut-barrier-metabolic — PMID 38430822** — Four-week randomized crossover trial in healthy adults and adults with metabolic syndrome found decaffeinated green-tea extract reduced circulating endotoxin and selected gut-inflammatory/permeability markers and fasting glucose, without a systemic-inflammatory effect.
22. **green-tea-kombucha-overweight-inflammation — PMID 39339787** — Ten-week RCT found green-tea kombucha added to caloric restriction did not enhance weight or fat loss versus caloric restriction alone, although selected inflammation, lipid-accumulation and salivary-microbiome measures differed.
23. **polyphenols-cardiometabolic-meta — PMID 39683599** — Large systematic review/meta-analysis of 281 studies found different polyphenol classes produced modest outcome-specific changes in blood pressure, lipids and glycemic markers; pooling across catechins, anthocyanins, curcumin and others requires a category firewall.
24. **green-tea-type2-diabetes-meta — PMID 39809182** — Meta-analysis of 15 trials and 722 participants with type 2 diabetes reported improvements in fasting glucose, HbA1c and insulin-resistance indices with green tea, but product composition and trial heterogeneity remain important.
25. **flavan3ols-blood-pressure-endothelial-meta — PMID 40126033** — Meta-analysis of 145 RCTs and 5,205 participants found flavan-3-ol-rich foods/supplements modestly reduced office and ambulatory blood pressure, with larger effects at higher baseline pressure; interventions spanned cocoa, tea, grape, apple and isolated compounds.
