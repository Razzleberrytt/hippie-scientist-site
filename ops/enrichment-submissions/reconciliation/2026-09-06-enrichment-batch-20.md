# Enrichment Batch 20 — 2026-09-06

## Purpose

Research-only enrichment pass extending the full-corpus inventory in `2026-09-06-full-corpus-inventory.md`. This batch is **reconciliation/staging evidence, not canonical/public mutation**. Existing findings are deliberately carried forward with tighter claim boundaries rather than counted as new evidence merely because they are newly organized.

## Operating boundaries

- Human clinical evidence remains distinct from observational, mechanistic, animal, traditional, biomarker, and microbiome evidence.
- Reviews/meta-analyses remain review-level evidence; they do not become synthetic primary trials.
- Exact intervention identity, formulation, species, route, population, indication, and endpoint remain attached.
- Null, mixed, heterogeneous, low-certainty, and safety-counterweight findings remain first-class.
- No consumer dose or recommendation is inferred from a studied regimen.
- No source is promoted here; canonical source admission and governed implementation remain separate.

## Batch findings — 50 targets

1. **Taurine — PMID 40320621.** 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis of randomized trials assessing cognition; pooled overall cognitive benefit was not significant. Keep the prior taurine/citrulline-malate source-contamination defect separate and retain the null result as a calibration anchor.
2. **Acetyl-L-carnitine — PMID 42261369.** 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis of mood-disorder studies; heterogeneous populations and regimens. Preserve subgroup, adjunct/monotherapy, risk-of-bias, and indication boundaries rather than treating a pooled depressive-symptom signal as universal antidepressant efficacy.
3. **Gotu kola / Centella asiatica — PMID 28878245.** Systematic review/meta-analysis of cognition/mood trials; no significant overall cognitive improvement versus placebo. Separate Centella-alone from combination interventions and do not inherit unsupported dosing.
4. **Holy basil / Tulsi — PMID 36185698.** 2022 randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial in 100 stressed adults using standardized Holixer *Ocimum tenuiflorum* extract, 125 mg twice daily for 8 weeks. Preserve formulation, population, sponsor/product provenance, subjective stress/sleep signals, and objective-sleep limitations.
5. **Lion's Mane — PMID 38004235.** 2023 randomized placebo-controlled pilot in 41 healthy adults. Preserve small-sample/short-duration limits and fruiting-body preparation identity; do not turn this into generic *Hericium* efficacy.
6. **Lion's Mane — PMID 40276537.** 2025 randomized placebo-controlled double-blind crossover acute study in healthy younger adults using a specified 3 g 10:1 fruiting-body preparation. Preserve mostly-null/global-outcome findings and acute-exposure limits.
7. **American ginseng — PMID 41017752.** 2026 review of *Panax quinquefolius* and human neurocognitive function. Species identity is mandatory: do not merge with *P. ginseng* evidence.
8. **Valerian — PMID 38359657.** 2024 umbrella review of valerian for insomnia. Safety appears generally favorable, but efficacy is not established; subjective sleep-quality findings should remain distinct from quantitative/objective measures.
9. **Chamomile — PMID 39106912.** 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis of clinical sleep trials. Some subjective sleep domains favored chamomile, particularly awakenings/staying asleep, while not all domains improved; objective-data limitations remain.
10. **Chamomile — PMID 31006899.** Meta-analysis of chamomile for anxiety; no overall anxiety effect across all populations, with more favorable selected GAD/timepoint findings. Preserve indication and subgroup boundaries.
11. **Saffron — PMID 38424688.** 2024 systematic review of clinical evidence spanning neurological/psychiatric outcomes and 46 RCTs. Keep outcome-by-outcome certainty, formulation/active-ingredient differences, combination boundaries, and heterogeneity.
12. **Saffron safety — PMID 42057871.** 2026 systematic review of adverse events from clinical trials and case reports. Safety evidence should remain dose/formulation/population-specific and must not be collapsed into a generic "safe" statement.
13. **Creatine — PMID 41189312.** 11 trials / 1,093 participants in a depression systematic review/meta-analysis; SMD −0.34 with very-low GRADE certainty, substantial heterogeneity, and average effect below the cited minimal important difference. Treat primarily as an uncertainty/counterweight anchor.
14. **Lavender / Silexan — PMID 36717399.** Proprietary oral Silexan evidence is formulation-specific. Do not generalize oral Silexan anxiety evidence to inhaled, topical, tea, or unspecified lavender preparations.
15. **Psyllium / fiber in IBS — PMID 42600900.** 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis: 30 RCTs/1,904 people with IBS; clinical response RR 1.21 overall and RR 1.53 for psyllium, while overall GI symptom scores were not significantly improved. Psyllium-specific response should not become a universal fiber or weight-loss claim.
16. **Psyllium lipids — PMID 41366295.** RCT synthesis supporting lipid-related outcomes. Keep lipid endpoints separate from IBS response and from integrated weight-loss claims.
17. **Psyllium anthropometrics — PMID 41126340.** RCT synthesis of anthropometric outcomes. Preserve outcome-specificity; do not combine heterogeneous fiber products into a single weight-loss effect without intervention matching.
18. **Psyllium glycemia — PMID 38844885.** Glycemic review evidence. Keep glycemic biomarkers distinct from clinical diabetes outcomes and preserve preparation/dose differences.
19. **Zinc — PMID 41617950.** 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis of RCTs on anthropometrics/adipokines. Most body-composition/adipokine outcomes require cautious interpretation; small changes do not authorize generic weight-management claims.
20. **Zinc in CKD — PMID 41651464.** CKD-specific evidence must remain population-specific and cannot be inherited by healthy adults or generic zinc supplementation claims.
21. **Zinc glycemia — PMID 42594341.** Glycemic systematic-review evidence should remain endpoint-specific and separated from anthropometric findings.
22. **Selenium in Graves' orbitopathy — PMID 42355878.** 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis of RCTs with trial-sequential analysis. Earlier positive signals coexist with inconsistent findings across selenium status and disease severity; preserve the Graves'-orbitopathy indication and uncertainty.
23. **Citicoline/phosphatidylserine — PMID 42538853.** Review of 11 controlled studies/10 RCTs. Pediatric ADHD and adult populations must remain separate; combination/ingredient identity must not be flattened.
24. **CoQ10 depression/fatigue — PMID 41294251.** 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis of RCTs in diagnosed depression with or without medical illness. Depression outcomes and fatigue should be reported separately; do not turn mitochondrial mechanism into clinical efficacy.
25. **CoQ10 metabolic — PMID 42192187.** Large RCT synthesis of metabolic outcomes. Preserve endpoint and population boundaries and avoid converting biomarker changes into broad disease-treatment claims.
26. **CoQ10 blood pressure — PMID 40495903.** 45-RCT synthesis reported an SBP signal while DBP/HR were null. Preserve endpoint separation rather than calling CoQ10 a general cardiovascular treatment.
27. **Citrulline malate — PMID 42356270.** 30 RCTs/138 effect sizes/644 participants; small performance effect (g≈0.16) with exertion outcome nulls and low/very-low GRADE. Do not let athletic-context evidence contaminate taurine or other amino-acid entities.
28. **Vitamin D cardiometabolic — PMID 42521227.** 2026 meta-analysis of 45 RCTs found modest reductions in LDL-C, SBP, FBG and HbA1c. Clinical significance remains uncertain; preserve baseline-vitamin-D and age subgroup boundaries.
29. **Vitamin D MASLD — PMID 41478593.** MASLD-specific evidence remains disease/population-specific and should not be merged into generic cardiometabolic efficacy.
30. **Vitamin D cognition — PMID 42100929.** Cognitive evidence must remain distinct from metabolic outcomes and cannot be used to backfill a generic vitamin-D cognitive claim.
31. **Vitamin D PMS — PMID 42355996.** Premenstrual-syndrome evidence is indication-specific; do not generalize to mood disorders or dysmenorrhea without direct evidence.
32. **Inorganic nitrate/endothelial function — PMID 40679494.** 13 RCTs showed improved FMD (mean difference 1.48 percentage points) but with very high heterogeneity (I² 98.2%). This is an endothelial-function surrogate; do not convert it into cardiovascular-event prevention.
33. **Nitrate/beetroot blood pressure — PMID 41619653.** Preserve dietary beetroot/nitrate versus isolated inorganic nitrate distinctions and keep BP endpoints separate from endothelial-function surrogates.
34. **Astaxanthin — PMID 41710469.** 7 RCTs/188 participants; 4–28 mg/day for 4 days–12 weeks, with an AOPP signal while many oxidative/injury biomarkers were null. Preserve short-duration/formulation boundaries and avoid broad antioxidant-disease claims.
35. **Spirulina — PMID 41873104.** 8 RCTs using 1–8 g/day for 3–16 weeks; CRP showed a small signal while TNF/IL-6 were null. Biomarker-only evidence must not become clinical anti-inflammatory efficacy.
36. **Plant bioactive polysaccharides — PMID 41707853.** 2026 umbrella review of 33 systematic reviews/meta-analyses of RCTs covering 111 metabolic indicators. Category-level evidence cannot be assigned to an individual polysaccharide without direct identity support.
37. **Curcumin in critical illness — PMID 41652864.** 7 trials/571 critically ill participants with ALT/bilirubin/SOFA/ICU-stay signals. Preserve critical-illness population and do not transfer to healthy users or routine liver claims.
38. **Herbal supplements for erectile dysfunction — PMID 41696741.** 14 RCTs/1,227 men with mixed herbal products. Signals for some saffron/ginseng preparations coexist with insufficient evidence for Tribulus/Maca; product identity and combination status are mandatory.
39. **Herbal medicine safety in type 2 diabetes — PMID 41539457.** 22 cross-sectional studies across 19 countries covering 49 herbs. Use as a safety/interaction context map, not as proof that every listed herb has the same risk.
40. **Olive leaf/pomace — PMID 42303499.** 30 RCTs/1,726 participants; olive-leaf cardiometabolic/inflammatory signals with glycemic nulls, while olive pomace lacked consistent cardiometabolic benefit and had an IL-8 signal. Keep leaf and pomace separate.
41. **Herbal/natural products with PPI therapy — PMID 42280436.** 18 studies/1,984 participants; H. pylori eradication RR 1.20 with nonstandard effective-rate outcomes and exploratory microbiome findings. Preserve combination/PPI context and avoid universal eradication claims.
42. **MDD nutraceutical/phytoceutical NMA — PMID 42680855.** 163 studies/15,757 participants with reported EPA, vitamin D3 and St John's wort signals, but high heterogeneity and author-flagged implausible effects. Sensitivity/disagreement must remain visible.
43. **East Asian herbal medicine + antipsychotics in schizophrenia — PMID 42426034.** 270 studies/26,053 participants, but adjunct-only and low-certainty evidence. Do not represent these findings as monotherapy efficacy.
44. **Herbal exposure in pregnancy — PMID 41519415.** 111 studies; adjusted observational evidence did not show a clear major adverse outcome overall, but specific signals remain. Preserve confounding, evidence-quality, and herb-specific boundaries.
45. **Chinese herbal dosage forms — PMID 41618670.** 82 meta-analyses/1,263 RCTs. No universal decoction-versus-capsule/granule effect-size rule; dosage-form claims require intervention-specific evidence.
46. **Garlic — PMID 40580481.** 108 RCTs/7,137 participants with multiple lipid/glycemic/BP/inflammatory marker signals, while several BMI/weight/adiponectin endpoints were null. Preserve endpoint-specificity and preparation identity.
47. **Aged garlic — PMID 40628369.** 19-trial synthesis with SBP and LDL signals. The earlier PMID 39437887 meta-analysis is retracted via PMID 42527229 and must remain quarantined from active aggregation.
48. **Ashwagandha mental health — PMID 41644067.** 22-RCT synthesis; preserve product, route, dose and population complexity rather than treating all as interchangeable ashwagandha.
49. **Ashwagandha hormones — PMID 41740946.** Hormonal-endpoint evidence remains population- and regimen-specific and should not become generic endocrine benefit.
50. **Ashwagandha glucose — PMID 42641800.** 11 trials/667 participants; FBG signal was modest and HbA1c effect was small with CI crossing zero. Preserve null/uncertain glycemic evidence and do not overstate diabetes treatment.
