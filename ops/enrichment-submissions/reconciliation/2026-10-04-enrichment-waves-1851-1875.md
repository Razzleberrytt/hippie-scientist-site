# Research enrichment Waves 1851–1875 — 2026-10-04

Fifteenth pass of the Waves 1501–2000 program, emphasizing amino-acid and performance evidence while preserving co-training, multi-ingredient, mechanistic, population-specific and direct-null boundaries.

## Waves 1851–1875
1. **beta-alanine-plyometric-swimmers — PMID 42218755** — Randomized 8-week study in 30 trained male swimmers found upper-body plyometric training improved performance in both training groups, while beta-alanine produced greater gains in selected power/endurance measures and altered testosterone, cortisol and IgA; co-training design prevents pure supplement attribution.
2. **carnosine-endurance-rct — PMID 42308284** — Placebo-controlled trial in 299 adults found 12 weeks of carnosine produced only selective age/sex-specific endurance signals and no consistent grip-strength benefit; overall effects were limited rather than broadly ergogenic.
3. **beta-alanine-glutamine-muscle-brain-review — PMID 42322395** — Mechanistic review proposes beta-alanine/carnosine buffering and glutamine metabolism as interacting components of muscle-brain crosstalk, but the neuroprotection framework is largely mechanistic and requires direct human validation.
4. **ergogenic-aids-perceived-effort-review — PMID 42338317** — Narrative review identifies beta-alanine, caffeine and carbohydrate mouth rinse as better-supported ergogenic strategies while characterizing BCAA, citrulline, taurine and probiotics as less directly established for perceived-effort-mediated endurance effects.
5. **masters-athletes-supplements-review — PMID 42385164** — Narrative review maps protein, caffeine, creatine, beta-alanine, nitrates and peptide-related supplements in older/Masters athletes; useful evidence mapping but not a substitute for intervention-specific systematic review.
6. **citrulline-hypertension-exercise-crossover — PMID 42313111** — Crossover trial in 12 untreated hypertensive men found six days of L-citrulline improved autonomic recovery and reduced resting/post-exercise blood pressure responses after high-intensity exercise; very small male-only study needs replication.
7. **citrulline-exercise-muscle-review — PMID 42315852** — Review describes possible L-citrulline effects on exercise performance, protein synthesis and muscle metabolism but identifies major gaps around strategy, transport, mitochondria and gut-muscle mechanisms.
8. **citrulline-maternal-neonatal-review — PMID 42356310** — Perinatal review describes strong metabolic/NO rationale for L-citrulline but emphasizes that adequately powered clinical trials are still needed before routine maternal or neonatal use can be recommended.
9. **citrulline-pediatric-vascular-review — PMID 42356513** — Pediatric vascular review summarizes L-citrulline as an investigational nitric-oxide-supporting therapy in systemic/pulmonary vascular disease; disease-specific pharmacotherapy evidence should not be generalized to wellness supplementation.
10. **citrulline-older-women-exercise-review — PMID 42423997** — Review suggests exercise plus L-citrulline may support vascular and muscle outcomes in postmenopausal/older women, but explicitly calls for larger high-quality RCTs and long-term clinical confirmation.
11. **citrulline-melas-phase1 — PMID 42520344** — Phase-I study in 10 adults with MELAS found no dose-limiting toxicity across tested L-citrulline doses and exploratory cerebral-blood-flow increases, while high doses raised guanidino compounds; efficacy requires a placebo-controlled trial.
12. **hmb-critical-illness-factorial-rct — PMID 41102810** — Multicenter 2x2 factorial RCT in 266 ICU adults found resistance training improved function and longer-term outcomes, whereas HMB only modestly improved phase angle and fatigue with no benefit on most outcomes and no synergy with training.
13. **hmb-older-adults-meta — PMID 41305674** — Systematic review/meta-analysis evaluates HMB added to resistance training in older adults across muscle quality, body composition and physical function; retain as category-level synthesis pending source-specific effect extraction.
14. **creatine-hmb-older-adults-crossover — PMID 41511610** — Randomized crossover trial in 30 active older adults found creatine plus HMB during supervised multicomponent training improved mobility and selected metabolic outcomes versus placebo; combined supplements and exercise prevent component-specific attribution.
15. **hmb-cirrhosis-pilot-rct — PMID 41544700** — Pilot RCT in 21 adults with decompensated cirrhosis found HMB-containing oral nutrition did not significantly improve muscle mass or grip strength versus control, despite within-group improvements in phase angle, arm circumference and chair-stand time.
16. **prebiotic-hmb-sarcopenia-rct — PMID 41627702** — Thirty-day RCT in older adults with sarcopenia found HMB improved selected muscle measures and adding fructooligosaccharides produced larger intestinal-barrier/inflammatory changes; combination design and short duration limit inference.
17. **hmb-muscle-transcriptome — PMID 41683256** — Acute human muscle-biopsy study found HMB altered hundreds of transcripts related to protein turnover, amino-acid transport and signaling; transcriptomic response is mechanistic evidence, not proof of long-term functional benefit.
18. **hmb-bcaa-hemodialysis-crossover — PMID 41683304** — Double-blind crossover study in 24 hemodialysis patients found an HMB+BCAA+zinc medical food improved muscle-thickness/body-composition and selected inflammatory markers but not short-term physical performance; multi-ingredient evidence.
19. **creatine-hmb-redox-secondary — PMID 41712056** — Secondary analysis of a 30-person crossover trial found nominal creatine+HMB effects on glutathione-centered redox measures that did not survive false-discovery correction; exploratory correlations should not be presented as established antioxidant benefit.
20. **hmb-protein-weightloss-rct — PMID 42444106** — Double-blind RCT in 102 adults with obesity found a protein/CaHMB supplement preserved skeletal muscle and improved fat-loss measures during energy restriction versus maltodextrin, but protein co-formulation prevents isolation of HMB-specific effects.
21. **hmb-t2d-masld-observational — PMID 42763880** — Small nonrandomized study in older adults with obesity, T2D and MASLD found heterogeneous muscle-mass changes during HMB-containing nutrition and no statistically significant overall gain; authors characterize the result as negative and exploratory.
22. **caffeine-morning-sprint-null — PMID 42588165** — Double-blind crossover study in 17 low-caffeine-consuming men found 300 mg morning caffeine lowered perceived exertion but did not clearly improve repeated sprint, strength, jump, mood or physiologic outcomes under standardized chronobiological conditions.
23. **caffeine-adolescent-risk-null — PMID 42656491** — Sleep-manipulation plus double-blind caffeine study in 41 adolescents found no clear practically meaningful average change in value-based risk decisions from caffeine, sleep restriction or their interaction.
24. **caffeine-migraine-triggers-meta — PMID 42662243** — Meta-analysis found caffeine was self-reported as a migraine trigger by about 12% of participants, but heterogeneity exceeded 95% and observational self-report cannot justify universal caffeine avoidance.
25. **caffeine-intraocular-pressure-meta — PMID 42666870** — Meta-analysis of randomized studies found acute caffeine, especially above 100 mg, transiently increased intraocular pressure in healthy and glaucomatous populations; clinically relevant safety context for susceptible users.
